📘 CHAPTER 3

YOUR FIRST OPINION SENTENCE: TURNING A THOUGHT INTO A FRENCH ARGUMENT SEED

🔴 HYPOTHETICAL PROBLEM

You are a TEF candidate named Aisha
She studied Chapter 1 → She understands the 4 scoring dimensions
She studied Chapter 2 → She can decode any prompt using the 3-layer extraction protocol
She sits down with this practice prompt:

"De nos jours, de nombreuses personnes préfèrent faire leurs achats en ligne plutôt qu'en magasin. Pensez-vous que cette tendance soit positive? Justifiez votre réponse."

Aisha successfully applies her Chapter 2 skills:

  • CONTEXT → People increasingly prefer online shopping over in-store shopping
  • QUESTION → Do you think this trend is positive? (Question Type 1: Direct Opinion + Type 5: Trend Evaluation)
  • FORMAT → Justify your response (arguments needed)

She knows EXACTLY what is being asked
She has a clear opinion in her head: "Yes, I think online shopping is mostly positive"
But when she picks up her pen, she freezes
She writes: "Je pense que oui."
Three words. Then nothing.
She does not know HOW to express her opinion as a proper French sentence
She does not know what structures exist beyond "Je pense que..."
She does not know how to make her opinion sound like an ARGUMENT rather than a casual chat response

❓ THE CORE QUESTION

  • How do you convert a mental opinion into a properly structured French opinion sentence?
  • What are the French expressions that signal "this is my position"?
  • How do you make your first sentence sound like B2-level argumentation, not A1-level conversation?
  • What is the difference between STATING an opinion and PLANTING an argument seed?

🟡 HOW TO APPROACH IT

Step 1: Understand What an "Opinion Sentence" Actually Does in Section B

  • An opinion sentence is NOT just telling someone what you think
  • In TEF Section B, your opinion sentence serves 3 simultaneous functions:
FUNCTION 1: DECLARATION
├── It tells the evaluator your POSITION on the question
└── Without this, the evaluator does not know where you stand

FUNCTION 2: DIRECTION
├── It sets the DIRECTION for everything that follows
├── Every argument, every example must connect back to this sentence
└── It is the compass for your entire response

FUNCTION 3: DEMONSTRATION
├── It demonstrates your ability to use opinion-expressing structures in French
├── The QUALITY of this sentence signals your level to the evaluator
└── "Je pense que oui" = A1 signal
    "Il me semble que cette tendance présente davantage d'avantages" = B2 signal

Step 2: Understand the Anatomy of a French Opinion Sentence

  • Every French opinion sentence has 3 components:
COMPONENT 1: OPINION MARKER
├── The expression that signals "this is what I believe"
├── Examples: Je pense que / Il me semble que / À mon avis
└── This is the VEHICLE that carries your opinion

COMPONENT 2: POSITION
├── The actual stance you are taking
├── Examples: positive / négative / bénéfique / nuisible / nécessaire
└── This is the CONTENT of your opinion

COMPONENT 3: SCOPE QUALIFIER (optional but powerful)
├── A word or phrase that shows nuance
├── Examples: dans une certaine mesure / globalement / principalement
└── This shows the evaluator you can think with NUANCE, not just black/white
  • Formula:
[OPINION MARKER] + [POSITION] + [SCOPE QUALIFIER if applicable]

Example:
"Je suis convaincu que" + "cette tendance est globalement positive" + "même si elle comporte certains risques"

🟢 HOW CAN WE SOLVE IT

The Solution = Mastering 4 Tiers of French Opinion Expressions

  • Not all opinion expressions are equal
  • They range from basic (A1–A2) to advanced (B2–C1)
  • Using higher-tier expressions directly impacts your Dimension 3 (Vocabulary) and Dimension 4 (Grammar) scores from Chapter 1
  • You must know ALL tiers so you can choose strategically

🔹 TIER 1: BASIC OPINION MARKERS (A1–A2 Level)

These are correct but signal low proficiency when used alone

French ExpressionEnglish EquivalentGrammar Note
Je pense que...I think that...Followed by indicatif
Je crois que...I believe that...Followed by indicatif
Je trouve que...I find that...Followed by indicatif
Pour moi...For me...No verb conjugation needed after this
À mon avis...In my opinion...No verb conjugation needed after this

Example sentences using Tier 1:

  • Je pense que le commerce en ligne est une bonne chose.
  • À mon avis, cette tendance est positive.
  • Je trouve que c'est bien.

Problem with using ONLY Tier 1:

  • → Every beginner uses these same 5 expressions
  • → They do not differentiate you from A2-level candidates
  • → They are grammatically simple (no complex structures triggered)
  • → Using them is NOT wrong — but using ONLY them limits your score ceiling

🔹 TIER 2: INTERMEDIATE OPINION MARKERS (B1 Level)

These show more range and slightly higher grammatical control

French ExpressionEnglish EquivalentGrammar Note
Il me semble que...It seems to me that...Impersonal construction → signals awareness of formal French
Je suis persuadé(e) que...I am persuaded that...Past participle agreement required (gender marker)
Je suis convaincu(e) que...I am convinced that...Past participle agreement required (gender marker)
J'estime que...I consider/estimate that...More formal synonym of "je pense que"
Je considère que...I consider that...Slightly more analytical tone
Selon moi...According to me...Formal alternative to "pour moi"

Example sentences using Tier 2:

  • Il me semble que cette évolution comporte à la fois des avantages et des inconvénients.
  • Je suis convaincue que le commerce en ligne facilite la vie quotidienne.
  • J'estime que cette tendance mérite une analyse nuancée.

Advantage over Tier 1:

  • → Shows vocabulary range (Dimension 3 improvement)
  • → Triggers grammatical features like agreement (Dimension 4 improvement)
  • → Sounds more analytical and less conversational

🔹 TIER 3: UPPER-INTERMEDIATE OPINION MARKERS (B2 Level)

These are the TARGET level for most TEF candidates — they signal B2 competence clearly

French ExpressionEnglish EquivalentGrammar Note
Je suis d'avis que...I am of the opinion that...Formal, analytical
Il est indéniable que...It is undeniable that...Impersonal + strong assertion
Force est de constater que...One must observe that...Very formal, essay-level expression
Il va sans dire que...It goes without saying that...Strong conviction marker
Il convient de souligner que...It is appropriate to emphasize that...Academic register
Je suis fermement convaincu(e) que...I am firmly convinced that...Tier 2 + adverb intensifier
Tout porte à croire que...Everything suggests that...Evidence-based opinion framing

Example sentences using Tier 3:

  • Force est de constater que le commerce en ligne a profondément transformé nos habitudes de consommation.
  • Il est indéniable que cette tendance présente des avantages considérables pour les consommateurs.
  • Je suis fermement convaincue que les bénéfices de cette évolution l'emportent sur ses inconvénients.

Why Tier 3 is the sweet spot for TEF:

  • → Immediately signals B2-level competence to the evaluator
  • → Triggers complex grammatical structures naturally
  • → Shows mastery of formal written register (critical for Dimension 3)
  • → Differentiates you from the majority of candidates who stay at Tier 1–2

🔹 TIER 4: ADVANCED OPINION MARKERS (C1 Level)

These demonstrate sophisticated argumentation — use sparingly and accurately

French ExpressionEnglish EquivalentGrammar Note
Bien que certains puissent arguer que... je maintiens que...Although some may argue that... I maintain that...Triggers SUBJUNCTIVE (puissent) → high-level grammar
Loin de moi l'idée de nier que... toutefois...Far from me the idea of denying that... however...Concessive structure → intellectual sophistication
Sans prétendre détenir la vérité absolue, il m'apparaît que...Without claiming to hold absolute truth, it appears to me that...Modesty + confidence balance → very C1
Au risque de paraître catégorique, j'affirme que...At the risk of seeming categorical, I affirm that...Self-awareness + strong assertion
S'il est vrai que [counter-argument], il n'en demeure pas moins que...While it is true that... it nonetheless remains that...Concession + reaffirmation → dialectical mastery

Example sentences using Tier 4:

  • Bien que certains puissent arguer que le commerce en ligne détruit les emplois locaux, je maintiens que ses bénéfices économiques globaux sont considérables.
  • S'il est vrai que les achats en ligne réduisent le contact humain, il n'en demeure pas moins que cette évolution répond à un besoin réel de praticité.

Warning about Tier 4:

  • → Only use if you can execute the grammar PERFECTLY
  • → A Tier 4 expression with a grammar error is WORSE than a Tier 2 expression with no errors
  • → Evaluators notice failed attempts at sophistication and it hurts your score
  • → Master Tiers 2–3 first → Add Tier 4 only when fully confident

🔵 WHAT COULD BE DONE

Option A: Memorize one Tier 3 expression and use it for every response

  • → Better than using only Tier 1
  • → But repetition across practice responses builds a rigid habit
  • → If the memorized expression does not fit a specific prompt, you are stuck
  • → Recommendation: Learn 3–4 Tier 3 expressions and rotate them

Option B: Always start with a Tier 4 expression to impress the evaluator

  • → High risk strategy
  • → If the grammar is wrong, the evaluator sees overreach, not competence
  • → Tier 4 with errors < Tier 2 without errors
  • → Use only if grammar execution is flawless

Option C: Use one expression from each tier in the same response

  • → This is actually an excellent strategy for showing RANGE
  • → Introduction → Tier 3 expression (strong opening)
  • → Body paragraph → Tier 2 expression (supporting argument)
  • → Conclusion → Tier 3 or Tier 1 expression (natural closure)
  • → Variety signals Dimension 3 (vocabulary range)

Option D: Avoid opinion markers entirely and just state facts

  • → Fatal error for Section B
  • → The prompt asks for YOUR opinion
  • → Without opinion markers, the evaluator sees a report, not an argument
  • → You fail Dimension 1 (not answering the question as asked)

✅ WHAT SHOULD DO

1. Build a Personal Arsenal of 8–10 Opinion Expressions Across Tiers

YOUR RECOMMENDED ARSENAL:
├── FROM TIER 1 (keep 2 for informal moments):
│   ├── Je pense que...
│   └── À mon avis...
│
├── FROM TIER 2 (keep 3 for body paragraphs):
│   ├── Il me semble que...
│   ├── Je suis convaincu(e) que...
│   └── J'estime que...
│
├── FROM TIER 3 (keep 3 for introductions and strong statements):
│   ├── Force est de constater que...
│   ├── Il est indéniable que...
│   └── Je suis fermement convaincu(e) que...
│
└── FROM TIER 4 (keep 1–2 for special impact, only if grammar is secure):
    ├── S'il est vrai que..., il n'en demeure pas moins que...
    └── Bien que certains puissent arguer que..., je maintiens que...

2. Practice Forming Complete Opinion Sentences, Not Just Markers

  • Knowing "Je suis convaincu que" is useless without the ability to complete the sentence
  • Practice drill:
    • → Take any topic (education, technology, health)
    • → Write 5 complete opinion sentences using 5 different markers
    • → Check grammar in each sentence
    • → This builds FLUENCY with opinion structures

3. Match Your Opinion Marker to Your ACTUAL Position Strength

How Strongly You FeelRecommended Tier
"I somewhat agree" → Nuanced position→ Tier 2: Il me semble que... / Tier 3: Tout porte à croire que...
"I clearly agree/disagree" → Strong position→ Tier 3: Je suis fermement convaincu(e) que... / Il est indéniable que...
"I see both sides but lean one way" → Balanced position→ Tier 4: S'il est vrai que..., il n'en demeure pas moins que...
"I have no strong opinion" → Neutral position→ Tier 2: Il me semble que... / J'estime que... (these allow soft positioning)

4. Always Include Your Position Within the First 2–3 Sentences of Your Response

  • The evaluator should know WHERE YOU STAND within the first few lines
  • Do NOT build suspense → This is not a novel
  • Delayed position = evaluator uncertainty = lower Dimension 2 score (coherence)
  • State your position early → Then spend the rest of the response DEFENDING it

5. Gender Agreement Practice for Tier 2+ Expressions

  • "Je suis convaincu que..." → If you are male or using default
  • "Je suis convaincue que..." → If you are female
  • "Je suis persuadé que..." → Male
  • "Je suis persuadée que..." → Female
  • This agreement is MANDATORY in formal written French
  • Forgetting it → Grammar error → Dimension 4 penalty
  • In TEF, use the gender that applies to you → Be consistent throughout

❌ WHAT SHOULD NOT DO

1. Do NOT Use the Same Opinion Marker More Than Once in a Response

  • If you write "Je pense que..." three times → Evaluator sees limited vocabulary range
  • Dimension 3 (lexical competence) requires VARIETY
  • Use a DIFFERENT marker each time you express or reinforce your opinion

2. Do NOT Use Tier 1 Markers as Your OPENING Sentence

  • Your first sentence creates the evaluator's first impression
  • "Je pense que le shopping en ligne est bien." → A1 impression = hard to recover from
  • "Force est de constater que le commerce en ligne a transformé nos habitudes." → B2 impression = strong start
  • First impressions matter — evaluators are human

3. Do NOT Combine Two Opinion Markers in the Same Sentence

  • ❌ "À mon avis, je pense que cette tendance est positive."
  • → "À mon avis" and "je pense que" both mean the same thing
  • → This is REDUNDANCY, not sophistication
  • → Choose ONE marker per sentence

4. Do NOT Use Opinion Markers Without Completing the Thought

  • ❌ "Je suis convaincu que le commerce en ligne." (Incomplete)
  • ✅ "Je suis convaincu que le commerce en ligne facilite considérablement la vie quotidienne." (Complete)
  • An incomplete opinion sentence is worse than a simple complete one
  • Every opinion sentence needs: MARKER + SUBJECT + VERB + COMPLEMENT (minimum)

5. Do NOT Express Your Opinion as an Absolute Universal Truth Without a Marker

  • ❌ "Le commerce en ligne est la meilleure chose au monde." (Presented as fact, no opinion marker)
  • ✅ "J'estime que le commerce en ligne représente une avancée majeure." (Presented as your reasoned opinion)
  • Section B asks for YOUR opinion → You must signal it IS an opinion
  • Stating something as absolute fact without qualification sounds arrogant and unreasoned
  • Exception: You CAN state widely accepted facts without markers → "Le commerce en ligne s'est développé rapidement ces dernières années." (This is a fact, not an opinion)

6. Do NOT Use "Je" in Every Single Sentence

  • Overusing "Je" makes the text sound self-centered and repetitive
  • Alternate between:
    • → "Je" sentences: "Je suis convaincu que..."
    • → Impersonal sentences: "Il est indéniable que..." / "Force est de constater que..."
    • → General statements: "Cette tendance présente des avantages significatifs."
  • This alternation creates rhythm and variety → Dimensions 3 and 4 improvement

⏱️ WHEN TO USE WHAT

SituationWhich Tier to Use
Opening your introduction→ Tier 3 (strong, formal, impressive first impression)
Restating your position in the body→ Tier 2 (solid, clear, slightly different from opening)
Introducing a concession before your counter-argument→ Tier 4 if confident: "S'il est vrai que..., il n'en demeure pas moins que..."
Closing your conclusion→ Tier 2 or Tier 3 (reinforcing your position without exact repetition)
You are running low on time→ Tier 1 (quick, safe, grammatically easy — better than no opinion marker at all)
The prompt asks a very simple, direct question→ Tier 2 is sufficient — do not over-sophisticate a simple question
The prompt is abstract or philosophical→ Tier 3 or Tier 4 (the complexity of the prompt warrants complex expression)

🚫 WHEN NOT TO USE

When NOT to Use Opinion Markers

  • When stating FACTS in your body paragraphs
    • → "Selon une étude récente, 70% des consommateurs achètent en ligne." → This is evidence, not opinion → No opinion marker needed
  • When describing the CONTEXT in your introduction
    • → "De nos jours, le commerce en ligne connaît une croissance considérable." → This is scene-setting → No opinion marker needed
  • When presenting the OPPOSING viewpoint
    • → "Certains estiment que le commerce en ligne nuit aux commerces locaux." → This is reporting others' views → Use "certains" not "je"

When NOT to Use Tier 4 Expressions

  • When you are not 100% confident in subjunctive conjugation
  • When the prompt is simple and Tier 4 would sound disproportionately heavy
  • When you have already used a Tier 4 expression once → Using it twice looks forced

⚙️ HOW IT ONLY WORKS (INTERNAL MECHANICS)

Why Opinion Markers Exist in French Argumentative Writing

LINGUISTIC FUNCTION:
├── French argumentative tradition distinguishes between:
│   ├── FAIT (Fact) → Presented without personal attribution
│   ├── OPINION (Opinion) → Presented with explicit personal attribution
│   └── ARGUMENT (Argument) → Opinion + logical support
│
├── Opinion markers serve as EPISTEMIC MARKERS
│   → They signal the DEGREE OF CERTAINTY the writer has
│   → "Il me semble que" = moderate certainty
│   → "Il est indéniable que" = high certainty
│   → "Je suis fermement convaincu que" = very high certainty
│
└── The evaluator reads these markers to assess:
    → Can the candidate distinguish fact from opinion?
    → Can the candidate modulate certainty levels?
    → Can the candidate use varied linguistic tools for the same function?

How the Evaluator Processes Your Opinion Sentence

EVALUATOR'S MENTAL PROCESS:
│
├── STEP 1: Does the candidate state a clear position?
│   ├── YES → Dimension 1 partially satisfied (consigne followed)
│   └── NO → Immediate concern about Dimension 1
│
├── STEP 2: What level of opinion marker is used?
│   ├── Tier 1 only → Dimension 3 assessment: basic vocabulary
│   ├── Tier 2–3 → Dimension 3 assessment: appropriate vocabulary
│   └── Tier 4 → Dimension 3 assessment: rich vocabulary (IF correct)
│
├── STEP 3: Is the grammar of the opinion sentence correct?
│   ├── "Je suis convaincue que" (correct agreement) → Dimension 4 positive signal
│   └── "Je suis convaincu que" (when writer is female and should agree) → Dimension 4 negative signal
│
└── STEP 4: Does the opinion sentence set up the rest of the response?
    ├── YES → Dimension 2 positive signal (coherence)
    └── NO → Dimension 2 concern (the opinion goes nowhere)

The "Seed" Metaphor — Why This Chapter Calls It an "Argument Seed"

  • Your opinion sentence is a SEED, not a flower
  • It contains the POTENTIAL for a full argument but is not the full argument itself
  • The seed must be PLANTED correctly (clear, grammatical, well-chosen marker)
  • Then it must be WATERED (with reasons — coming in Chapter 4)
  • Then it must GROW (with structure — coming in Chapter 5)
  • A seed planted in the wrong soil (off-topic) dies → Chapter 2's prompt decoding prevents this
  • A seed that is never watered (no reasons given) stays a seed → Chapter 4 prevents this

🕳️ COMMON PITFALLS AND FAILURE MODES

Pitfall 1: The Parrot Trap

  • What happens: Candidate learns one expression ("Je pense que") and uses it 5 times in the response
  • Why it happens: Comfort zone — the candidate trusts this one expression and fears making errors with others
  • How to prevent: Practice at least 3 different markers until they feel natural → Then rotate them in every response

Pitfall 2: The Sophistication Overreach

  • What happens: Candidate uses a Tier 4 expression but makes a grammar error within it
  • Example: "Bien que certains peuvent arguer que..." → ERROR: "peuvent" should be "puissent" (subjunctive required after "bien que")
  • Why it happens: Candidate memorized the expression without understanding its grammatical requirements
  • How to prevent: Never use an expression unless you understand AND can execute its grammar requirements
  • The rule: If you cannot explain WHY a verb form changes in the expression, do NOT use that expression

Pitfall 3: The Missing Position

  • What happens: Candidate uses an opinion marker but never actually states a clear position
  • Example: "Il me semble que cette question est très intéressante et qu'il y a beaucoup de choses à dire."
  • Why it fails: This says NOTHING about the candidate's actual position — it is filler
  • How to prevent: After writing your opinion sentence, ask: "Does this sentence tell the reader whether I am FOR or AGAINST (or nuanced)?" If not → Rewrite

Pitfall 4: The Opinion-Fact Confusion

  • What happens: Candidate presents opinions as facts and facts as opinions
  • Example of opinion presented as fact: "Le commerce en ligne est mauvais pour la société." (No marker — sounds like universal truth)
  • Example of fact presented as opinion: "Je pense que le commerce en ligne existe depuis les années 1990." (This is a verifiable fact, not an opinion)
  • How to prevent:
    • → Verifiable information = present as FACT (no opinion marker)
    • → Personal judgment = present as OPINION (with opinion marker)

Pitfall 5: The Hedge-Everything Trap

  • What happens: Candidate uses so many softening expressions that the position becomes invisible
  • Example: "Il me semble que peut-être, dans une certaine mesure, on pourrait éventuellement penser que cette tendance n'est pas entièrement négative."
  • Why it fails: The evaluator cannot identify a clear position → Dimension 1 (consigne) weakened
  • How to prevent: Nuance is good, but your CORE position must be identifiable
  • The balance: One qualifier is nuanced → Three qualifiers in the same sentence is evasion

🔑 KEY TAKEAWAY

What Chapter 3 Established

An opinion sentence has 3 components:
  1. OPINION MARKER (the expression signaling personal position)
  2. POSITION (the actual stance — for/against/nuanced)
  3. SCOPE QUALIFIER (optional nuance — shows intellectual sophistication)

4 Tiers of opinion markers exist:
  Tier 1 (A1–A2): Je pense que / À mon avis → Safe but limiting
  Tier 2 (B1): Il me semble que / Je suis convaincu(e) que → Solid
  Tier 3 (B2): Force est de constater / Il est indéniable → Target level
  Tier 4 (C1): S'il est vrai que... il n'en demeure pas moins que → Use with caution

Strategic rules:
  → Never use the same marker twice in one response
  → Open with Tier 3 for strong first impression
  → Never use Tier 4 if grammar execution is uncertain
  → Always ensure your position is IDENTIFIABLE within the opinion sentence

  • Chapter 2 → Taught you to decode the prompt (you must know the question BEFORE forming an opinion)
  • Chapter 3 → Taught you to EXPRESS that opinion in French at the appropriate level

What Is Still Missing

  • You can now decode the question AND state your opinion
  • But stating "I believe online shopping is positive" is a CLAIM without PROOF
  • An unsupported opinion is not an argument — it is just a statement
  • The evaluator needs to see WHY you hold this position
  • What comes after "Je suis convaincu que cette tendance est positive" → WHY?
