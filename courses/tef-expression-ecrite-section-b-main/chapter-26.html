🟡 HOW TO APPROACH IT

Step 1: Understand the Time Reality of TEF Expression Écrite

TOTAL TIME FOR EXPRESSION ÉCRITE: 60 minutes
  → Section A (letter/message): ~25 minutes
  → Section B (argumentative text): ~35 minutes

THIS IS NOT FLEXIBLE:
  → If you spend 30 minutes on Section A → You have only 30 minutes for Section B
  → If you spend 20 minutes on Section A → You have 40 minutes for Section B
  → The COMBINED time is fixed → Every minute given to one section is TAKEN from the other

RECOMMENDED SPLIT:
  Section A: 22–25 minutes
  Section B: 35–38 minutes
  → Section B is MORE complex and MORE heavily weighted in scoring
  → Give it the LARGER share of time

MARCUS'S MISTAKE:
  → He spent 28 minutes on Section A → Only 32 minutes for Section B
  → He was already 3–6 minutes behind before Section B even started

Step 2: Understand WHY Uneven Time Distribution Kills Scores

THE SCORING REALITY:

The evaluator does NOT score each section separately and average them
The evaluator scores the COMPLETE response as a UNIFIED performance

A response with:
  → Excellent introduction (A+)
  → Excellent Body 1 (A+)
  → Weak Body 2 (C)
  → No synthèse (F)
  → Weak conclusion (D)
  
  → Does NOT score as: (A+ + A+ + C + F + D) / 5 = C+
  → Instead scores as: "Uneven, incomplete, structurally flawed" = C or C-

VERSUS a response with:
  → Good introduction (B+)
  → Good Body 1 (B+)
  → Good Body 2 (B+)
  → Decent synthèse (B)
  → Good conclusion (B+)
  
  → Scores as: "Consistent, complete, well-structured" = B+ to A-

THE PRINCIPLE:
  CONSISTENT GOOD > INCONSISTENT EXCELLENT
  
  → 5 sections at 70% quality = 70% overall (strong B2)
  → 2 sections at 95% + 3 sections at 40% = 63% overall (weak B2)
  → The WEAKEST section DRAGS DOWN the entire score disproportionately
  → This is why time must be distributed EVENLY, not concentrated

Step 3: Understand the 3 Time Enemies

ENEMY 1: OVER-PLANNING
  → Spending 7+ minutes planning before writing anything
  → The plan is perfect but there is no time to EXECUTE it
  → Marcus spent 9 minutes on Steps 1–4 (should have been 6–7 minutes max)

ENEMY 2: OVER-POLISHING
  → Rewriting sentences during the writing phase
  → Marcus rewrote his introduction twice → 5 minutes on 40 words
  → That is 1 minute per 8 words → Unsustainable pace
  → First draft quality with the protocol (Chapter 25) is BETTER than
     polished writing with no review time

ENEMY 3: UNEVEN INVESTMENT
  → Spending 10 minutes on Body 1 but only 5 minutes on Body 2
  → Body 1 is beautiful → Body 2 is rushed → The contrast is VISIBLE to the evaluator
  → The evaluator penalizes INCONSISTENCY more than consistent moderate quality

TOTAL SECTION B TIME: 35 minutes (assuming 25 minutes on Section A)

THE MASTER TIME BUDGET:

🔶 COMPONENT 2: THE SPEED-QUALITY CALIBRATION

How to write at the RIGHT speed — not too slow (runs out of time) and not too fast (quality drops)

The Target Writing Speed:

TOTAL WORDS TO WRITE: ~220 words
TOTAL WRITING TIME: ~21 minutes (Steps 5–7)
TARGET SPEED: ~10–11 words per minute

THIS MEANS:
  → Approximately 1 sentence every 1.5–2 minutes
  → Approximately 1 paragraph every 4–7 minutes
  → This pace feels MODERATE — not rushed, not leisurely

MARCUS'S SPEED PROBLEM:
  Introduction: 40 words in 5 minutes = 8 words/minute → TOO SLOW
  Body 1: 80 words in 10 minutes = 8 words/minute → TOO SLOW
  Body 2: 60 words in 5 minutes = 12 words/minute → Correct speed but FORCED by panic
  Conclusion: 15 words in 2 minutes = 7.5 words/minute → Incomplete due to panic

THE IDEAL:
  Every section written at approximately the SAME speed (10–11 words/minute)
  No section receives disproportionate attention
  Consistency of speed = Consistency of quality

The "Good Enough First Draft" Principle:

THE PRINCIPLE:
  Your FIRST DRAFT of each sentence should be "GOOD ENOUGH" — not perfect
  "Good enough" = grammatically correct + logically sound + B2 vocabulary
  "Perfect" = rhetorically polished + maximally varied + C1 vocabulary

WHY "GOOD ENOUGH" WINS:
  → "Good enough" takes 1.5 minutes per sentence
  → "Perfect" takes 3–4 minutes per sentence
  → In 21 minutes: "Good enough" produces 14 sentences (complete response)
  → In 21 minutes: "Perfect" produces 6–7 sentences (incomplete response)
  → A complete "good enough" response scores HIGHER than an incomplete "perfect" one

THE UPGRADE PATH:
  → Write "good enough" first drafts for ALL sections
  → Then use the Chapter 25 protocol to CATCH and FIX errors
  → The protocol upgrades "good enough" to "good" or even "very good"
  → This is MORE efficient than trying to write "perfect" during the writing phase

MARCUS'S MISTAKE:
  → He tried to write "perfect" during the writing phase
  → He rewrote his introduction twice → Wasted 2 minutes on 40 words
  → Those 2 minutes could have produced 22 additional words in the conclusion
  → FIRST DRAFT + PROTOCOL > POLISHED DRAFT + NO PROTOCOL

The "No Rewriting During Writing" Rule:

ABSOLUTE RULE:
  During Steps 5–7 (writing phase), you write FORWARD ONLY
  You NEVER go back to rewrite a sentence you have already completed
  If a sentence is imperfect but understandable → LEAVE IT → MOVE FORWARD

WHY:
  → Rewriting during writing = DOUBLE the time per sentence
  → Sentence 1 written + rewritten = 3 minutes
  → Sentence 1 written once = 1.5 minutes
  → Saved 1.5 minutes = 16 more words in a later section
  → The Chapter 25 protocol will catch errors LATER → More efficient

THE ONLY EXCEPTION:
  → If you realize MID-SENTENCE that you are going OFF-TOPIC
  → Cross out the partial sentence → Start the sentence fresh
  → This is a DIRECTION CORRECTION, not a quality rewrite
  → It prevents a Dimension 1 (consigne) violation → Worth the time

🔶 COMPONENT 4: THE PANIC RECOVERY SYSTEM

What to do when time has gone critically wrong

Scenario A: 15 Minutes Left, Only Introduction Written

SITUATION: You spent too long planning + writing the introduction
  → 15 minutes remain → You need Body 1 + Body 2 + Conclusion minimum

RECOVERY PROTOCOL:

  Minute 0–6: Write Body 1 — COMPRESSED
    → 3 sentences only (not 4–5)
    → 1 argument + 1 example + 1 consequence
    → Skip causal chain — use simple "parce que" / "c'est pourquoi"
    → Target: 45 words

  Minute 6–10: Write Body 2 — COMPRESSED
    → 2 sentences only
    → 1 concession ("Certes...mais") + 1 counter-argument
    → Target: 30 words

  Minute 10–12: Write Conclusion
    → 1–2 sentences
    → "En définitive, [position]. [Final thought]."
    → Target: 20 words

  Minute 12–15: Run Emergency Review (Scans 1 + 4 only)
    → Check structure exists (intro + body + conclusion) ✅
    → Check register ("ça" → "cela," missing "ne") ✅

  RESULT: Complete response at ~130–150 words
    → Below target word count → Some Dimension 1 penalty
    → BUT: All structural elements present → Dimension 2 protected
    → Better than: Excellent intro + Body 1 + nothing else
    → Estimated recovery: Lose 30–40 points vs. target → Save 50–70 points vs. incomplete response

Scenario B: 10 Minutes Left, Introduction + Body 1 Written

SITUATION: Body 1 took too long
  → 10 minutes remain → You need Body 2 + Conclusion + Review

RECOVERY PROTOCOL:

  Minute 0–5: Write Body 2 — STANDARD (not compressed)
    → 3–4 sentences
    → Include concession ("Certes...mais") → Saves nuance score
    → Target: 50 words

  Minute 5–7: Write Conclusion — STANDARD
    → 2 sentences
    → "En définitive, [synthesis]. [Final position]."
    → Target: 25 words

  Minute 7–10: Run Core Review (Scans 1–5)
    → Structure check ✅
    → Consigne check ✅
    → Cohesion check ✅
    → Register check ✅
    → Subjunctive check ✅

  RESULT: Complete response at ~185–210 words
    → Within acceptable word count range
    → Missing synthèse → Dimension 2 slightly lower (B2 ceiling)
    → BUT: All essential elements present → Solid B2 score maintained
    → Estimated: Lose 15–20 points vs. target → Save 40–60 points vs. panic response

Scenario C: 5 Minutes Left, Only Introduction Written

SITUATION: Severe time crisis — maximum emergency

RECOVERY PROTOCOL:

  Minute 0–2: Write ONE body paragraph — ULTRA-COMPRESSED
    → 2–3 sentences combining thèse + antithèse in one paragraph
    → "D'une part, [main argument]. D'autre part, certes [concession],
       mais [counter]. C'est pourquoi [consequence]."
    → Target: 40–50 words

  Minute 2–3: Write Conclusion — MINIMAL
    → 1 sentence
    → "En définitive, j'estime que [clear position]."
    → Target: 12–15 words

  Minute 3–5: Run Micro Review (Scan 1 + Scan 4 partial)
    → Structure: Introduction + Body + Conclusion exist ✅
    → Register: No "ça," no missing "ne" ✅

  RESULT: Complete response at ~100–120 words
    → Significantly below target → Dimension 1 penalty for length
    → BUT: Structure is present → Dimension 2 partially protected
    → Position is stated → Dimension 1 partially protected
    → Better than: Introduction only → MUCH better than panic freeze

  CRITICAL PRINCIPLE:
    → A short complete response ALWAYS scores higher than a long incomplete one
    → 120 words with intro + body + conclusion > 150 words with intro + body only
    → COMPLETION IS NON-NEGOTIABLE — even if quality suffers

Scenario D: 3 Minutes Left, Nothing Written After Planning

SITUATION: Extreme crisis — you over-planned or froze

RECOVERY PROTOCOL:

  THE 3-MINUTE COMPLETE RESPONSE:

  Sentence 1 (Introduction): "[Topic] suscite un débat important."
  Sentence 2 (Thesis): "Il me semble que [clear position]."
  Sentence 3 (Argument): "En effet, [main reason]."
  Sentence 4 (Example): "Par exemple, [concrete example]."
  Sentence 5 (Concession): "Certes, [opposing point], mais [counter]."
  Sentence 6 (Conclusion): "En définitive, [restate position]."

  → 6 sentences → Approximately 70–90 words
  → ALL structural elements present (intro + body + concession + conclusion)
  → ALL dimensions addressed minimally
  → Score: B1 level (180–225 range) → NOT your target but NOT catastrophic

  THIS IS YOUR ABSOLUTE EMERGENCY TEMPLATE
  → Memorize this 6-sentence structure
  → It can be deployed in 3 minutes for ANY prompt
  → It prevents a score of 0 or near-0
  → It is your PARACHUTE — you hope never to use it but you MUST have it

🔷 COMPONENT 5: THE REAL-TIME DECISION FRAMEWORK

How to make strategic decisions DURING the exam when things are not going according to plan

The Decision Points:

DECISION POINT 1: After Section A — "How much time do I have?"

  IF 35+ minutes → Standard pipeline → All sections at target quality
  IF 30–34 minutes → Standard pipeline → Reduce planning to 4 minutes
  IF 25–29 minutes → Compressed pipeline → Skip synthèse → Reduce review to Scans 1–4
  IF under 25 minutes → Emergency pipeline → Compressed everything → 6-sentence minimum

DECISION POINT 2: At Checkpoint 1 (Minute 6) — "Is my plan complete?"

  IF plan is complete → Proceed to writing ✅
  IF plan is 80% complete → Start writing → Finish planning MENTALLY while writing intro
  IF plan is less than 50% → STOP planning → Pick the strongest 2 arguments → START WRITING
  → An incomplete plan executed > A perfect plan never executed

DECISION POINT 3: At Checkpoint 2 (Minute 9) — "Is my intro done?"

  IF intro is done → Proceed to Body 1 ✅
  IF intro is almost done → Finish current sentence → Move to Body 1
  IF intro is far from done → Write ONE thesis sentence → That IS your intro → Move to Body 1
  → A 1-sentence intro + complete body > A 3-sentence intro + incomplete body

DECISION POINT 4: At Checkpoint 3 (Minute 16) — "Is Body 1 done?"

  IF Body 1 is done → Proceed to Body 2 ✅
  IF Body 1 is almost done → Finish current sentence → Move to Body 2
  IF Body 1 is far from done → Finish current sentence → Body 1 IS done → Move to Body 2
  → Never spend more than 8 minutes on Body 1 regardless of quality

DECISION POINT 5: At Checkpoint 4 (Minute 21) — "Is Body 2 done?"

  IF Body 2 is done AND you are targeting C1 → Write Synthèse (4 minutes)
  IF Body 2 is done AND you are targeting B2 → Skip Synthèse → Write Conclusion
  IF Body 2 is done AND you are behind schedule → Skip Synthèse → Write Conclusion → Extra review time
  IF Body 2 is NOT done → Finish current sentence → Move to Conclusion

DECISION POINT 6: At Minute 29 — "Is everything written?"

  IF yes → Run full 7-Scan Protocol (5 minutes) ✅
  IF conclusion is missing → Write 1-sentence conclusion (30 seconds) → Run Scans 1–5
  IF body is incomplete → Write 1-sentence conclusion → Run Scans 1 + 4 only
  → ALWAYS ensure a conclusion exists before starting review

🔷 COMPONENT 7: THE WRITING SPEED TECHNIQUES

How to physically write FASTER without sacrificing quality

7A — The Abbreviation-in-Planning Technique:

DURING PLANNING (Step 4), use abbreviations:
  → "env" = environnement
  → "cq" = conséquence
  → "→" = entraîne / conduit à
  → "≠" = en revanche / contrairement à
  → "+" = de plus / en outre
  → "∴" = c'est pourquoi / par conséquent
  → "ex:" = par exemple

THIS SAVES:
  → 30–60 seconds during planning
  → Your plan is for YOUR eyes only → Abbreviations are fine
  → The actual response must be in FULL French → No abbreviations in the final text

7B — The Pre-Formed Sentence Opening Technique:

MEMORIZE these sentence BEGINNINGS so they flow automatically:

INTRODUCTION OPENINGS (no thinking needed):
  → "À une époque où..."
  → "La question de... suscite un débat croissant."
  → "Il me semble que..."

BODY PARAGRAPH OPENINGS (instant deployment):
  → "En premier lieu, ..."
  → "D'une part, ... D'autre part, ..."
  → "En effet, ..."
  → "Cela entraîne..."
  → "Certes, ... mais ..."

CONCLUSION OPENINGS (automatic):
  → "En définitive, ..."
  → "J'estime donc que..."

WHEN THESE ARE AUTOMATIC:
  → You save 10–15 seconds per sentence opening
  → Over 12 sentences = 2–3 minutes saved
  → Those minutes go to content, not to "how do I start this sentence?"

7C — The "Think-Then-Write" Technique:

WRONG APPROACH: "Write-Think-Write" (thinking WHILE writing)
  → You start a sentence → You pause mid-sentence → You think → You continue
  → The pauses add up → Each sentence takes 2–3 minutes
  → Your handwriting becomes messy because your hand stops and starts

RIGHT APPROACH: "Think-Then-Write" (thinking BEFORE writing)
  → You THINK the entire sentence in your head first (5–10 seconds)
  → Then you WRITE it in one continuous flow (15–20 seconds)
  → No pauses → No crossed-out words → Clean handwriting → Faster output

HOW TO PRACTICE:
  → Before writing each sentence → Close your eyes for 3 seconds
  → Formulate the COMPLETE sentence mentally
  → Then open your eyes and write it in one go
  → After 10 practice responses → This becomes automatic
  → You will write 20–30% faster with BETTER quality

7D — The Handwriting Speed Optimization:

IF YOU ARE TAKING THE PAPER-BASED TEF:

  → Write LEGIBLY but not beautifully
  → The evaluator needs to READ it, not admire it
  → Slightly larger handwriting = faster than tiny careful writing
  → Use the FULL line width → Do not leave large margins
  → Skip lines between paragraphs (for visual structure) but NOT between sentences

IF YOU ARE TAKING THE COMPUTER-BASED TEF:

  → Practice typing in French BEFORE the exam
  → Know where accents are on the keyboard (é, è, ê, à, ù, ç)
  → Typing is generally FASTER than handwriting → Time pressure is slightly less
  → But typing errors are DIFFERENT → Scan 4 (register) must also check typos

  → Some candidates over-develop their strongest argument → 100+ words in Body 1
  → FIX: Set a HARD STOP at 75 words for Body 1 → If you have reached 75 words → Move to Body 2
  → Even if Body 1 could be "even better" → The marginal improvement is not worth the time

  → FIX: The concession is 1–2 sentences MAXIMUM → Then counter → Then move on

IDENTIFY YOUR TIME SINK:
  → Track your section timing across 5 practice responses
  → Which section consistently exceeds its time target?
  → That is YOUR time sink → Apply the specific FIX for that section

3. Build the "2-Minute Check" Habit

  → Compare with your checkpoint list (2 seconds)

              "Am I 1–2 minutes behind?" → Write slightly faster → Skip one detail
              "Am I 3+ minutes behind?" → Activate compression strategy

THIS HABIT:
  → Prevents the "surprise" of looking at the clock and finding 5 minutes left
  → Gives you EARLY WARNING of time problems
  → Allows GRADUAL adjustment instead of SUDDEN panic
  → Takes 4 seconds per check → Negligible time cost → Massive time management benefit

❌ WHAT SHOULD NOT DO

1. Do NOT Perfect Any Single Section at the Expense of Others

THE PERFECTIONISM TRAP:
  "This introduction isn't quite right... let me rewrite it..."
  → 2 minutes lost → Those 2 minutes come from the CONCLUSION
  → The evaluator sees: Perfect intro + No conclusion → WORSE than: Good intro + Good conclusion

THE ANTI-PERFECTIONISM RULE:
  → Once a section meets its TIME TARGET → Move on
  → Even if you KNOW it could be better
  → The marginal improvement in one section < The cost of missing another section
  → DISTRIBUTE quality evenly → Do NOT concentrate it

2. Do NOT Skip the Planning Phase to "Save Time"

THE FALSE ECONOMY:
  "If I skip planning, I gain 6 minutes for writing!"
  → WITHOUT a plan → You write 2 sentences → You realize your argument is weak
  → You cross out → You restart → You lose 4 minutes on false starts
  → Net "saving": 6 minutes gained − 4 minutes lost = 2 minutes
  → BUT: Your response is DISORGANIZED → Dimension 2 penalty = 20+ points lost

THE TRUTH:
  → 6 minutes of planning SAVES time during writing
  → You know EXACTLY what to write before you start
  → No false starts → No crossing out → No mid-writing direction changes
  → The plan is an INVESTMENT that pays back 2x–3x during writing
  → NEVER skip the plan → Compress it to 4 minutes if needed → But never skip it

3. Do NOT Check the Clock Every 30 Seconds

THE CLOCK OBSESSION:
  → Checking time every 30 seconds → 70 checks in 35 minutes
  → Each check breaks your WRITING FLOW
  → You lose your sentence mid-thought → You restart → Time wasted
  → The clock-checking itself becomes a time sink

THE BALANCED APPROACH:
  → Check the clock at CHECKPOINTS ONLY (6 times in 35 minutes)
  → Plus 2–3 informal glances during long writing sections
  → Total: 8–9 clock checks → Sufficient for time awareness
  → Between checks → FULL FOCUS on writing → No interruptions

4. Do NOT Attempt the Synthèse When You Are Behind Schedule

THE SYNTHÈSE DECISION:

  ON SCHEDULE at Checkpoint 4 (minute 21):
  → Include synthèse ✅ → 4 minutes available → C1 scoring potential

  1–3 MINUTES BEHIND at Checkpoint 4:
  → SKIP synthèse ❌ → Use saved 4 minutes for conclusion + full review
  → Score impact: Lose ~10–15 points from missing synthèse
  → Score saved: Gain ~20–30 points from having review time
  → NET GAIN: +5–15 points → Skipping synthèse is the CORRECT decision

  4+ MINUTES BEHIND at Checkpoint 4:
  → DEFINITELY skip synthèse → Compress conclusion → Maximum review time
  → The synthèse is a C1 BONUS → It is NOT worth sacrificing completion

  THE RULE:
  → Synthèse is the FIRST section to cut when time is short
  → It is the highest-value section per word → But ONLY if time allows
  → A complete B2 response (no synthèse) > An incomplete C1 attempt (with synthèse)

5. Do NOT Write the Conclusion in Your Head and "Save" It for Later

THE MENTAL CONCLUSION TRAP:
  "I know what my conclusion will be — I'll write it after the review"
  → You start the review → Time runs out → Conclusion never written
  → OR: You write the conclusion from memory → It does not match what you planned
  → OR: You forget what you planned → Conclusion is improvised and weak

THE RULE:
  → ALWAYS write the conclusion BEFORE starting the review
  → The conclusion is WRITTEN TEXT on the page → Non-negotiable
  → The review comes AFTER all writing is complete
  → Refer to Chapter 25: "Step 8 runs AFTER Step 7 — never during"

6. Do NOT Spend Extra Time on Sections You Enjoy

THE ENJOYMENT BIAS:
  → You enjoy writing causal chains (Chapter 15) → Body 1 gets 10 minutes
  → You dislike writing concessions (Chapter 16) → Body 2 gets 3 minutes
  → Result: Unbalanced response → Evaluator penalizes the weak Body 2

THE DISCIPLINE:
  → Each section gets its ALLOCATED time → Regardless of your preference
  → Treat time targets as HARD CONSTRAINTS, not suggestions
  → Your enjoyment is irrelevant → The evaluator's scoring grid is what matters

🕳️ COMMON PITFALLS AND FAILURE MODES

Pitfall 1: The Section A Time Theft

  • Spending 30+ minutes on Section A → Starving Section B
  • Prevention: Set a HARD STOP for Section A at minute 25 — no exceptions
  • Recovery: If Section A took 30 minutes → Start Section B with compressed pipeline immediately

Pitfall 2: The Planning Paralysis

  • Cannot start writing because the plan "isn't ready yet"
  • Prevention: Hard stop at 6 minutes → Write with whatever plan exists
  • Recovery: If 8 minutes spent planning → Start writing NOW → Plan was probably sufficient at minute 5

Pitfall 3: The Introduction Perfectionism

  • Rewriting the introduction 2–3 times
  • Prevention: Write ONE version → Move on → The protocol will polish it later
  • Recovery: If 5+ minutes spent on intro → Stop mid-sentence → Move to Body 1 → The intro IS done

Pitfall 4: The "Just One More Sentence" Trap

  • Staying in a section beyond its time target because "one more sentence would make it perfect"
  • Prevention: At the checkpoint → Finish current sentence → Move on → No exceptions
  • Recovery: The sentence you add to Body 1 STEALS from Body 2 or Conclusion → Not worth it

Pitfall 5: The Review Skip

  • "I'm out of time — I'll just submit without reviewing"
  • Prevention: Hard stop writing at minute 29 → Even 2 minutes of Scans 1+4 catches major errors
  • Recovery: Even if you feel the response is "good enough" → The protocol ALWAYS finds something → Run it

Pitfall 6: The Panic Spiral

  • Falling behind schedule → Panicking → Writing worse → Falling further behind → More panic
  • Prevention: The checkpoint system gives EARLY WARNING → Gradual adjustment, not sudden panic
  • Recovery: If panic hits → Take 3 deep breaths (5 seconds) → Activate compression strategy → Follow the decision matrix → The structure SAVES you even when emotion does not

Pitfall 7: The "I Write Slowly" Excuse

  • Some candidates believe they are inherently slow writers and cannot speed up
  • Truth: Speed comes from PREPARATION, not physical writing speed
    • → If you have to THINK about which connector to use → Slow
    • → If connectors are AUTOMATIC (memorized) → Fast
    • → If you have to THINK about vocabulary → Slow
    • → If vocabulary is PRE-SELECTED (Step 3) → Fast
    • → Writing speed is 80% mental preparation + 20% physical hand speed
  • Solution: Practice the pre-formed sentence openings (Component 7B) until they are automatic
