📘 CHAPTER 15

CAUSE AND EFFECT MASTERY: THE EXPRESSIONS THAT MAKE YOUR LOGIC VISIBLE

🔴 HYPOTHETICAL PROBLEM

You are a TEF candidate named Julien
Julien has completed Chapters 1–14 of his preparation
He understands the scoring grid (Chapter 1), can decode prompts (Chapter 2), formulates opinions (Chapter 3), gives basic reasons with "parce que / car / puisque" (Chapter 4), and has mastered the intermediate connector arsenal including opposition, addition, and consequence connectors (Chapter 13)
He has also learned the Thèse-Antithèse body structure (Chapter 14)
He sits down to write a practice response on this prompt:

"L'utilisation excessive des réseaux sociaux a-t-elle des conséquences négatives sur les relations humaines? Justifiez votre opinion."

Julien writes this body paragraph:

"Les réseaux sociaux sont mauvais parce que les gens ne se parlent plus. Les familles ne dînent plus ensemble parce que tout le monde regarde son téléphone. Les jeunes sont déprimés parce qu'ils comparent leur vie avec les autres. Les couples se séparent parce qu'il y a moins de communication."

His grammar → correct
His opinion → clear
His examples → present
But his evaluator marks Dimension 2 (Cohérence/Cohésion) as weak and Dimension 3 (Lexique) as repetitive
Julien is confused — he gave four reasons, all supported, all logical
What went wrong?

❓ THE CORE QUESTION

  • Why does repeating "parce que" four times destroy your score even when each reason is valid?
  • How do you express causality in French beyond the three basic tools learned in Chapter 4?
  • What is the difference between expressing a CAUSE and expressing an EFFECT — and why must you master BOTH?
  • How do you build a "causal chain" where one argument LEADS to the next instead of standing alone?

🟡 HOW TO APPROACH IT

Step 1: Diagnose Julien's Actual Problem

  • Julien's paragraph has FOUR arguments
  • Every single one uses "parce que" as its causal connector
  • This creates two simultaneous problems:

Problem A → Lexical Poverty (Dimension 3)

  • Using the same connector four times signals limited vocabulary
  • The evaluator sees: "This candidate knows only ONE way to express causality"
  • Even if the ideas are strong, the language wrapping them appears weak

Problem B → Flat Logical Architecture (Dimension 2)

  • Every sentence follows the identical pattern: [Result] + parce que + [Cause]
  • There is no variation in HOW causality is presented
  • There is no CHAIN → each sentence is independent
  • The paragraph reads like a list, not like a flowing argument
  • Refer to Chapter 7 (connector toolkit) → connectors must VARY to create flow
  • Refer to Chapter 13 (intermediate connectors) → you already learned opposition and consequence connectors, but Julien did not apply consequence connectors to causal reasoning

Step 2: Understand the Two Directions of Causality

  • Every cause-effect relationship can be expressed from TWO directions:

Direction 1 → FROM CAUSE TO EFFECT (Forward Logic)

  • You state the CAUSE first → then show what it PRODUCES
  • "L'utilisation excessive des réseaux sociaux provoque un isolement social."
  • Movement: Cause ➜ Effect

Direction 2 → FROM EFFECT TO CAUSE (Backward Logic)

  • You state the EFFECT first → then explain what CAUSED it
  • "L'isolement social s'explique par l'utilisation excessive des réseaux sociaux."
  • Movement: Effect ➜ Cause

Step 3: Recognize That Chapter 4 Only Taught Direction 2

  • "Parce que," "car," and "puisque" (Chapter 4) ALL work in Direction 2
  • They answer the question "WHY?" after stating a result
  • Chapter 15 now completes your causal toolkit by adding Direction 1 AND advanced Direction 2 expressions

🟢 HOW CAN WE SOLVE IT

THE COMPLETE CAUSE-EFFECT EXPRESSION SYSTEM

🔷 CATEGORY A: EXPRESSING THE CAUSE (What Produces the Effect)

These expressions NAME the cause — they answer "What is responsible?"

A1 → Positive Cause Expressions (The cause produces something GOOD)

ExpressionMeaningStructureExample
Grâce à + nounThanks toGrâce à + [positive cause], [positive result]Grâce à la technologie, les gens peuvent travailler à distance.
Grâce au fait que + clauseThanks to the fact thatGrâce au fait que + [subject + verb], [result]Grâce au fait que les écoles utilisent le numérique, l'apprentissage devient plus interactif.

A2 → Negative Cause Expressions (The cause produces something BAD)

ExpressionMeaningStructureExample
À cause de + nounBecause ofÀ cause de + [negative cause], [negative result]À cause de la pollution, de nombreuses espèces disparaissent.
À cause du fait que + clauseBecause of the fact thatÀ cause du fait que + [subject + verb], [result]À cause du fait que les jeunes passent trop de temps en ligne, leur santé mentale se dégrade.
En raison de + nounDue to / On account ofEn raison de + [cause], [result]En raison de la crise économique, le chômage a augmenté.

A3 → Neutral Cause Expressions (No positive/negative judgment)

ExpressionMeaningStructureExample
À la suite de + nounFollowing / As a result ofÀ la suite de + [event], [consequence]À la suite de la pandémie, le télétravail s'est généralisé.
Du fait de + nounDue to / Owing toDu fait de + [cause], [result]Du fait de la mondialisation, les cultures se mélangent davantage.
Étant donné + nounGivenÉtant donné + [circumstance], [result]Étant donné les progrès médicaux, l'espérance de vie a augmenté.
Étant donné que + clauseGiven thatÉtant donné que + [subject + verb], [result]Étant donné que la population augmente, les ressources deviennent insuffisantes.

🔶 CATEGORY B: EXPRESSING THE EFFECT (What the Cause Produces)

These expressions NAME the effect — they answer "What happens as a result?"

B1 → Verb-Based Effect Expressions

ExpressionMeaningStructureExample
Cela provoqueThis causes / triggers[Cause statement]. Cela provoque + [effect noun]Les écrans émettent de la lumière bleue. Cela provoque des troubles du sommeil.
Cela entraîneThis leads to / results in[Cause statement]. Cela entraîne + [effect noun]La déforestation massive détruit les habitats. Cela entraîne la disparition de nombreuses espèces.
Cela génèreThis generates[Cause statement]. Cela génère + [effect noun]Le tourisme de masse transforme les villes. Cela génère des tensions avec les habitants locaux.
Cela engendreThis gives rise to[Cause statement]. Cela engendre + [effect noun]L'inégalité économique persiste. Cela engendre un sentiment d'injustice sociale.
Cela conduit àThis leads to[Cause statement]. Cela conduit à + [effect noun/infinitive]Le manque d'exercice physique est préoccupant. Cela conduit à une augmentation des maladies chroniques.
Cela contribue àThis contributes to[Cause statement]. Cela contribue à + [effect noun/infinitive]La lecture quotidienne est bénéfique. Cela contribue à développer l'esprit critique.

B2 → Connector-Based Effect Expressions

Some of these were introduced in Chapter 13 as consequence connectors — here you see them specifically within CAUSAL CHAINS

ExpressionMeaningPositionExample

🔷 CATEGORY C: EXPRESSING CAUSE AND EFFECT IN A SINGLE SENTENCE

These are the most sophisticated structures — they compress the entire causal relationship into one flowing sentence

StructureExample
[Cause] entraîne [Effect]L'utilisation excessive des écrans entraîne une diminution de la concentration.
[Effect] résulte de [Cause]La solitude croissante des jeunes résulte d'un usage excessif des réseaux sociaux.
[Effect] s'explique par [Cause]Le déclin des relations humaines s'explique par la prédominance des échanges virtuels.
[Effect] est dû à [Cause]Ce phénomène est dû à un manque de communication authentique.
[Effect] est lié à [Cause]L'augmentation du stress est liée à la pression des réseaux sociaux.
[Effect] découle de [Cause]Ce problème découle d'une dépendance croissante à la technologie.

🔵 WHAT COULD BE DONE

Option A: Replace All "parce que" With Random Cause Expressions

  • → Technically improves lexical variety
  • → But risks using positive expressions for negative causes (grâce à for something bad)
  • → Creates semantic confusion
  • → NOT recommended

Option B: Use Only Effect Expressions and Abandon Cause Expressions

  • → Creates forward-only logic
  • → Loses the ability to explain WHY something happens
  • → Incomplete causal reasoning
  • → NOT recommended

Option C: Strategically Alternate Between Cause Expressions and Effect Expressions Within the Same Paragraph

  • → Creates dynamic logical flow
  • → Shows lexical range (Dimension 3)
  • → Demonstrates sophisticated coherence (Dimension 2)
  • → THIS IS THE CORRECT APPROACH

Option D: Build Causal Chains Where One Effect Becomes the Cause of the Next Effect

  • → This is the HIGHEST level of causal reasoning
  • → It creates an unbreakable logical flow
  • → It is what separates B2 from C1 responses
  • → THIS IS THE MASTERY TARGET — explained in detail below

✅ WHAT SHOULD DO

1. Match Polarity: Positive Cause = "Grâce à" / Negative Cause = "À cause de"

  • This is the MOST COMMON error candidates make with causal expressions
  • RULE:
    • → If the cause produces a POSITIVE outcome → use grâce à
    • → If the cause produces a NEGATIVE outcome → use à cause de
    • → If neutral or factual → use en raison de / du fait de / étant donné
✅ Correct❌ Incorrect
Grâce à l'éducation, les citoyens sont mieux informés.À cause de l'éducation, les citoyens sont mieux informés.
À cause de la pollution, l'air est irrespirable.Grâce à la pollution, l'air est irrespirable.

2. Vary Your Causal Expression Every Time You State a New Reason

  • Within a single paragraph of 3–4 arguments:
    • → Argument 1 → use a CAUSE expression (e.g., "En raison de...")
    • → Argument 2 → use an EFFECT expression (e.g., "Cela entraîne...")
    • → Argument 3 → use a single-sentence structure (e.g., "...s'explique par...")
    • → Argument 4 → use a connector-based effect (e.g., "C'est pourquoi...")
  • This variation alone can elevate your Dimension 3 score by an entire level

3. Build Causal Chains (The Most Powerful Technique in This Chapter)

What Is a Causal Chain?

  • A causal chain is when Effect A becomes the Cause of Effect B, which becomes the Cause of Effect C
  • It creates a DOMINO logic that the evaluator can follow effortlessly
  • It demonstrates intellectual depth (you see BEYOND the surface)

Julien's Original Paragraph (Flat — No Chain):

Cause → Effect (stop)
Cause → Effect (stop)
Cause → Effect (stop)
Cause → Effect (stop)
  • Each argument is independent
  • No argument LEADS to the next
  • The paragraph is a LIST, not a CHAIN

Julien's Rewritten Paragraph (Causal Chain):

Cause → Effect A
Effect A becomes Cause → Effect B
Effect B becomes Cause → Effect C
Effect C → Final Consequence

Concrete Example of This Chain:

En raison de l'utilisation excessive des réseaux sociaux, les individus passent moins de temps en interaction directe avec leur entourage. Cela entraîne un affaiblissement des liens familiaux, car les moments de partage, comme les repas, sont remplacés par des écrans. De ce fait, un sentiment d'isolement se développe progressivement, ce qui conduit à une augmentation des troubles psychologiques, notamment chez les jeunes. C'est pourquoi de nombreux spécialistes recommandent de limiter le temps passé sur ces plateformes.

Analysis of the Chain:

StepExpression UsedRole in Chain
Step 1En raison deNames the initial CAUSE
Step 2Cela entraîneShows EFFECT A (weakened family bonds)
Step 3De ce faitEffect A → becomes cause of EFFECT B (isolation)
Step 4ce qui conduit àEffect B → becomes cause of EFFECT C (psychological disorders)
Step 5C'est pourquoiFinal consequence / conclusion drawn from the entire chain
  • Notice: "parce que" appears ZERO times
  • Notice: FIVE different causal/effect expressions are used
  • Notice: Each sentence pulls the reader into the next
  • This is what a B2–C1 level causal paragraph looks like

4. Use "Cela" as Your Chain-Building Pronoun

  • "Cela" (or "Ceci") is the invisible glue of causal chains
  • It refers back to the ENTIRE previous idea without repeating it
  • It allows you to start a new sentence that is automatically connected to the previous one
  • Refer to Chapter 19 (cohesion tools) for deeper pronoun reference techniques — but "Cela" is your essential tool NOW

5. Place Cause Expressions at the BEGINNING of Your Paragraph, Effect Expressions at the END

  • Paragraph opening → State the root cause clearly
    • → "En raison de... / À cause de... / Du fait de..."
  • Paragraph middle → Develop the chain of effects
    • → "Cela provoque... / Cela entraîne... / De ce fait..."
  • Paragraph closing → State the final consequence or draw a conclusion
    • → "C'est pourquoi... / Par conséquent... / Ainsi..."
  • This creates a natural FLOW from cause → through effects → to conclusion

❌ WHAT SHOULD NOT DO

1. Do NOT Use "Grâce à" for Negative Outcomes

  • As established above — this is a polarity violation
  • "Grâce à" ALWAYS implies gratitude or positive attribution
  • Using it for negative causes sounds absurd to a French evaluator
  • → It is the equivalent of saying "Thanks to pollution, people are dying" in English — grammatically possible but semantically jarring and penalized

2. Do NOT Use the Same Causal Expression Twice in One Paragraph

  • If you use "Cela entraîne" in sentence 2, do NOT use it again in sentence 4
  • Switch to "Cela provoque" or "Cela conduit à" or "De ce fait"
  • Repetition of ANY connector (not just "parce que") signals limited range

3. Do NOT Stack Multiple Cause Expressions Without Showing Any Effect

  • ❌ "En raison de la technologie, à cause des réseaux sociaux, du fait de la mondialisation, les gens changent."
  • → This piles up three causes before one effect
  • → It is syntactically overloaded and confusing
  • → One cause → One effect → Then move to the next cause-effect pair

4. Do NOT Confuse "Entraîner" (to lead to) With "S'entraîner" (to train/practice)

  • "Cela entraîne des problèmes" ✅ → This leads to problems
  • "Cela s'entraîne des problèmes" ❌ → Grammatically nonsensical
  • The reflexive form changes the meaning entirely

5. Do NOT Use Causal Chains When the Prompt Asks for a Simple Opinion

  • If the prompt says "Êtes-vous pour ou contre?" → You need clear positioning (Chapter 8)
  • Causal chains SUPPORT your position — they do not REPLACE it
  • Always state your opinion FIRST (Chapter 3), then use causal chains to JUSTIFY it

6. Do NOT Forget That "Car" and "Puisque" Still Have Value

  • Chapter 4 tools are not obsolete
  • They are useful for QUICK, mid-sentence justifications
  • Chapter 15 tools are for PARAGRAPH-LEVEL causal architecture
  • Use BOTH — but never rely on Chapter 4 tools ALONE anymore

⏱️ WHEN TO USE WHAT

SituationExpression Category to Use
Starting a paragraph by naming the ROOT cause→ Category A: "En raison de... / À cause de... / Du fait de..."
Showing what the cause PRODUCES in the next sentence→ Category B (verb-based): "Cela entraîne... / Cela provoque..."
Connecting the effect of one sentence to the next consequence→ Category B (connector-based): "De ce fait... / Par conséquent... / C'est pourquoi..."
Compressing cause + effect into ONE elegant sentence→ Category C: "...résulte de... / ...s'explique par... / ...est lié à..."
Attributing a POSITIVE outcome to its source→ "Grâce à..." (NEVER for negative outcomes)
Attributing a NEGATIVE outcome to its source→ "À cause de..." (NEVER for positive outcomes)
Stating a fact-based cause without judgment→ "En raison de... / Étant donné... / Du fait de..."
Quick mid-sentence justification within a longer chain→ Chapter 4 tools: "car / puisque" (lightweight connectors)
Building a multi-step logical argument across 4–5 sentences→ Full causal chain (Category A opening → Category B middle → Category B connector closing)

🚫 WHEN NOT TO USE

When NOT to Use Heavy Causal Expressions

  • In your Introduction → Keep it light, contextual

    • → The introduction frames the debate (Chapter 10), it does not build causal chains
    • → A simple "car" or "en effet" is sufficient if causality appears in the intro

  • In your Conclusion → Keep it synthetic, not analytical

    • → The conclusion summarizes and reinforces (Chapter 11)
    • → Using "Cela entraîne..." in a conclusion sounds like you are STILL arguing, not concluding
    • → Use "C'est pourquoi" or "Ainsi" as final-position connectors instead

  • When you have only one reason to give

    • → A causal chain requires at least 2–3 linked steps
    • → If you have only one cause-effect pair, a simple "parce que" or "en raison de" is sufficient
    • → Do not force a chain where the logic does not naturally extend

⚙️ HOW IT ONLY WORKS (INTERNAL MECHANICS)

The Cognitive Architecture of Causal Reasoning in French

LEVEL 1 (Chapter 4):
  Statement + parce que / car / puisque + Reason
  → BACKWARD logic only (effect stated first, cause explained after)
  → Single link only (one cause, one effect)
  → Sufficient for A2–B1 scoring

  Statement. Par conséquent / Ainsi + Consequence

  Root Cause (Category A) → Effect A (Category B verb)
  → Effect A becomes new cause → Effect B (Category B connector)
  → Effect B becomes new cause → Effect C (Category C single-sentence)
  → Final Consequence (closing connector)
  → MULTI-DIRECTIONAL logic
  → CHAIN structure (3–5 linked steps)
  → Required for B2–C1 scoring

Critical Grammar Alert: "Est dû à" Agreement

  • "Dû" agrees with the subject in gender and number:
    • → Ce problème est dû à... (masculine singular)
    • → Cette situation est due à... (feminine singular)
    • → Ces difficultés sont dues à... (feminine plural)
    • → Ces changements sont dus à... (masculine plural)
  • This is a frequent error that evaluators specifically watch for

🕳️ COMMON PITFALLS AND FAILURE MODES

Pitfall 1: The Polarity Swap

  • Using "grâce à" for negative causes or "à cause de" for positive causes
  • Detection method → After writing, ask: "Is the outcome positive or negative?"
  • Positive → grâce à / Negative → à cause de / Neutral → en raison de

Pitfall 2: The Broken Chain

  • Starting a causal chain but failing to connect step 2 to step 3
  • Effect A is stated → then a completely NEW, unrelated cause is introduced
  • Detection method → After writing your chain, read it aloud and ask: "Does each sentence FLOW from the previous one?"

Pitfall 3: The Circular Chain

  • Effect C accidentally restates the original Cause
  • "À cause du stress, les gens dorment mal. Cela entraîne de la fatigue. De ce fait, ils sont stressés."
  • → You ended where you started → This is circular, not progressive
  • Detection method → Your final effect must be DIFFERENT from your initial cause

Pitfall 4: The Over-Chained Paragraph

  • Building a 7–8 step chain that loses the reader
  • → Maximum recommended chain length: 4–5 steps
  • → Beyond that, start a new paragraph with a new argument

Pitfall 5: Forgetting to Actually State an Opinion

  • Getting so absorbed in building a beautiful causal chain that you forget to state whether you are FOR or AGAINST
  • → Causal chains SERVE your opinion (Chapter 3) — they do not replace it
  • → Always anchor your chain to your stated position

Pitfall 6: Using "Cela" Without a Clear Referent

  • "Cela" must refer to the IMMEDIATELY preceding idea
  • If there are multiple ideas in the previous sentence, "Cela" becomes ambiguous
  • Solution → When ambiguous, replace "Cela" with a brief noun phrase summary
    • → Instead of "Cela entraîne..." → "Cette dépendance technologique entraîne..."

🔑 KEY TAKEAWAY

What Chapter 15 Established

CAUSALITY IN FRENCH HAS THREE LEVELS:
  Level 1 (Chapter 4): parce que / car / puisque → single backward link
  Level 2 (Chapter 13): Par conséquent / Ainsi → single forward link
  Level 3 (THIS CHAPTER): Full causal chain system

THREE CATEGORIES OF CAUSAL EXPRESSION:
  Category A → Names the CAUSE (grâce à, à cause de, en raison de, du fait de, étant donné)
  Category B → Names the EFFECT (cela entraîne, cela provoque, de ce fait, c'est pourquoi)
  Category C → Compresses both into ONE sentence (résulte de, s'explique par, est dû à)

THE CAUSAL CHAIN is the highest-scoring paragraph architecture:
  Root Cause → Effect A → Effect B → Effect C → Final Consequence
  Each step uses a DIFFERENT expression
  Each effect becomes the cause of the next effect
  Maximum chain length: 4–5 steps

POLARITY RULE:
  Positive outcome → grâce à
  Negative outcome → à cause de
  Neutral/factual → en raison de / du fait de / étant donné
