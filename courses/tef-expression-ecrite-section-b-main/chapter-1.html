📘 CHAPTER 1

THE INVISIBLE RULES: WHAT TEF SECTION B ACTUALLY DEMANDS FROM YOU

🔴 HYPOTHETICAL PROBLEM

You are a TEF candidate named Priya
You have decent French grammar knowledge
You can write sentences, conjugate verbs, use some vocabulary
You sit for the TEF exam and reach Expression Écrite Section B
You see a prompt asking your opinion about a social topic
You write freely — sharing your thoughts in French like a journal entry
You feel confident — you wrote 200 words, no major spelling errors
Result: You score 158/450 (far below your target of 310+)
You are confused — your French was correct, so why did you fail?

❓ THE CORE QUESTION

  • Why does grammatically correct French still fail in TEF Section B?
  • What is TEF Section B actually measuring that Priya missed entirely?
  • What are the invisible rules that separate a failing response from a scoring one?

🟡 HOW TO APPROACH IT

Step 1: Understand What TEF Expression Écrite Actually Is

  • TEF = Test d'Évaluation de Français
  • It is a standardized test recognized by IRCC (Canada), French universities, and international institutions
  • Expression Écrite is ONE of five sections in TEF
  • It has TWO parts:
    • Section A → Writing a formal/informal message (letter, email, etc.)
    • Section B → Writing an argumentative response expressing and defending an opinion
  • Section B is worth a significant portion of your writing score
  • Total time for BOTH Section A and Section B combined = 60 minutes
  • This means Section B gets roughly 30–35 minutes of your time

Step 2: Understand What Section B Specifically Asks You to Do

  • You receive a prompt (called a "sujet" or "consigne")
  • This prompt presents a debatable topic — something people can disagree about
  • You must write a structured argumentative text of approximately 200–250 words
  • You are NOT writing a story
  • You are NOT writing a letter
  • You are NOT writing a description
  • You ARE writing an opinion-based argument with logical support

Step 3: Understand the Prompt Format You Will See

  • Typical Section B prompts look like this:

"De nos jours, de plus en plus de gens préfèrent travailler à distance. Pensez-vous que le télétravail soit bénéfique pour la société? Justifiez votre point de vue avec des arguments et des exemples."

  • Translation of what is being asked:
    • Here is a topic (remote work)
    • Here is a debatable angle (is it beneficial?)
    • Here is your task (give your opinion + justify it + use examples)

Step 4: Understand What "Justifiez votre point de vue" Really Means

  • It does NOT mean "say what you feel"
  • It MEANS:
    • → State a clear position (for or against or nuanced)
    • → Give organized reasons WHY you hold this position
    • → Support each reason with a concrete example or evidence
    • → Structure everything in a logical, readable flow
    • → Open the text properly and close it properly

🟢 HOW CAN WE SOLVE IT

The Solution = Understanding the 4 Scoring Dimensions BEFORE Writing

  • TEF evaluators do NOT read your text and give a "general impression" score
  • They use a structured scoring grid (grille d'évaluation)
  • This grid evaluates your text across 4 distinct dimensions
  • If you fail in ANY dimension, your total score drops dramatically
  • Priya failed because she only addressed ONE dimension (grammar) and ignored three others

THE 4 SCORING DIMENSIONS OF TEF SECTION B

🔹 DIMENSION 1: Respect de la consigne (Following the Instructions)

  • Did you actually answer the question that was asked?
  • Did you write the right type of text (argumentative, not narrative)?
  • Did you meet the word count expectation (approximately 200–250 words)?
  • Did you address ALL parts of the prompt (opinion + justification + examples)?
  • Priya's mistake → She wrote freely without checking if she addressed every element the prompt demanded

🔹 DIMENSION 2: Cohérence et cohésion (Coherence and Cohesion)

  • Coherence = Does your text make logical sense from beginning to end?
    • → Does each idea connect to the next?
    • → Is there a clear progression of thought?
    • → Can a reader follow your reasoning without getting lost?
  • Cohesion = Are you using the right tools to LINK your ideas?
    • → Logical connectors (d'abord, ensuite, cependant, en conclusion...)
    • → Pronoun references that avoid repetition
    • → Paragraph organization that separates different argument stages
  • Priya's mistake → She jumped from idea to idea randomly, with no connectors, no paragraph breaks, no logical flow

🔹 DIMENSION 3: Compétence lexicale (Vocabulary Quality)

  • Are you using vocabulary appropriate to the topic?
  • Are you using varied vocabulary (not repeating the same 10 words)?
  • Are you using vocabulary at the B2+ level (not just basic A2 words)?
  • Are your word choices precise (the RIGHT word, not just ANY word)?
  • Priya's mistake → She used very basic, repetitive vocabulary that signaled A2 level, not B2

🔹 DIMENSION 4: Compétence grammaticale (Grammar Quality)

  • Are your verb conjugations correct?
  • Are your gender/number agreements correct?
  • Are you using varied grammatical structures (not just subject + verb + object every time)?
  • Are you attempting complex structures (relative clauses, subjunctive, conditional)?
  • Priya's partial success → She did reasonably well here, but this is only ONE of four dimensions

🔵 WHAT COULD BE DONE

Option A: Memorize model responses and reproduce them

  • → Possible but extremely risky
  • → If the prompt differs even slightly from what you memorized, you fail Dimension 1
  • → Evaluators recognize memorized templates and penalize them

Option B: Focus only on grammar perfection

  • → This is what Priya did
  • → It addresses only 1 of 4 dimensions
  • → Maximum possible score is capped because 3 dimensions remain weak

Option C: Learn the scoring system FIRST, then build skills dimension by dimension

  • → This is the correct approach
  • → You understand WHAT is being measured before you try to perform
  • → You build targeted skills for each dimension
  • → Your practice becomes purposeful, not random

Option D: Write as much as possible in French every day without structure

  • → Builds fluency but NOT exam performance
  • → Without understanding the scoring grid, you reinforce bad habits
  • → Volume without direction = wasted effort for TEF purposes

✅ WHAT SHOULD DO

1. Internalize the Scoring Grid as Your Mental Compass

  • Before writing a single word in any practice session, ask yourself:
    • → Am I answering the EXACT question? (Dimension 1)
    • → Am I organizing my ideas logically with connectors? (Dimension 2)
    • → Am I using rich, varied, topic-appropriate vocabulary? (Dimension 3)
    • → Am I using correct and varied grammar? (Dimension 4)

2. Always Read the Prompt Twice — Minimum

  • First read → Understand the topic generally
  • Second read → Identify the EXACT task (opinion? advantages/disadvantages? solution proposal?)

3. Accept That Section B Is NOT Free Writing

  • It is structured argumentation
  • It follows specific French academic conventions
  • It has an expected format: Introduction → Body → Conclusion
  • Deviating from this format costs you points in Dimension 2

4. Recognize That Word Count Matters

  • Too few words (under 150) → Penalized in Dimension 1
  • Too many words (over 300) → Risk of more errors, time pressure, no extra credit
  • Sweet spot → 200–250 words
  • Learn to feel this length instinctively through practice

5. Understand That TEF Section B Tests THINKING in French, Not Just Writing

  • The evaluator is checking:
    • → Can you think logically?
    • → Can you organize thoughts?
    • → Can you persuade?
    • → Can you do ALL of this in French?

❌ WHAT SHOULD NOT DO

1. Do NOT Write a Personal Story

  • Section B is not a narrative
  • "When I was young, my mother told me..." → This is storytelling, not argumentation
  • Personal anecdotes are only acceptable as brief EXAMPLES to support an argument, never as the main content

2. Do NOT List Random Opinions Without Structure

  • "I think technology is good. Also, education is important. And health matters too."
  • → This is a list of disconnected thoughts
  • → It scores near zero on Dimension 2 (coherence/cohesion)

3. Do NOT Ignore the Prompt to Write About Something You Prepared

  • If the prompt asks about "remote work" and you write about "the environment" because you prepared that topic
  • → You score ZERO on Dimension 1
  • → All other dimensions become irrelevant

4. Do NOT Write in Informal/Spoken French

  • No "j'sais pas," no "c'est trop bien," no "genre," no "truc"
  • Section B expects formal written register
  • Informal language signals low proficiency to evaluators

5. Do NOT Skip the Introduction or Conclusion

  • Some candidates jump directly into arguments
  • This immediately signals poor structural awareness
  • Even a brief 2-sentence introduction and 2-sentence conclusion dramatically improve your Dimension 2 score

6. Do NOT Spend All 60 Minutes on Section B

  • Remember: Section A and Section B share 60 minutes
  • If you spend 50 minutes on Section B, Section A suffers catastrophically
  • Budget: ~25 minutes Section A, ~35 minutes Section B (approximate)

⏱️ WHEN TO USE WHAT

SituationWhat to Apply
You see the prompt for the first time→ Activate Dimension 1 thinking: "What EXACTLY is being asked?"
You start planning your response→ Activate Dimension 2 thinking: "How will I organize this logically?"
You begin writing sentences→ Activate Dimensions 3 + 4 simultaneously: vocabulary precision + grammatical accuracy
You finish writing→ Run a mental checklist across all 4 dimensions before moving on
You have 3 minutes left→ Prioritize fixing Dimension 2 (add a missing connector or conclusion) over fixing a spelling error (Dimension 4) — coherence errors cost more points

🚫 WHEN NOT TO USE

When NOT to Overthink the Scoring Grid

  • During the actual writing phase, do NOT pause after every sentence to check all 4 dimensions
  • The grid is for planning (before writing) and reviewing (after writing)
  • During writing, let your preparation guide you naturally
  • Constant self-monitoring during writing → slow output → incomplete response → penalized

When NOT to Apply Section B Rules

  • Section A is a DIFFERENT task (message, letter, email)
  • The scoring dimensions overlap but the FORMAT expectations differ completely
  • Do NOT write an argumentative essay when Section A asks for an email

⚙️ HOW IT ONLY WORKS (INTERNAL MECHANICS)

The Evaluator's Actual Process

Step 1 → Evaluator reads your response completely once (general impression)
Step 2 → Evaluator re-reads with the scoring grid in hand
Step 3 → For EACH dimension, evaluator assigns a level:
         → Level 0: Not achieved
         → Level 1: Partially achieved
         → Level 2: Adequately achieved
         → Level 3: Well achieved
         → Level 4: Excellently achieved
Step 4 → Levels across all 4 dimensions are combined into a composite score
Step 5 → This composite score maps to the TEF point scale

Why This Matters

  • You cannot compensate one weak dimension with another strong one beyond a certain point
  • A response with PERFECT grammar but ZERO structure will score lower than a response with GOOD grammar and GOOD structure
  • Balance across all 4 dimensions is the scoring secret
  • Think of it as a table with 4 legs → if one leg is missing, the table falls regardless of how strong the other 3 are

The Invisible Weighting

  • Dimensions 1 and 2 (consigne + coherence) are often considered the foundation
  • If you fail these, Dimensions 3 and 4 cannot save you
  • If you succeed at Dimensions 1 and 2 with moderate performance on 3 and 4, you can still achieve a respectable score
  • Hierarchy: Consigne → Coherence → Vocabulary → Grammar
  • This does NOT mean grammar is unimportant — it means grammar alone is insufficient

🕳️ COMMON PITFALLS AND FAILURE MODES

Pitfall 1: The Grammar Perfectionist Trap

  • Candidate focuses exclusively on zero grammar errors
  • Writes only simple sentences to avoid mistakes
  • Result → Perfect grammar, childlike argumentation → Low score

Pitfall 2: The Off-Topic Trap

  • Candidate misreads the prompt
  • Writes a beautiful, well-structured text about the WRONG topic
  • Result → Zero on Dimension 1 → Everything else is irrelevant

Pitfall 3: The Word Count Panic

  • Candidate writes 100 words and thinks it is enough
  • Or candidate writes 400 words and runs out of time for review
  • Result → Penalized for not meeting expectations OR penalized for rushed errors

Pitfall 4: The "I'll Just Write Naturally" Trap

  • Candidate believes conversational French ability = writing ability
  • Writes as if texting a friend
  • Result → Register mismatch → Low scores on Dimensions 3 and 4

Pitfall 5: The Single-Dimension Focus

  • Candidate practices only vocabulary lists OR only grammar drills
  • Never practices writing a COMPLETE structured response
  • Result → Strong in isolation, weak in integration → Low composite score

🔑 KEY TAKEAWAY

What Chapter 1 Established

TEF Section B is NOT a free-writing exercise
It is a SCORED PERFORMANCE evaluated across 4 specific dimensions:
  1. Consigne (Did you answer the right question?)
  2. Cohérence/Cohésion (Is your text logically organized?)
  3. Lexique (Is your vocabulary rich and appropriate?)
  4. Grammaire (Is your grammar correct and varied?)

Balance across all 4 = high score
Excellence in 1 but failure in others = low score
The scoring grid is your invisible compass for EVERYTHING that follows

Why This Must Come First

  • Without understanding WHAT is being measured, all practice is blind
  • Every technique in Chapters 2–30 exists to improve one or more of these 4 dimensions
  • You will now always know WHY you are learning something, not just WHAT
