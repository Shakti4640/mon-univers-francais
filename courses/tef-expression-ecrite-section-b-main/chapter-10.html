📘 CHAPTER 10

THE INTRODUCTION FORMULA: FRAMING THE DEBATE IN THREE SENTENCES

🔴 HYPOTHETICAL PROBLEM

You are a TEF candidate named Fatima
She completed Phase 1 → She can assemble complete responses
She studied Chapters 7–9 → She has connectors, bilateral thinking, and concrete examples
She writes responses that score well on body content
But she notices a pattern in her self-scoring: her introductions are WEAK

Here are the introductions from her last 5 practice responses:

Response 1: "Je pense que la technologie est importante. Je vais expliquer pourquoi."

Response 2: "De nos jours, beaucoup de gens parlent de l'environnement. Je suis convaincue que c'est un sujet important."

Response 3: "La question posée est intéressante. Je vais donner mon opinion."

Response 4: "Aujourd'hui, tout le monde utilise Internet. Je pense que c'est bien."

Response 5: "Le sujet de l'éducation est très important dans notre société. Je suis d'accord avec cette idée."

Fatima notices these introductions all share the same problems:

  • They are VAGUE ("important," "intéressant," "bien")
  • They ANNOUNCE what she will do instead of DOING it ("Je vais expliquer," "Je vais donner mon opinion")
  • They use only Tier 1 opinion markers ("Je pense que")
  • They do NOT frame the specific DEBATE — they state the general TOPIC
  • They create a WEAK first impression

The evaluator reads the introduction FIRST
Within 10 seconds, the evaluator forms an initial level assessment
Fatima's introductions signal A2–B1 level before the evaluator even reaches her strong body paragraphs
Her excellent body content must fight UPHILL against a poor first impression

❓ THE CORE QUESTION

  • Why do most candidates write weak introductions even when their body content is strong?
  • What specific FORMULAS exist for writing B2-level introductions?
  • How do you transform the 3 slots from Chapter 5 (Amorce → Problématique → Thèse) into polished, impressive sentences?
  • What are the specific sentence patterns that signal "this candidate writes at B2 level"?
  • How do you create a strong first impression in just 30–50 words?

🟡 HOW TO APPROACH IT

Step 1: Understand WHY the Introduction Is Disproportionately Important

THE FIRST IMPRESSION EFFECT:
│
├── PSYCHOLOGICAL REALITY:
│   ├── Evaluators are human — they form impressions quickly
│   ├── The first 2–3 sentences create an ANCHOR IMPRESSION
│   ├── This anchor BIASES the reading of everything that follows
│   ├── Strong anchor → Evaluator reads body with positive expectations
│   │   → Ambiguous sentences get interpreted favorably
│   └── Weak anchor → Evaluator reads body with skeptical expectations
│       → Ambiguous sentences get interpreted unfavorably
│
├── THE ANCHORING MATH:
│   ├── Introduction = ~15–20% of your word count
│   ├── But it creates ~40–50% of the evaluator's INITIAL impression
│   ├── This means each introduction word has 2–3x the impact of a body word
│   └── Investing effort in introduction quality has OUTSIZED returns
│
└── FATIMA'S SPECIFIC PROBLEM:
    ├── Her introductions signal A2–B1 level
    ├── Her body content demonstrates B2 level
    ├── The evaluator must OVERRIDE their initial impression
    ├── This override is cognitively harder than CONFIRMING an impression
    └── Result: Her true B2 ability is UNDERVALUED because of the weak opening

Step 2: Diagnose the 5 Common Introduction Diseases

DISEASE 1: THE ANNOUNCEMENT DISEASE
├── Symptom: "Je vais expliquer..." / "Je vais donner mon opinion..." / "Je vais parler de..."
├── Diagnosis: Candidate ANNOUNCES what they will do instead of DOING it
├── Why it fails: The evaluator wants to see your argument, not a preview of it
├── Cure: DELETE all announcements → Replace with the actual thesis
│   ❌ "Je vais expliquer pourquoi la technologie est importante."
│   ✅ "Je suis fermement convaincue que la technologie constitue un levier
│       essentiel de progrès pour nos sociétés."
└── The second version DOES what the first version merely PROMISES

DISEASE 2: THE VAGUENESS DISEASE
├── Symptom: "C'est un sujet important" / "C'est intéressant" / "C'est bien"
├── Diagnosis: Candidate uses empty adjectives that apply to ANY topic
├── Why it fails: "Important" and "intéressant" carry ZERO analytical content
├── Cure: Replace vague adjectives with SPECIFIC, topic-related language
│   ❌ "L'environnement est un sujet important."
│   ✅ "La dégradation environnementale constitue l'un des défis les plus
│       urgents auxquels nos sociétés sont confrontées."
└── The second version is SPECIFIC — it names the exact nature of the importance

DISEASE 3: THE COPY-PASTE DISEASE
├── Symptom: Introduction repeats the prompt's exact words
├── Diagnosis: Candidate lacks the vocabulary to REPHRASE
├── Why it fails: Shows no linguistic autonomy → Evaluator sees: "Cannot reformulate"
├── Cure: Read the prompt → Close your eyes → Express the same idea in YOUR words
│   Prompt: "De plus en plus de personnes choisissent de manger végétarien."
│   ❌ "De plus en plus de personnes choisissent de manger végétarien."
│   ✅ "Le régime végétarien séduit un nombre croissant d'individus à travers le monde."
└── Same idea, DIFFERENT words → Demonstrates reformulation skill

DISEASE 4: THE TOPIC-WITHOUT-DEBATE DISEASE
├── Symptom: Introduction states the topic but not the DEBATE
├── Diagnosis: Candidate names what the text is about but not what QUESTION is being explored
├── Why it fails: The evaluator sees a topic label, not an argumentative framing
├── Cure: Always include the TENSION or QUESTION within the introduction
│   ❌ "Aujourd'hui, beaucoup de gens utilisent Internet." (topic only)
│   ✅ "Si Internet a indéniablement transformé nos modes de communication,
│       la question de son impact réel sur la qualité des échanges humains
│       reste vivement débattue." (topic + debate + tension)
└── The second version creates TENSION that the body will resolve

DISEASE 5: THE OPINION-TOO-EARLY DISEASE
├── Symptom: Very first sentence is the opinion with no context
├── Diagnosis: Candidate skips context and jumps directly to thesis
├── Why it fails: No framing → The reader is dropped into a position without understanding the landscape
├── Cure: Always provide at least ONE sentence of context before stating your position
│   ❌ "Je pense que le sport est essentiel." (opinion with zero context)
│   ✅ "Dans une société de plus en plus sédentaire, la question de la place
│       du sport dans notre quotidien se pose avec acuité. Je suis convaincue
│       que l'activité physique constitue un pilier fondamental d'une vie équilibrée."
└── The second version: Context FIRST → Then position

Step 3: Recall the 3-Slot Blueprint From Chapter 5

CHAPTER 5 ESTABLISHED:
│
├── SLOT A: L'AMORCE (The Hook / Context Opener)
│   └── 1 sentence introducing the general topic
│
├── SLOT B: LA PROBLÉMATIQUE (The Issue / Debate Framing)
│   └── 1 sentence narrowing to the specific question/tension
│
└── SLOT C: LA THÈSE (The Thesis Announcement)
    └── 1 sentence stating your position (Chapter 3 opinion marker)

Chapter 5 gave the SKELETON
Chapter 10 gives the FLESH — specific formulas for each slot

🟢 HOW CAN WE SOLVE IT

The Solution = Mastering Specific Sentence Formulas for Each Slot

  • Each slot has multiple formula options
  • You select the formula that best fits your prompt
  • Formulas are TEMPLATES with blanks you fill based on the topic
  • Mastering 2–3 formulas per slot gives you enough flexibility for any prompt

🔹 SLOT A — L'AMORCE: 6 Formulas for Opening Your Introduction

FORMULA A1: The "De nos jours" Temporal Opener

TEMPLATE: "De nos jours, [phenomenon/trend] [verb describing its development]."

EXAMPLES:
├── "De nos jours, le numérique occupe une place prépondérante dans notre quotidien."
├── "De nos jours, la question de l'environnement se trouve au cœur des préoccupations mondiales."
└── "De nos jours, les modes de travail connaissent une transformation sans précédent."

REGISTER: Standard B1–B2
RISK: Overused — many candidates start with "De nos jours"
RECOMMENDATION: Use occasionally, but vary with other formulas

FORMULA A2: The "Depuis quelques années" Evolution Opener

TEMPLATE: "Depuis quelques années/décennies, [phenomenon] [verb of change/growth/development]."

EXAMPLES:
├── "Depuis quelques années, le télétravail s'est imposé comme une alternative crédible au travail en présentiel."
├── "Depuis plusieurs décennies, la mondialisation transforme profondément les échanges culturels et économiques."
└── "Depuis l'avènement d'Internet, nos habitudes de consommation ont été radicalement bouleversées."

REGISTER: Formal B2
ADVANTAGE: Shows temporal awareness — situates the topic in a timeline
BEST FOR: Prompts about trends, changes, or evolutions (Type 5)

FORMULA A3: The "Il est indéniable que" Factual Opener

TEMPLATE: "Il est indéniable que [widely accepted fact about the topic]."

EXAMPLES:
├── "Il est indéniable que les nouvelles technologies ont profondément modifié notre rapport au savoir."
├── "Il est indéniable que la protection de l'environnement constitue un enjeu majeur du XXIe siècle."
└── "Il est indéniable que l'éducation joue un rôle déterminant dans le développement des sociétés."

REGISTER: Formal B2
ADVANTAGE: Creates authority — starts with a statement no one can disagree with
BEST FOR: Any topic — universally applicable
NOTE: This is also a Tier 3 opinion expression from Chapter 3 — but used here
      for a FACT, not an opinion → Different function in the introduction

FORMULA A4: The "Si + condition" Conditional Opener

TEMPLATE: "Si [widely accepted fact], [consequence or tension that creates the debate]."

EXAMPLES:
├── "Si les avancées technologiques ont indéniablement facilité notre quotidien, elles soulèvent également des interrogations légitimes quant à leur impact sur les relations humaines."
├── "Si le voyage a toujours été considéré comme une source d'enrichissement personnel, son rôle éducatif fait l'objet de débats."
└── "Si la mondialisation a ouvert de nouvelles perspectives économiques, elle a également engendré des inégalités préoccupantes."

REGISTER: Formal B2–C1
ADVANTAGE: Creates TENSION within the first sentence → Highly sophisticated
BEST FOR: Prompts with a clear "for and against" dimension
NOTE: This formula often COMBINES Slot A and Slot B in one sentence
      → Allows a 2-sentence introduction instead of 3
GRAMMAR: "Si" here means "While/Although" (concessive), NOT "If" (conditional)
         → Followed by INDICATIF (passé composé or present), NOT conditional mood

FORMULA A5: The "Dans une société" Societal Context Opener

TEMPLATE: "Dans une société [characterized by X], [topic] [verb of relevance/debate]."

EXAMPLES:
├── "Dans une société de plus en plus connectée, la question de la protection des données personnelles revêt une importance capitale."
├── "Dans une société où la performance est valorisée, l'équilibre entre vie professionnelle et vie personnelle devient un enjeu crucial."
└── "Dans une société marquée par les défis environnementaux, les choix alimentaires de chaque individu prennent une dimension nouvelle."

REGISTER: Formal B2
ADVANTAGE: Frames the topic within a BROADER SOCIETAL CONTEXT → Shows intellectual breadth
BEST FOR: Prompts about social issues, lifestyle choices, policy questions

FORMULA A6: The "Force est de constater que" Observation Opener

TEMPLATE: "Force est de constater que [observable trend or fact]."

EXAMPLES:
├── "Force est de constater que les habitudes alimentaires des populations occidentales ont considérablement évolué ces dernières décennies."
├── "Force est de constater que le débat sur l'éducation numérique occupe une place grandissante dans le discours public."
└── "Force est de constater que les modes de transport traditionnels sont de plus en plus remis en question."

REGISTER: Very Formal B2–C1
ADVANTAGE: Signals high-level written French immediately → Strong first impression
BEST FOR: Any topic — universally applicable
NOTE: Also a Tier 3 opinion expression from Chapter 3 — used here for
      factual observation, not personal opinion

CHOOSING YOUR FORMULA A — DECISION GUIDE

DECISION TREE FOR SLOT A:
│
├── Is the prompt about a TREND or CHANGE?
│   ├── YES → Formula A2 ("Depuis quelques années") or A4 ("Si...")
│   └── NO ↓
│
├── Is the prompt about a SOCIAL ISSUE?
│   ├── YES → Formula A5 ("Dans une société") or A3 ("Il est indéniable")
│   └── NO ↓
│
├── Is the prompt about a POLICY or ACTION?
│   ├── YES → Formula A6 ("Force est de constater") or A3 ("Il est indéniable")
│   └── NO ↓
│
├── Are you UNSURE which formula fits?
│   └── DEFAULT → Formula A1 ("De nos jours") — safe, universal, always works
│
└── VARIETY RULE: Do NOT use the same formula in every practice response
    Rotate across A1–A6 to build flexibility

🔹 SLOT B — LA PROBLÉMATIQUE: 4 Formulas for Framing the Debate

FORMULA B1: The Indirect Question

TEMPLATE: "Il convient donc de se demander si/dans quelle mesure [rephrased prompt question]."

EXAMPLES:
├── "Il convient donc de se demander si cette évolution est véritablement bénéfique pour la société."
├── "Il convient donc de se demander dans quelle mesure le voyage peut être considéré comme un outil éducatif."
└── "Dès lors, il est légitime de s'interroger sur les conséquences réelles de cette transformation."

REGISTER: Formal B2
ADVANTAGE: Explicitly frames the DEBATE → Evaluator sees clear problématique
KEY PHRASES:
├── "Il convient de se demander si..." (It is appropriate to ask whether...)
├── "Il est légitime de s'interroger sur..." (It is legitimate to question...)
├── "La question se pose de savoir si..." (The question arises whether...)
└── "Dès lors, on peut se demander..." (From there, one may wonder...)

FORMULA B2: The Tension Statement

TEMPLATE: "[Topic] suscite un débat / fait l'objet de controverses / divise les opinions."

EXAMPLES:
├── "Cette tendance suscite toutefois un débat entre ses partisans et ses détracteurs."
├── "Ce phénomène fait l'objet de controverses quant à ses effets réels sur la société."
└── "Cette question divise les opinions entre ceux qui y voient un progrès et ceux qui s'en inquiètent."

REGISTER: Formal B2
ADVANTAGE: Creates explicit TENSION → The reader knows a debate exists
KEY VOCABULARY:
├── "susciter un débat" (to spark a debate)
├── "faire l'objet de controverses" (to be the subject of controversies)
├── "diviser les opinions" (to divide opinions)
├── "alimenter les discussions" (to fuel discussions)
└── "partisans et détracteurs" (supporters and opponents)

FORMULA B3: The "Certains... tandis que d'autres..." Opposing Views

TEMPLATE: "Certains [view A], tandis que/alors que d'autres [view B]."

EXAMPLES:
├── "Certains y voient une avancée majeure, tandis que d'autres s'inquiètent de ses conséquences à long terme."
├── "Certains considèrent cette mesure comme indispensable, alors que d'autres la jugent excessive."
└── "Certains estiment que le voyage est la meilleure école de la vie, tandis que d'autres privilégient l'éducation formelle."

REGISTER: Standard-Formal B1–B2
ADVANTAGE: Explicitly presents BOTH SIDES of the debate → Sets up bilateral argumentation (Chapter 8)
NOTE: This formula echoes the bilateral thinking from Chapter 8
      → It signals to the evaluator that you are AWARE of both perspectives

FORMULA B4: The Combined A+B Formula (Merging Context and Debate)

TEMPLATE: "Si [context/fact from Slot A], [debate/tension from Slot B]."

This is Formula A4 applied as a COMBINED A+B sentence
When used, you save one sentence → Introduction becomes 2 sentences instead of 3

EXAMPLES:
├── "Si le télétravail s'est imposé comme une pratique courante depuis 2020,
│    ses effets à long terme sur la productivité et le bien-être restent vivement débattus."
│    → Context (A) = remote work is common since 2020
│    → Debate (B) = effects on productivity and well-being are debated
│
├── "Si les voyages ont toujours été associés à l'enrichissement personnel,
│    leur capacité à constituer un véritable outil éducatif mérite d'être examinée."
│    → Context (A) = travel associated with personal enrichment
│    → Debate (B) = educational value needs examination
│
└── "Si l'interdiction des sacs en plastique vise à protéger l'environnement,
     les implications économiques et pratiques de cette mesure soulèvent des interrogations."
     → Context (A) = ban aims to protect environment
     → Debate (B) = economic and practical implications raise questions

REGISTER: Formal B2–C1
ADVANTAGE: Elegant, concise, sophisticated → Combines two functions in one sentence
BEST FOR: When you want a SHORT introduction (2 sentences) to maximize body space

🔹 SLOT C — LA THÈSE: 4 Formulas for Announcing Your Position

FORMULA C1: The Direct Thesis (Standard)

TEMPLATE: "[Tier 2–3 opinion marker from Chapter 3] + [clear position] + [optional scope qualifier]."

EXAMPLES:
├── "Je suis fermement convaincue que cette évolution représente un progrès significatif pour la société."
├── "Il me semble que les bénéfices de cette mesure l'emportent largement sur ses inconvénients."
└── "J'estime que le voyage constitue effectivement l'un des moyens les plus enrichissants de s'éduquer."

REGISTER: Formal B2
THIS IS THE DEFAULT THESIS FORMULA — works for any prompt type
REMINDER: Use Tier 2–3 markers from Chapter 3 — NEVER Tier 1 in the introduction

FORMULA C2: The Thesis With Preview (Announces argument direction)

TEMPLATE: "[Opinion marker] + [position], tant sur le plan [angle 1] que [angle 2]."

EXAMPLES:
├── "Je suis convaincu que cette interdiction est justifiée, tant sur le plan écologique qu'économique."
├── "Il me semble que le télétravail présente des avantages considérables, tant pour les employés que pour les entreprises."
└── "J'estime que l'apprentissage précoce des langues est bénéfique, tant sur le plan cognitif que culturel."

REGISTER: Formal B2
ADVANTAGE: Tells the reader WHICH angles your body will cover
           → Creates anticipation → Evaluator knows your plan → Dimension 2 boost
STRUCTURE SIGNAL: "tant... que..." previews 2 body arguments
NOTE: Your body arguments MUST then actually cover the 2 angles previewed
      → If you preview "écologique et économique" but argue "écologique et social" → Incoherence

FORMULA C3: The Nuanced Thesis (Shows complexity from the start)

TEMPLATE: "[Opinion marker] + [position], bien que/même si [brief acknowledgment of complexity]."

EXAMPLES:
├── "Je suis fermement convaincue que cette mesure est nécessaire, même si elle implique certains ajustements à court terme."
├── "Il me semble que les voyages constituent un outil éducatif remarquable, bien que d'autres formes d'apprentissage conservent leur importance."
└── "J'estime que le télétravail améliore globalement la qualité de vie, même s'il soulève certaines questions d'organisation."

REGISTER: Formal B2–C1
ADVANTAGE: Shows nuance IMMEDIATELY → Evaluator sees bilateral thinking from sentence 1
           → Strongest possible first impression for Dimension 2
GRAMMAR NOTE: "bien que" + subjunctive (Chapter 8 warning applies)
              → If unsure → Use "même si" + indicative (safer)
CONNECTS TO: Chapter 8's bilateral thinking — the thesis itself contains the concession seed

FORMULA C4: The Assertive Thesis (Strong conviction, no hedging)

TEMPLATE: "[Strong Tier 3 marker] + [unambiguous position]."

EXAMPLES:
├── "Force est de constater que cette interdiction constitue une avancée indispensable."
├── "Il est indéniable que les voyages représentent une expérience éducative sans équivalent."
└── "Tout porte à croire que le télétravail s'imposera comme le modèle dominant dans les années à venir."

REGISTER: Very Formal B2–C1
ADVANTAGE: Creates AUTHORITY → No hesitation, no hedging → Evaluator sees confidence
BEST FOR: When you have very strong arguments and want to project total conviction
RISK: If your body arguments are weak, the assertive thesis creates expectations you cannot meet
      → Only use when your arguments and examples are genuinely strong

🔵 WHAT COULD BE DONE

Option A: Memorize one formula per slot and use it for every response

  • → Creates a rigid habit → Responses start sounding identical
  • → Evaluator (if same person scores multiple responses) may notice the pattern
  • → Better: Master 2–3 formulas per slot and rotate

Option B: Always use the most sophisticated formula available

  • → Formula A4/A6 + B4 + C3 every time
  • → Risk: If grammar execution fails on sophisticated structures → Worse than simpler formulas done correctly
  • → Match formula complexity to your grammar confidence level

Option C: Adapt formula selection to the specific prompt type

  • → This is the OPTIMAL strategy
  • → Different prompts call for different framings
  • → Flexibility = the mark of a skilled writer

Option D: Write the introduction last (after the body)

  • → Viable strategy for advanced writers (Chapter 5, Option A mentioned this)
  • → You know exactly what your body argues → Introduction can perfectly preview it
  • → Risk: Time pressure may leave you with no introduction at all
  • → Recommended only if you are very confident with time management (Chapter 26)

2. Practice Introduction Assembly as an Isolated Skill

INTRODUCTION-ONLY DRILL:
│
├── Take 10 prompts from your practice bank
├── For EACH prompt, write ONLY the introduction (3 sentences)
├── Time limit: 3 minutes per introduction
├── After each, check:
│   ├── □ Is Slot A present? (Context established?)
│   ├── □ Is Slot B present? (Debate framed?)
│   ├── □ Is Slot C present? (Position stated with Tier 2–3 marker?)
│   ├── □ Is the total 30–50 words?
│   ├── □ Did I REPHRASE the prompt (not copy it)?
│   ├── □ Did I avoid all 5 diseases?
│   └── □ Would this create a strong first impression?
│
├── VARIATION: Write 2 different introductions for the SAME prompt
│   ├── Introduction A: Using Formula A2 + B1 + C1
│   └── Introduction B: Using Formula A4 (combined A+B) + C3
│   → This trains FLEXIBILITY — seeing multiple ways to frame the same debate
│
└── GOAL: 10 prompts × 2 introductions each = 20 introductions
    After this drill, introduction writing becomes automatic

3. Master the Art of REPHRASING the Prompt

REPHRASING TECHNIQUES:
│
├── TECHNIQUE 1: SYNONYM SUBSTITUTION
│   ├── Prompt word → Your synonym
│   ├── "les personnes" → "les individus" / "les citoyens" / "la population"
│   ├── "choisissent" → "optent pour" / "privilégient" / "se tournent vers"
│   ├── "de plus en plus" → "un nombre croissant de" / "une proportion grandissante de"
│   └── "bénéfique" → "avantageux" / "positif" / "profitable" / "salutaire"
│
├── TECHNIQUE 2: STRUCTURAL TRANSFORMATION
│   ├── Active → Passive: "Les gens choisissent..." → "Le végétarisme est choisi par..."
│   ├── Verb → Noun: "voyager" → "le voyage" / "les voyages"
│   ├── Noun → Verb: "l'interdiction" → "interdire"
│   └── Simple → Complex: "C'est bien" → "constitue une avancée significative"
│
├── TECHNIQUE 3: ABSTRACTION ELEVATION
│   ├── Prompt says something specific → You say the same thing at a higher level of abstraction
│   ├── Prompt: "manger végétarien" → You: "les choix alimentaires"
│   ├── Prompt: "les sacs en plastique" → You: "la consommation de produits à usage unique"
│   └── Prompt: "les téléphones portables" → You: "les outils numériques" / "les technologies mobiles"
│
└── TECHNIQUE 4: PERSPECTIVE SHIFT
    ├── Prompt frames from individual perspective → You frame from societal perspective
    ├── Prompt: "Pensez-vous que les voyages soient bénéfiques?"
    │   → Individual: "Le voyage enrichit la personne qui voyage"
    │   → Societal: "Le voyage constitue un vecteur d'enrichissement culturel pour nos sociétés"
    └── Societal framing sounds more analytical → Higher register → Better first impression

4. Eliminate the 5 Introduction Diseases Permanently

DISEASE ELIMINATION CHECKLIST (apply after every introduction):
│
├── □ DISEASE 1 — ANNOUNCEMENT: Do I say "Je vais expliquer/parler/donner mon opinion"?
│   ├── YES → DELETE and replace with the actual thesis
│   └── NO → Clean ✅
│
├── □ DISEASE 2 — VAGUENESS: Do I use "important/intéressant/bien" without specificity?
│   ├── YES → Replace with SPECIFIC analytical language
│   └── NO → Clean ✅
│
├── □ DISEASE 3 — COPY-PASTE: Did I use the prompt's exact words?
│   ├── YES → Rephrase using the 4 techniques above
│   └── NO → Clean ✅

❌ WHAT SHOULD NOT DO

1. Do NOT Begin With "Bonjour" or Any Greeting

  • Section B is an argumentative essay, not a letter (Section A)
  • ❌ "Bonjour, aujourd'hui je vais parler de..."
  • → This is a letter/presentation opening, not an essay opening
  • → Dimension 1 error: Wrong text type conventions

2. Do NOT Begin With a Dictionary Definition

  • ❌ "Le végétarisme est un régime alimentaire qui exclut la viande et le poisson."
  • → This is a definition, not an argumentative opening
  • → It wastes precious introduction words on information the evaluator already knows
  • → If you MUST define a term, integrate it into the context sentence naturally
  • ✅ "Le régime végétarien, qui séduit un nombre croissant d'individus, soulève des questions sur ses bénéfices réels."
  • → Definition is EMBEDDED within the context, not isolated

3. Do NOT Ask a Direct Question in the Introduction

  • ❌ "Est-ce que le voyage est vraiment éducatif?"
  • → Direct questions are appropriate in spoken presentations, not in formal written essays
  • → Use INDIRECT questions instead (Formula B1)
  • ✅ "Il convient de se demander si le voyage constitue véritablement un outil éducatif."
  • → Same question, formal register, appropriate for written argumentation

4. Do NOT Write an Introduction Longer Than 50 Words

  • Chapter 5 established: Introduction ≈ 30–50 words (~15–20% of response)
  • Every word over 50 in the introduction is a word STOLEN from the body
  • Body is where your arguments and examples live → Body needs the word budget
  • If your introduction exceeds 50 words → You are over-framing → Cut context details

5. Do NOT Use Multiple Opinion Markers in the Introduction

  • The introduction has ONE thesis sentence (Slot C) → ONE opinion marker
  • ❌ "À mon avis, je suis convaincue que je pense que cette mesure est bénéfique."
  • → Three opinion markers in one sentence = redundancy (Chapter 3 rule reinforced)
  • ✅ "Je suis convaincue que cette mesure est bénéfique."
  • → ONE marker, clear and clean

6. Do NOT Preview Your Arguments in Detail

  • Formula C2 allows a BRIEF preview ("tant sur le plan X que Y")
  • But do NOT write: "Je vais d'abord parler de l'environnement, puis de l'économie, et ensuite je donnerai ma conclusion."
  • → This is a TABLE OF CONTENTS, not a thesis
  • → Announcements are Disease 1 — always eliminate them
  • → The preview in C2 is THEMATIC ("écologique et économique"), not PROCEDURAL ("first I will, then I will")

Prompt about POLICY (Type 6)→ A3 ("Il est indéniable") + B1 ("Il est légitime de s'interroger") + C3 (Nuanced thesis)

🚫 WHEN NOT TO USE

When NOT to Use Formula A4 ("Si...")

  • When you are not comfortable with concessive "si" constructions
  • When the prompt does not have a clear "two sides" dimension
  • When you want a 3-sentence introduction (A4 merges A+B into 1 sentence → Gives you only 2 sentences)

When NOT to Use Formula C3 (Nuanced Thesis)

  • When the prompt asks for a simple, clear position and nuance would weaken it
  • When you are not confident with "bien que" + subjunctive grammar
  • When your body arguments are purely one-sided → A nuanced thesis creates expectations of bilateral body that you may not deliver

When NOT to Invest Heavily in the Introduction

  • When time is extremely limited → Write a 2-sentence minimum introduction → Invest remaining time in body
  • A strong body with a basic introduction scores better than a perfect introduction with a weak body
  • The introduction is a MULTIPLIER (Chapter 5 principle) → But the body is the SUBSTANCE being multiplied
  • Zero substance × perfect multiplier = zero

WITHOUT FORMULAS (Fatima's approach):

    ├── Quality is GUARANTEED by the formula → You cannot produce Disease 1–5
    └── Speed increases dramatically → More time for body development

The 3-Slot Harmony Principle

THE 3 SLOTS MUST FLOW AS A LOGICAL PROGRESSION:
│
├── SLOT A sets the LANDSCAPE (broad context)
│   → "In today's world, X is happening"
│
├── SLOT B creates the TENSION within that landscape
│   → "But this raises a question / But opinions are divided"
│
├── SLOT C resolves the tension with YOUR POSITION
│   → "I believe that..."
│
├── THE FLOW: Broad → Narrow → Specific
│   LANDSCAPE → TENSION → POSITION
│   Context → Question → Answer
│
├── IF THIS FLOW IS VIOLATED:
│   ├── Position before context → Reader is disoriented
│   ├── Context without tension → Reader sees no reason for the essay
│   ├── Tension without resolution → Reader does not know your stance
│   └── Each violation = Dimension 2 (coherence) penalty
│
└── THE ANALOGY:
    Slot A = Zooming out to see the whole map
    Slot B = Zooming in to one contested territory
    Slot C = Planting YOUR flag on that territory

🕳️ COMMON PITFALLS AND FAILURE MODES

Pitfall 1: The Formula Collision

  • What happens: Candidate uses Formula A4 (which combines A+B) AND a separate Slot B formula
  • → Result: Introduction has 4 sentences → Too long → Over-framing
  • How to prevent: If using A4 → Skip separate Slot B → Move directly to Slot C

Pitfall 2: The Context-Thesis Mismatch

  • What happens: Slot A talks about technology, but Slot C states an opinion about education
  • → The context does not lead to the thesis → Incoherent introduction
  • How to prevent: Slot A must DIRECTLY relate to the topic of Slot C → They are part of the same logical chain

Pitfall 3: The Beautiful But Empty Introduction

  • What happens: Introduction uses sophisticated formulas and vocabulary but says nothing substantive
  • Example: "Dans une société en constante mutation, les évolutions récentes suscitent un débat considérable. Il convient de se demander si ces changements sont positifs. Je suis convaincue qu'ils le sont."
  • → WHAT society? WHAT evolutions? WHAT changes? → Completely vague despite formal register
  • How to prevent: Formulas provide STRUCTURE — you must fill them with SPECIFIC CONTENT
    • → Name the ACTUAL topic in Slot A
    • → Name the ACTUAL debate in Slot B
    • → State your ACTUAL position on the ACTUAL question in Slot C

Pitfall 4: The Overly Ambitious Introduction

  • What happens: Candidate tries to use A6 + B1 + C3 + additional sentences
  • → Introduction becomes 70+ words → Eats into body word budget
  • How to prevent: Hard limit: 50 words maximum for introduction → If you exceed → Cut Slot A to essentials

Pitfall 5: The Register Inconsistency

  • What happens: Slot A uses very formal language ("Force est de constater") but Slot C uses basic language ("Je pense que c'est bien")
  • → The drop in register is jarring → Evaluator notices inconsistency
  • How to prevent: Match register across ALL 3 slots
    • → If Slot A is formal → Slot C must also be formal
    • → Formula A6 pairs with Formula C1/C3/C4 (all formal)
    • → Formula A1 pairs with Formula C1 (both standard-formal)
