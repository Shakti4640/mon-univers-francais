📘 CHAPTER 4

BECAUSE IS NOT ENOUGH: GIVING YOUR FIRST REAL REASON IN FRENCH

🔴 HYPOTHETICAL PROBLEM

You are a TEF candidate named Daniel
He studied Chapter 1 → He understands the 4 scoring dimensions
He studied Chapter 2 → He can decode any prompt and extract the exact question
He studied Chapter 3 → He can form proper opinion sentences using Tier 2–3 markers
He sits down with this practice prompt:

"Certains estiment que l'apprentissage d'une langue étrangère devrait être obligatoire dès l'école primaire. Êtes-vous d'accord avec cette idée? Justifiez votre point de vue."

Daniel applies his Chapter 2 extraction protocol:

  • CONTEXT → Some people think foreign language learning should be mandatory from primary school
  • QUESTION → Do you agree? (Question Type 1: Direct Opinion)
  • FORMAT → Justify your viewpoint

Daniel applies his Chapter 3 opinion skills:

  • He writes: "Je suis fermement convaincu que l'apprentissage d'une langue étrangère devrait commencer dès le plus jeune âge."
  • Beautiful Tier 3 opening → Strong first impression

Then he continues:
"Parce que c'est important. Parce que les enfants apprennent vite. Parce que c'est bien pour le futur."

Three "parce que" sentences in a row
Each reason is vague, undeveloped, and structurally identical
The evaluator notes: "Arguments superficiels — justification insuffisante — manque de développement"
Daniel's Dimension 2 (coherence) and Dimension 3 (vocabulary) scores collapse

❓ THE CORE QUESTION

  • Why does repeating "parce que" fail even when the reasons themselves are valid?
  • What French structures exist BEYOND "parce que" for giving reasons?
  • How do you make a reason feel DEVELOPED rather than just STATED?
  • What is the difference between a reason and a JUSTIFICATION?

🟡 HOW TO APPROACH IT

Step 1: Understand the Difference Between a REASON and a JUSTIFICATION

REASON (Raison):
├── A single statement explaining WHY you hold your opinion
├── Example: "Children learn languages faster"
├── This is a CLAIM about reality
└── By itself, it is just another unsupported statement

JUSTIFICATION (Justification):
├── A reason + EXPLANATION of how/why that reason supports your opinion
├── Example: "Children learn languages faster because their brains
│   are more receptive to new sounds and grammatical patterns
│   during the critical development period before age 10"
├── This CONNECTS the reason to the opinion through logic
└── This is what the evaluator actually wants to see
  • Daniel's error: He gave three REASONS but zero JUSTIFICATIONS
  • Each "parce que" was a claim, not an explained argument
  • The evaluator sees: opinion → claim → claim → claim
  • The evaluator wants: opinion → justified argument → justified argument

Step 2: Understand the Anatomy of a Complete Justification Unit

  • Every justification in TEF Section B should follow this internal structure:
JUSTIFICATION UNIT:
│
├── COMPONENT 1: CONNECTOR
│   ├── The linking word that signals "here comes a reason"
│   ├── NOT always "parce que"
│   └── Examples: En effet / Car / Puisque / En raison de
│
├── COMPONENT 2: REASON STATEMENT
│   ├── The core claim that supports your opinion
│   ├── Must be SPECIFIC, not vague
│   └── "c'est important" = vague ❌
│       "cela développe les capacités cognitives" = specific ✅
│
├── COMPONENT 3: ELABORATION
│   ├── 1–2 sentences that EXPLAIN the reason further
│   ├── HOW does this work? WHY is this true? WHAT does this mean concretely?
│   └── This is where most candidates stop too early — Daniel stopped here
│
└── COMPONENT 4: CONNECTION BACK TO OPINION (optional but powerful)
    ├── A brief phrase linking this reason back to your original position
    ├── Shows the evaluator that your argument is COHERENT
    └── Example: "C'est pourquoi l'enseignement précoce des langues me paraît essentiel."

Step 3: Understand WHY "Parce que" Alone Fails

  • "Parce que" is grammatically correct
  • But using it repeatedly creates 3 problems simultaneously:
PROBLEM 1: MONOTONY (Impacts Dimension 3 — Vocabulary)
├── Same connector repeated = limited lexical range demonstrated
└── Evaluator sees: "This candidate knows only one causal connector"

PROBLEM 2: SHALLOW STRUCTURE (Impacts Dimension 2 — Coherence)
├── "Parce que" + short reason = list format, not argumentative development
├── Lists are for shopping, not for argumentation
└── Evaluator sees: "No logical depth or development"

PROBLEM 3: REGISTER ISSUE (Impacts Dimension 3 — Vocabulary)
├── "Parce que" is the MOST BASIC causal connector in French
├── It is perfectly natural in spoken French
├── In formal written French, alternatives are expected at B2 level
└── Evaluator sees: "Spoken French register in a written task"

🟢 HOW CAN WE SOLVE IT

The Solution = Mastering 3 Categories of French Causal Expressions

  • Just as Chapter 3 organized opinion markers into tiers, causal expressions can be organized into 3 functional categories
  • Each category serves a different logical PURPOSE
  • Knowing WHICH category to use WHERE is the key skill

🔹 CATEGORY 1: DIRECT CAUSE CONNECTORS (Connecteurs de cause directe)

These directly answer "WHY?" — they link your opinion to its reason

French ExpressionEnglish EquivalentRegister LevelGrammar Note
parce quebecauseBasic (A2)Followed by subject + verb
carbecause / forFormal (B1)Followed by subject + verb — NEVER starts a sentence
puisquesince / given thatFormal (B1–B2)Implies the reason is obvious or already known
étant donné quegiven thatFormal (B2)Followed by subject + verb
du fait quedue to the fact thatFormal (B2)Followed by subject + verb
dans la mesure oùinsofar as / to the extent thatVery Formal (B2–C1)Shows conditional causality

When to use each:

"parce que" → When you need a quick, simple reason (use sparingly in Section B)
"car" → Direct replacement for "parce que" in formal writing (NEVER at sentence start)
"puisque" → When the reason is something the reader should already accept as true
"étant donné que" → When presenting factual/contextual reasons
"du fait que" → When emphasizing the factual nature of the cause
"dans la mesure où" → When the reason applies only partially or conditionally

Example — Same reason, different connectors:

  • ❌ L'apprentissage précoce est bénéfique parce que les enfants apprennent vite.
  • ✅ L'apprentissage précoce est bénéfique, car les enfants disposent d'une plasticité cérébrale supérieure.
  • ✅ Puisque les recherches en neurosciences confirment la réceptivité accrue des jeunes enfants, l'apprentissage précoce s'avère particulièrement efficace.
  • ✅ Étant donné que le cerveau des enfants est plus réceptif aux nouvelles structures linguistiques, il paraît logique de commencer cet apprentissage tôt.

🔹 CATEGORY 2: CAUSE-INTRODUCING PREPOSITIONS AND NOUN PHRASES (Prépositions de cause)

These introduce a cause using a NOUN or NOUN PHRASE instead of a full clause
They create more concise, sophisticated sentence structures

French ExpressionEnglish EquivalentRegister LevelGrammar Note
à cause de + nombecause of (negative connotation)Standard (B1)Followed by a NOUN, not a verb clause
grâce à + nomthanks to (positive connotation)Standard (B1)Followed by a NOUN, not a verb clause
en raison de + nomdue to / by reason ofFormal (B2)Neutral connotation — works for both positive and negative
faute de + nomfor lack of / due to absence ofFormal (B2)Implies the cause is a DEFICIENCY
à force de + infinitifby dint of / through repeatedFormal (B2)Followed by an INFINITIVE verb
suite à + nomfollowing / as a result ofFormal (B2)Implies chronological cause-effect

Critical distinction — "à cause de" vs. "grâce à":

"à cause de" = The cause leads to something NEGATIVE
├── "À cause de la pollution, les habitants souffrent de problèmes respiratoires."
└── Using "à cause de" for positive outcomes = ERROR in register

"grâce à" = The cause leads to something POSITIVE
├── "Grâce à l'apprentissage précoce, les enfants développent une meilleure oreille musicale."
└── Using "grâce à" for negative outcomes = ERROR in register

"en raison de" = NEUTRAL — works for both positive and negative
├── "En raison de la plasticité cérébrale des enfants, l'apprentissage est plus efficace."
└── Safe choice when you are unsure about positive/negative framing

Why Category 2 matters for your score:

  • Category 1 connectors all require full clauses (subject + verb)
  • Category 2 expressions allow NOUN-BASED constructions
  • This creates sentence variety → Dimension 4 (Grammar) improvement
  • Mixing Category 1 and Category 2 in the same response → Shows grammatical range

🔹 CATEGORY 3: EXPLANATION AND ELABORATION EXPRESSIONS (Expressions d'explication)

These do NOT introduce a new cause — they DEVELOP and DEEPEN a reason already stated
This is the category Daniel was completely missing

French ExpressionEnglish EquivalentFunction
En effet, ...Indeed, ... / In fact, ...Confirms and reinforces the reason just given
C'est-à-dire que...That is to say...Clarifies or rephrases the reason more precisely
Autrement dit, ...In other words, ...Rephrases for clarity
Cela signifie que...This means that...Draws out the implication of the reason
Cela s'explique par le fait que...This is explained by the fact that...Provides deeper causal explanation
Il faut rappeler que...It should be remembered that...Adds background information that supports the reason
En d'autres termes, ...In other terms, ...Reformulation for emphasis

How "En effet" transforms a shallow reason into a deep justification:

Daniel's version:

"L'apprentissage précoce est bénéfique parce que les enfants apprennent vite."
→ One sentence. One shallow claim. No development. Score: low.

Improved version using Category 3:

"L'apprentissage précoce est bénéfique, car les enfants disposent d'une capacité d'absorption linguistique remarquable. En effet, les recherches en neurosciences démontrent que le cerveau humain est particulièrement réceptif aux nouvelles structures phonétiques et grammaticales avant l'âge de dix ans. Cela signifie que les enfants exposés à une langue étrangère dès l'école primaire développent une prononciation plus naturelle et une compréhension intuitive de la grammaire."
→ Three sentences. One reason fully DEVELOPED. Score: high.

The internal logic:

SENTENCE 1: State the reason (using Category 1 connector: "car")
SENTENCE 2: Develop the reason (using Category 3: "En effet")
SENTENCE 3: Draw out the implication (using Category 3: "Cela signifie que")

This 3-sentence pattern = ONE complete justification unit
This is what the evaluator recognizes as DEVELOPED argumentation

🔵 WHAT COULD BE DONE

Option A: Replace every "parce que" with "car" and change nothing else

  • → Slight improvement in register (Dimension 3)
  • → But the DEVELOPMENT problem remains
  • → "Car" + shallow reason is still a shallow reason
  • → Necessary but insufficient

Option B: Use only Category 2 noun-based expressions

  • → Shows grammatical sophistication
  • → But limits your sentence variety (every sentence becomes noun-based)
  • → Best used as 1–2 sentences within a longer justification, not as the only structure

Option C: Master the 3-sentence justification pattern (Category 1 → Category 3 → Category 3)

  • → This is the correct core strategy
  • → It ensures every reason is DEVELOPED, not just STATED
  • → It creates the logical depth evaluators look for
  • → Combined with Category 2 for occasional variety → Maximum scoring potential

Option D: Give 5 reasons with no development instead of 2 reasons with full development

  • → This is a common strategic error
  • → Quantity of reasons ≠ quality of argumentation
  • → 5 shallow reasons score LOWER than 2 fully developed reasons
  • → The evaluator values DEPTH over BREADTH in Section B

✅ WHAT SHOULD DO

1. Build a Personal Causal Expression Arsenal (Matching Chapter 3's Opinion Arsenal)

YOUR RECOMMENDED CAUSAL ARSENAL:
│
├── FROM CATEGORY 1 (Direct Cause — pick 3–4):
│   ├── car (formal replacement for "parce que")
│   ├── puisque (for obvious/accepted reasons)
│   ├── étant donné que (for factual contexts)
│   └── dans la mesure où (for conditional/partial causes — advanced)
│
├── FROM CATEGORY 2 (Noun-Based Cause — pick 3):
│   ├── grâce à (positive causes)
│   ├── en raison de (neutral causes)
│   └── faute de (causes based on lack/deficiency)
│
└── FROM CATEGORY 3 (Elaboration — pick 3–4):
    ├── En effet (to confirm and develop)
    ├── Cela signifie que (to draw out implications)
    ├── Autrement dit (to rephrase for clarity)
    └── Cela s'explique par le fait que (to deepen explanation)

2. Practice the 3-Sentence Justification Pattern

PATTERN:
Sentence 1: [Opinion marker from Ch.3] + [Position] → CLAIM
Sentence 2: [Category 1 connector] + [Specific reason] → SUPPORT
Sentence 3: [Category 3 expression] + [Elaboration/Implication] → DEPTH

PRACTICE DRILL:
├── Take any topic
├── Write your opinion sentence (Chapter 3 skill)
├── Write ONE reason using a Category 1 connector
├── Write ONE elaboration using a Category 3 expression
├── Check: Does the 3-sentence block feel like a complete argument?
│   ├── YES → Success — this is one justification unit
│   └── NO → The elaboration sentence is probably too vague — make it more specific
└── Repeat with 10 different topics

3. Aim for Exactly 2 Fully Developed Justification Units in Section B

RESPONSE STRUCTURE TARGET:
├── Introduction (2–3 sentences) → Contains opinion sentence from Ch.3
├── Justification Unit 1 (3–4 sentences) → Reason 1 fully developed
├── Justification Unit 2 (3–4 sentences) → Reason 2 fully developed
└── Conclusion (2–3 sentences) → Synthesis

Total: ~12–14 sentences → ~200–250 words → Perfect for Section B
  • 2 developed reasons is the OPTIMAL number for Section B word count
  • 1 reason = too thin → Evaluator sees insufficient development
  • 3 reasons = each becomes shallow due to word count constraint
  • 2 is the magic number

4. Vary Your Connectors WITHIN a Single Response

EXAMPLE OF GOOD VARIETY:

Justification Unit 1:
"...car les enfants disposent d'une plasticité cérébrale remarquable.
En effet, les recherches démontrent que..."

Justification Unit 2:
"...étant donné que la maîtrise d'une langue étrangère constitue un atout professionnel.
Cela signifie que les élèves qui commencent tôt..."

NOTICE: Different Category 1 connector in each unit (car vs. étant donné que)
NOTICE: Different Category 3 expression in each unit (En effet vs. Cela signifie que)
This variety = Dimension 3 (vocabulary range) maximized

5. Make Your Reasons SPECIFIC, Not Generic

GENERIC REASONS (score poorly):
├── "C'est important pour le futur"
├── "C'est bien pour les enfants"
├── "Cela aide la société"
└── These could apply to ANY topic → they show no real thinking

SPECIFIC REASONS (score well):
├── "Cela développe les capacités cognitives et la flexibilité mentale"
├── "Les enfants acquièrent une prononciation plus authentique"
├── "La maîtrise d'une langue étrangère ouvre des perspectives professionnelles internationales"
└── These are SPECIFIC to the topic → they show genuine argumentation

The Specificity Test:

  • After writing a reason, ask: "Could this reason apply to 10 other topics?"
  • If YES → It is too generic → Make it more specific to THIS topic
  • If NO → It is appropriately specific → Keep it

❌ WHAT SHOULD NOT DO

1. Do NOT Use "Parce que" More Than Once in Your Entire Response

  • Once is acceptable (especially in a less prominent position)
  • Twice is noticeable repetition
  • Three times → Evaluator concludes limited connector knowledge
  • Replace with "car," "puisque," "étant donné que" → Instant improvement

2. Do NOT Start a Sentence With "Car"

  • "Car" is a coordinating conjunction in French
  • It CANNOT begin a sentence — it must follow a comma or semicolon within a sentence
  • ❌ "Car les enfants apprennent vite, cette méthode est efficace."
  • ✅ "Cette méthode est efficace, car les enfants apprennent vite."
  • This is a STRICT grammar rule — violating it = Dimension 4 error

3. Do NOT Confuse "À cause de" and "Grâce à"

  • As explained in Category 2:
    • "À cause de" = NEGATIVE cause → leads to bad outcome
    • "Grâce à" = POSITIVE cause → leads to good outcome
  • ❌ "Grâce à la pollution, les gens tombent malades." (Pollution is not something to thank)
  • ❌ "À cause de l'éducation, les enfants réussissent." (Education leading to success is positive)
  • ✅ "Grâce à l'éducation précoce, les enfants développent de meilleures compétences."
  • ✅ "À cause du manque de financement, les programmes linguistiques restent limités."
  • Confusing these two → Shows fundamental vocabulary misunderstanding → Dimension 3 penalty

4. Do NOT Give a Reason Without ANY Elaboration

  • Stating a reason without development = making a claim without proof
  • The evaluator reads: "Les enfants apprennent vite" and thinks: "Why? How? Prove it."
  • Always follow a reason with at least ONE sentence of elaboration (Category 3)
  • Minimum viable justification = 2 sentences (reason + elaboration)
  • Optimal justification = 3 sentences (reason + elaboration + implication)

5. Do NOT List Reasons With Bullet-Point Logic

  • Section B is a continuous text, NOT a list
  • ❌ Writing pattern that looks like a list:
    • "Premièrement, c'est bon pour le cerveau. Deuxièmement, c'est utile pour le travail. Troisièmement, c'est intéressant."
  • This is three undeveloped reasons listed sequentially → It reads like bullet points in paragraph form
  • ✅ Better: Two reasons, each as a developed paragraph with elaboration and examples
  • Numbering reasons (premièrement, deuxièmement) is acceptable for structure but ONLY if each numbered point is then DEVELOPED, not just stated

6. Do NOT Use "Parce que" at the Start of a Sentence in Formal Writing

  • In spoken French: "Parce que c'est important." → Acceptable
  • In formal written French: Starting with "parce que" sounds incomplete
  • ❌ "Parce que les enfants apprennent vite, l'apprentissage précoce est recommandé."
  • ✅ "L'apprentissage précoce est recommandé puisque les enfants apprennent vite."
  • ✅ "Étant donné que les enfants apprennent vite, l'apprentissage précoce est recommandé."
  • "Puisque" and "Étant donné que" CAN start sentences → "Parce que" should NOT in formal register

⏱️ WHEN TO USE WHAT

SituationWhich Category / Expression to Use
Introducing your FIRST reason after your opinion sentence→ Category 1: "car" or "puisque" (smooth transition from opinion to reason)
Developing a reason after stating it→ Category 3: "En effet" (the most versatile elaboration starter)
Introducing your SECOND reason→ Category 1: Use a DIFFERENT connector than the first (e.g., if first was "car," second is "étant donné que")
Mentioning a cause briefly without developing it→ Category 2: "grâce à" / "en raison de" (concise, noun-based)
Explaining the IMPLICATION of a reason→ Category 3: "Cela signifie que..." / "Autrement dit..."
Referring to a POSITIVE cause→ Category 2: "grâce à" (NEVER "à cause de")
Referring to a NEGATIVE cause→ Category 2: "à cause de" or neutral "en raison de"
Stating a reason the reader should already agree with→ Category 1: "puisque" (implies shared knowledge)
Stating a conditional/partial reason→ Category 1: "dans la mesure où" (advanced — shows nuance)
Running low on time and need a quick reason→ Category 1: "car" + one sentence → minimum viable justification

🚫 WHEN NOT TO USE

When NOT to Use Causal Connectors

  • When presenting the OPPOSING viewpoint (you will use concession connectors instead — Chapter 16)
  • When writing your introduction's context sentences (context is scene-setting, not argumentation)
  • When writing your conclusion (the conclusion SYNTHESIZES reasons already given, it does not introduce NEW reasons)
  • When giving an EXAMPLE (examples use different connectors like "par exemple" — Chapter 9)

When NOT to Use "En effet"

  • "En effet" means "indeed" — it CONFIRMS what was just said
  • Do NOT use it to introduce a CONTRASTING or NEW idea
  • ❌ "Les enfants apprennent vite. En effet, les adultes ont aussi des avantages." → The second sentence does not confirm the first — it shifts topic
  • ✅ "Les enfants apprennent vite. En effet, leur cerveau est plus réceptif." → The second sentence CONFIRMS and EXPLAINS the first

When NOT to Use "Dans la mesure où"

  • Only use when the cause is genuinely CONDITIONAL or PARTIAL
  • ❌ "Les langues sont utiles dans la mesure où elles sont utiles." → Circular
  • ✅ "L'apprentissage précoce est efficace dans la mesure où il est accompagné de méthodes pédagogiques adaptées." → Genuine condition

⚙️ HOW IT ONLY WORKS (INTERNAL MECHANICS)

The Evaluator's Processing of Your Justification

EVALUATOR READS YOUR JUSTIFICATION UNIT:

STEP 1: Is there a REASON given for the opinion?
├── YES → Dimension 1 satisfied (consigne asks for justification)
└── NO → Dimension 1 failure — opinion without support

STEP 2: Is the reason DEVELOPED beyond a single claim?
├── YES → Dimension 2 positive (coherent, developed argumentation)
├── NO → Dimension 2 concern (superficial treatment)
└── This is the step where Daniel failed — he had reasons but no development

STEP 3: What causal connectors are used?
├── Only "parce que" → Dimension 3 assessment: basic vocabulary
├── "Car" + "puisque" + "en raison de" → Dimension 3: varied vocabulary
├── Category 2 + Category 3 mixed → Dimension 3: rich vocabulary
└── The VARIETY of connectors directly impacts vocabulary scoring

STEP 4: Are the causal structures grammatically correct?
├── "Car" not starting a sentence → Correct → Dimension 4 positive
├── "Car" starting a sentence → Error → Dimension 4 negative
├── "Grâce à" + noun (correct) vs. "Grâce à" + clause (error)
└── Each connector has grammar rules — correctness is verified

Why the 3-Sentence Pattern Works Cognitively

COGNITIVE ARCHITECTURE OF PERSUASION:

SENTENCE 1 (Reason): Activates the reader's QUESTION reflex
├── Reader sees: "...car les enfants disposent d'une plasticité cérébrale"
└── Reader's brain asks: "Really? How? Prove it."

SENTENCE 2 (Elaboration): SATISFIES the question reflex
├── Reader sees: "En effet, les recherches démontrent que..."
└── Reader's brain: "Okay, this is supported by evidence."

SENTENCE 3 (Implication): Creates CLOSURE on this argument
├── Reader sees: "Cela signifie que les élèves qui commencent tôt..."
└── Reader's brain: "I understand the full chain of reasoning."

WITHOUT Sentence 2 and 3:
├── The question reflex is TRIGGERED but NEVER SATISFIED
├── The reader (evaluator) feels the argument is INCOMPLETE
└── This incomplete feeling → Lower score on Dimension 2

How Category 1, 2, and 3 Work Together Mechanically

CATEGORY 1 (Direct Cause): OPENS the justification
├── Creates the causal link between opinion and reason
├── Grammar: Always connects two CLAUSES (subject + verb → subject + verb)
└── Position: Usually in the FIRST sentence of the justification unit

CATEGORY 2 (Noun-Based Cause): COMPRESSES a cause into fewer words
├── Allows you to mention a cause WITHOUT building a full clause
├── Grammar: Connects a clause to a NOUN PHRASE
├── Position: Useful within elaboration sentences or as transitions
└── Example: "Grâce à cette plasticité, les résultats sont remarquables."

CATEGORY 3 (Elaboration): DEEPENS the justification
├── Does NOT introduce a new cause — develops the existing one
├── Grammar: Usually starts a new sentence
├── Position: Always AFTER a reason has been stated (Sentence 2 or 3)
└── Without Category 3, your argument stays at surface level

The Hidden Rule: Causality Must Flow in ONE Direction

CORRECT CAUSAL FLOW:
Opinion → because → Reason → indeed → Elaboration → this means → Implication
(Each step moves FORWARD — deeper into the argument)

INCORRECT CAUSAL FLOW:
Opinion → because → Reason → because → Different reason → because → Another reason
(Each step moves SIDEWAYS — no depth, just breadth)

Daniel's error was a SIDEWAYS flow
The evaluator wants a FORWARD flow
Forward flow = development = high Dimension 2 score
Sideways flow = listing = low Dimension 2 score

🕳️ COMMON PITFALLS AND FAILURE MODES

Pitfall 1: The "Parce que" Addiction

  • What happens: Candidate uses "parce que" as the ONLY causal connector throughout the response
  • Why it happens: "Parce que" is learned first and feels safest
  • How to prevent: During practice, BAN "parce que" entirely for 2 weeks → Force yourself to use alternatives → Then reintroduce it as ONE option among many

Pitfall 2: The Circular Reason

  • What happens: The reason just restates the opinion in different words
  • Example: Opinion: "L'apprentissage précoce est bénéfique." → Reason: "Car il est bon pour les enfants."
  • → "Bénéfique" and "bon" mean the same thing → This is circular, not justification
  • How to prevent: After writing your reason, ask: "Does this add NEW INFORMATION that was not in my opinion sentence?" If no → Rewrite

Pitfall 3: The Orphan Reason

  • What happens: A reason is stated but never connected to the opinion
  • Example: Opinion: "L'apprentissage précoce est essentiel." → Reason: "Les professeurs de langues sont souvent mal payés."
  • → Teacher salaries may be related to language education broadly, but the CONNECTION to "apprentissage précoce est essentiel" is not made
  • How to prevent: After writing your reason, ask: "Can the reader see HOW this reason supports my specific opinion?" If not → Either add a connecting sentence or choose a different reason

Pitfall 4: The "En effet" Misuse

  • What happens: "En effet" is used to introduce contrasting or new information instead of confirming previous information
  • Why it happens: Candidates learn "en effet" means "in fact" and use it like the English "in fact" (which CAN introduce contrast)
  • The rule: French "en effet" ONLY confirms → English "in fact" can confirm OR contrast → They are NOT identical
  • How to prevent: Before writing "en effet," check: "Does my next sentence SUPPORT what I just said?" If yes → Use it. If no → Use a different connector

Pitfall 5: The Reason-Without-Topic-Connection

  • What happens: Candidate gives a reason that is TRUE but not relevant to the SPECIFIC topic
  • Example for language learning prompt: "Car l'éducation est importante dans notre société."
  • → This is true but applies to ANY education topic, not specifically to early language learning
  • → Refer to the SPECIFICITY TEST from the "What Should Do" section above
  • How to prevent: Apply the specificity test every time → "Could this reason work for 10 other topics?" → If yes → Too generic

Pitfall 6: The Over-Elaboration Trap

  • What happens: Candidate develops ONE reason so extensively that there is no room for a second reason
  • → 150 words on one reason + no second reason = imbalanced response
  • How to prevent: Each justification unit should be 3–4 sentences maximum (approximately 50–70 words) → This leaves room for 2 units within the word limit

🔑 KEY TAKEAWAY

What Chapter 4 Established

A REASON is not the same as a JUSTIFICATION:
├── Reason = a claim explaining WHY (1 sentence, shallow)
└── Justification = a reason + elaboration + implication (3 sentences, developed)

3 Categories of French causal expressions:
├── Category 1: Direct cause connectors (car / puisque / étant donné que)
│   → Open the justification — link opinion to reason
├── Category 2: Noun-based cause expressions (grâce à / en raison de / à cause de)
│   → Compress causes into concise structures
└── Category 3: Elaboration expressions (En effet / Cela signifie que / Autrement dit)
    → Develop and deepen the reason — THIS is where most candidates fail

The 3-Sentence Justification Pattern:
├── Sentence 1: [Category 1 connector] + [Specific reason]
├── Sentence 2: [Category 3 expression] + [Elaboration]
└── Sentence 3: [Category 3 expression] + [Implication]

Optimal strategy: 2 fully developed justification units per response
├── 2 developed reasons > 5 shallow reasons
├── Depth > Breadth in TEF Section B
└── Each unit ≈ 50–70 words → fits perfectly within word count
