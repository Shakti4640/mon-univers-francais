You are a TEF candidate named Linh

She writes two practice responses on the same day to this prompt:

"Le sport devrait-il être obligatoire dans les écoles? Justifiez votre point de vue."

Linh's Response A (Too Informal):

Linh's Response B (Too Formal/Robotic):

"Il est impératif de souligner que la pratique sportive obligatoire au sein des établissements scolaires constitue une nécessité fondamentale et incontournable. Response A → Her evaluator writes: "Registre trop familier. Langue orale transposée à l'écrit. Inapproprié pour un texte argumentatif."
Response B → Her evaluator writes: "Registre excessivement soutenu. Le texte sonne artificiel et mécanique. Chaque phrase commence par une construction impersonnelle. Absence de voix personnelle."

BOTH responses received SIMILAR scores on Dimension 3 — neither hit "Well achieved"
Response A failed for being too LOW in register
Response B failed for being too HIGH and MECHANICAL in register
Linh needs to find the SWEET SPOT between these two extremes

🟡 HOW TO APPROACH IT

Step 1: Understand the French Register Spectrum

LEVEL 1: FAMILIER (Informal/Colloquial)
  → Spoken French transcribed to paper
  → Contractions, slang, incomplete sentences
  → "Genre," "du coup," "y'a," "c'est trop bien"
  → NEVER acceptable in TEF Section B

LEVEL 2: COURANT (Standard/Neutral)
  → Everyday written French
  → Correct grammar, simple vocabulary, natural flow
  → "Je pense que," "Il y a," "C'est important"
  → Acceptable for B1 scoring — but insufficient for B2+

LEVEL 3: SOUTENU (Formal/Elevated)
  → Academic and professional written French
  → Sophisticated vocabulary, complex structures, measured tone
  → "Il me semble que," "Il convient de," "Force est de constater"
  → TARGET REGISTER FOR TEF SECTION B

LEVEL 4: LITTÉRAIRE / HYPER-SOUTENU (Literary/Excessively Formal)
  → Archaic, extremely ornate, artificially elevated
  → Every sentence impersonal, every word maximally sophisticated
  → "Il appert que," "nonobstant les considérations susmentionnées"
  → TOO HIGH for TEF Section B — sounds robotic/artificial

🔷 DIMENSION A: PERSONAL PRONOUNS

How you refer to yourself, the reader, and people in general

The Pronoun Spectrum:

PronounRegister LevelTEF AcceptabilityExample
Moi, jeFamilier❌ NEVERMoi, je pense que le sport est important.
JeCourant✅ ACCEPTABLE — in moderationJe considère que le sport est essentiel.
NousSoutenu✅ PREFERRED for collective reasoningNous pouvons constater que le sport joue un rôle crucial.
OnCourant to Soutenu (depends on usage)✅ ACCEPTABLE — with conditionsOn observe que la sédentarité augmente.

Le candidat / Le soussignéHyper-soutenu❌ TOO FORMAL — sounds bureaucraticLe soussigné estime que...

The "Je" Calibration Rules:

RULE 1: "Je" IS ACCEPTABLE in TEF Section B
  → The prompt asks "Pensez-VOUS" → It INVITES personal opinion
  → Using "je" to state your opinion is NORMAL and EXPECTED
  → Candidates who NEVER use "je" sound robotic (Linh's Response B)

RULE 2: LIMIT "je" to 2–3 times per response
  → "Je" in the thesis statement → ✅ Natural
  → "Je" in the conclusion → ✅ Reinforcing
  → "Je" in every sentence → ❌ Too personal, sounds like a diary

RULE 3: UPGRADE "je pense" to more sophisticated "je" expressions
  → "Je pense que" → Courant → Acceptable but basic
  → "J'estime que" → Soutenu → Better
  → "Il me semble que" → Soutenu → Best (blends personal + impersonal)
  → "Je suis convaincu(e) que" → Soutenu → Strong positioning
  → "À mon sens" → Soutenu → Elegant

RULE 4: ALTERNATE "je" with impersonal constructions
  → Sentence 1: "Il me semble que..." (personal-impersonal blend)
  → Sentence 5: "On peut observer que..." (impersonal "on")
  → Sentence 10: "J'estime que..." (personal)
  → This ALTERNATION creates a NATURAL voice within formal register

The "On" Calibration Rules:

"ON" HAS THREE FACES IN FRENCH:

FACE 1: "On" = "Nous" (We — informal collective)
  → "On va au cinéma" = "Nous allons au cinéma"
  → This usage is COURANT — acceptable but not sophisticated

FACE 2: "On" = "Les gens" / "La société" (People in general — formal impersonal)
  → "On observe une augmentation de la sédentarité"
  → "On ne saurait ignorer les bienfaits du sport"
  → This usage is SOUTENU — PREFERRED in TEF Section B

FACE 3: "On" = "Quelqu'un" (Someone — very informal)
  → "On m'a dit que..." = "Quelqu'un m'a dit que..."
  → This usage is FAMILIER — AVOID in TEF

TEF RULE: Use "on" ONLY in Face 2 (impersonal general sense)
  ✅ "On constate que..." / "On peut affirmer que..." / "On ne saurait nier que..."
  ❌ "On va voir que..." / "On m'a dit que..." / "On fait comme ça"

The "Nous" vs. "On" Decision:

SituationUse "Nous"Use "On"
Formal assertion including yourself and the reader✅ Nous pouvons constater que...Also acceptable: On peut constater que...
General observation about societyLess natural✅ On observe que la sédentarité augmente.
Academic "editorial we"✅ Nous avons vu que... Nous examinerons...Less formal alternative: On a vu que...
Referring to "people in general"Too specific✅ On ne saurait ignorer...

The Ideal Pronoun Distribution in a 220-Word Response:

"Je" / "Il me semble" / "J'estime": 2–3 times (opinion moments)
"On" (impersonal): 2–3 times (general observations)
"Il" (impersonal constructions): 2–3 times (formal assertions)
"Nous" (collective): 0–2 times (optional, for academic "we")
"Ils/Elles" (third person reference): As needed for arguments
NO pronoun (noun subjects): Majority of sentences

THIS MIX = Natural voice within formal register = TARGET ZONE

How you form negative sentences reveals your register level INSTANTLY

The Negation Spectrum:

Critical Rule: NEVER Drop the "ne"

IN SPOKEN FRENCH:
  "Je sais pas" / "C'est pas vrai" / "Il y a pas de problème"
  → Dropping "ne" is NORMAL in speech
  → 90% of French speakers do this in conversation

IN TEF SECTION B:
  "Je ne sais pas" / "Ce n'est pas vrai" / "Il n'y a pas de problème"
  → "ne" is MANDATORY in formal writing
  → Dropping "ne" is the SINGLE MOST VISIBLE register error
  → Evaluators check for this SPECIFICALLY
  → ONE missing "ne" = instant register penalty

DETECTION METHOD:
  During review (Step 8 of Ch.20 pipeline):
  → Scan every negative sentence
  → Confirm "ne" is present before "pas/plus/jamais/rien/aucun"
  → This 30-second check can save significant Dimension 3 + 4 points

Sophisticated Negation Expressions for TEF:

Standard NegationUpgraded VersionRegister Boost
Ce n'est pas suffisant.Ce n'est guère suffisant."Guère" = barely → More measured, more formal
On ne peut pas ignorer...On ne saurait ignorer..."Ne saurait" = conditional of "savoir" → Very formal
Il n'y a pas de solution simple.Il n'existe aucune solution simple."N'existe aucune" → More precise, more formal
Ce n'est pas la bonne approche.Ce n'est nullement la bonne approche."Nullement" → Emphatic formal negation

Ça ne marche pas.Cette approche ne s'avère pas efficace.Complete lexical + structural upgrade

The "Ne...saurait" Power Tool:

"NE SAURAIT" = Could not / Cannot possibly
  → Conditional of "savoir" used as formal negation
  → This is one of the HIGHEST register negation forms in French
  → Using it correctly = INSTANT C1 signal to the evaluator

EXAMPLES:
  "On ne saurait ignorer les conséquences de la sédentarité."
  → = "One cannot possibly ignore the consequences of sedentary lifestyle"

  "Cela ne saurait justifier l'absence de mesures concrètes."
  → = "This cannot possibly justify the lack of concrete measures"

  "Les bénéfices individuels ne sauraient primer sur l'intérêt collectif."
  → = "Individual benefits cannot take precedence over collective interest"

USAGE LIMIT: 1–2 times per response
  → More than twice → Sounds repetitive and forced
  → Once in the body + once in the conclusion = perfect distribution

🔷 DIMENSION C: QUESTION FORMATION

How you form questions (including rhetorical ones from Chapter 22) reveals your register level

The Question Formation Spectrum:

Formation TypeRegisterTEF StatusExample
Intonation only (no structural change)Familier❌ NEVERLe sport est important?
Est-ce que + normal orderCourant✅ Acceptable but basicEst-ce que le sport est important?
Inversion (verb-subject)Soutenu✅ PREFERREDLe sport est-il important?
Complex inversion with noun subjectSoutenu✅ EXCELLENTLe sport joue-t-il un rôle essentiel?

Inversion Rules for TEF:

SIMPLE INVERSION (pronoun subject):
  "Peut-on ignorer...?" / "Faut-il obliger...?" / "Est-il nécessaire de...?"
  → Standard formal question → ALWAYS preferred in TEF

COMPLEX INVERSION (noun subject):
  "Le sport est-il essentiel?" → The noun ("le sport") stays, pronoun ("il") added after verb
  "L'éducation physique joue-t-elle un rôle fondamental?"
  → Note: "-t-" is inserted between vowels for pronunciation
  → This is the MOST FORMAL question type → Use for rhetorical questions (Ch.22)

"EST-CE QUE" — WHEN ACCEPTABLE:
  → When the question is embedded in a longer structure
  → "La question est de savoir est-ce que le sport..." → ❌ WRONG
  → "La question est de savoir si le sport..." → ✅ CORRECT
  → In TEF, "est-ce que" in standalone questions is ACCEPTABLE but not impressive
  → For rhetorical questions (Chapter 22): ALWAYS use inversion, never "est-ce que"

The "Si" Embedded Question (Indirect Question):

DIRECT QUESTION (with inversion — Chapter 22):
  "Le sport devrait-il être obligatoire?"
  → Used for RHETORICAL EFFECT → Appropriate

INDIRECT QUESTION (with "si" — no inversion):
  "On peut se demander si le sport devrait être obligatoire."
  → Used for NEUTRAL PRESENTATION of a question → Appropriate
  → "Si" replaces the question mark → No inversion needed
  → This is very useful for INTRODUCTION framing (Chapter 10)

COMMON ERROR:
  ❌ "On peut se demander est-ce que le sport devrait être obligatoire."
  → Mixing indirect question ("on peut se demander") with direct question ("est-ce que")
  → ALWAYS use "si" for indirect questions
  ✅ "On peut se demander si le sport devrait être obligatoire."

🔷 DIMENSION E: SENTENCE OPENINGS

The way you BEGIN sentences reveals your register level and controls the response's RHYTHM

Linh's Response B Problem — The "Il" Repetition:

"Il est impératif de..."
"Il convient de noter que..."
"Il apparaît indéniable que..."
"Il serait toutefois réducteur..."
"Il n'en demeure pas moins que..."

→ 5 sentences → ALL start with impersonal "Il"
→ This creates MECHANICAL MONOTONY
→ The response sounds like it was generated by a formula, not a human
→ Impersonal constructions (Chapter 17 Type 7) are EXCELLENT tools
→ But using them EXCLUSIVELY = robotic register

The Golden Rule of Sentence Openings:

NEVER start more than 2 consecutive sentences with the SAME type of opening

✅ VARIED:
  "L'activité physique..." (Noun subject)
  "En effet, elle contribue..." (Connector + pronoun)
  "Il convient toutefois de nuancer..." (Impersonal "Il")
  "Confrontés à ce défi, les éducateurs..." (Participial phrase)
  "On ne saurait ignorer que..." ("On" + verb)
  → 5 sentences → 5 DIFFERENT opening types → Natural formal flow

❌ MONOTONOUS:
  "Il est important de..."
  "Il convient de..."
  "Il faut souligner que..."
  "Il apparaît que..."
  "Il ne fait aucun doute que..."
  → 5 sentences → ALL start with impersonal "Il" → Robotic

How you state your personal opinion determines whether you sound like a confident writer or a hesitant student — or a robotic machine

À mon avisCourant-SoutenuModerate confidence✅ Good — natural and clear

The Opinion Expression Strategy:

THESIS STATEMENT (Introduction — Chapter 10):
  Use a STRONG, CLEAR opinion expression
  BEST OPTIONS:
  → "Il me semble que..." (measured + formal)
  → "J'estime que..." (confident + formal)
  → "Je suis convaincu(e) que..." (very strong — use if you have a clear position)

BODY PARAGRAPHS:
  Generally AVOID "je" — let the arguments speak for themselves
  Use impersonal constructions:
  → "On peut constater que..."
  → "Il apparaît que..."
  → "Force est de constater que..."
  → The arguments themselves ARE your opinion — you do not need to repeat "je pense"

CONCLUSION:
  Return to personal voice for REINFORCEMENT
  → "J'estime donc que..." / "À mon sens..."
  → This creates a FRAME: personal opening → impersonal body → personal closing
  → The evaluator sees a VOICE, not a machine

The "Hedging" Technique — Expressing Strong Opinions Without Sounding Dogmatic:

DOGMATIC (too absolute):
  "Le sport DOIT être obligatoire. C'est CERTAIN."
  → Sounds aggressive → No room for nuance → Contradicts concession (Chapter 16)

HEDGED (confident but measured):
  "Il me semble que le sport devrait être obligatoire, dans la mesure où ses bénéfices
   pour la santé des jeunes sont amplement documentés."
  → "Il me semble" = hedged (not "je suis sûr")
  → "devrait" = conditional (not "doit")
  → "dans la mesure où" = specifies conditions (not absolute)
  → This sounds CONFIDENT yet NUANCED → Perfect TEF register

G4 — The "Tu" Prohibition:
"TU" = NEVER USED IN TEF SECTION B

WHY:
  → "Tu" implies direct address to a specific informal interlocutor
  → TEF Section B is NOT a letter or a conversation
  → Even the prompt uses "vous" (formal) when addressing the candidate

WHAT TO USE INSTEAD:
  → "On" (impersonal general): "On peut observer que..."
  → "Nous" (collective formal): "Nous pouvons constater que..."
  → "Il" (impersonal construction): "Il est possible de..."
  → "Chacun" (each person): "Chacun devrait être conscient que..."
  → "Les individus" / "Les citoyens": Specific noun subjects

THE "VOUS" QUESTION:
  → "Vous" is acceptable in French formal writing as a general "you"
  → BUT in TEF Section B, it is rarely needed
  → Use "on" or "nous" instead for a more academic tone
  → Exception: Rhetorical question → "Peut-on réellement...?" uses "on" not "vous"

🔶 DIMENSION H: TONAL BALANCE — THE MASTER CALIBRATION

The final and most important dimension — how to MIX all elements for a natural formal voice

The Tonal Balance Formula:

THE PERFECT TEF PARAGRAPH CONTAINS:

1 impersonal construction (formal anchor)
+ 1 personal touch ("il me semble" or "j'estime" or "à mon sens")
+ 2–3 soutenu vocabulary items (Chapter 18)
+ 1–2 courant vocabulary items (readability)
+ 1 sophisticated negation ("ne saurait" or "ne guère")
+ Standard "ne...pas" negations elsewhere
+ Varied sentence openings (no pattern repetition)
+ 0 oral markers (completely eliminated)

= NATURAL FORMAL REGISTER (Target Zone)

Full Demonstration — Linh's Response CALIBRATED:

Linh's Response A (Too Informal) — For Reference:

"Bon, moi je pense que le sport à l'école c'est super important. Genre, les jeunes ils bougent pas assez et du coup ils sont en mauvaise forme..."

Linh's Response B (Too Formal) — For Reference:

"Il est impératif de souligner que la pratique sportive obligatoire au sein des établissements scolaires constitue une nécessité fondamentale et incontournable..."

Linh's Response C (CALIBRATED — Target Zone):

La question de l'obligation sportive dans les écoles suscite un débat légitime. Il me semble que rendre le sport obligatoire constitue une mesure essentielle, tant pour la santé physique que pour le bien-être psychologique des élèves.

En effet, la sédentarité croissante des jeunes engendre des problèmes de santé préoccupants. L'activité physique régulière, en favorisant la concentration et la confiance en soi, contribue directement à la réussite scolaire. On ne saurait ignorer, par ailleurs, que le sport développe des valeurs fondamentales telles que la coopération et le dépassement de soi.

3. Master the "Personal Frame" Technique

THE PERSONAL FRAME:

INTRODUCTION: Personal voice → "Il me semble que..." / "J'estime que..."
  → Sets the HUMAN tone → The evaluator knows a real person is arguing

BODY PARAGRAPHS: Impersonal voice → "On observe..." / "Il convient de..." / Noun subjects
  → Creates ACADEMIC distance → Arguments feel OBJECTIVE, not subjective
  → The evaluator sees ANALYTICAL thinking

CONCLUSION: Return to personal voice → "J'estime donc que..." / "À mon sens..."
  → CLOSES the personal frame → The evaluator remembers a HUMAN voice

THIS CREATES:
  HUMAN → ANALYTICAL → HUMAN
  → Like a professor who introduces themselves (personal)
  → Then delivers a lecture (impersonal/analytical)
  → Then shares their final thought (personal)
  → This is the NATURAL rhythm of academic French argumentation

4. Use the "Formality Gradient" Within Each Paragraph

WITHIN A SINGLE PARAGRAPH:

Sentence 1: MOST FORMAL (sets the tone)
  → "Il convient de souligner que l'activité physique joue un rôle fondamental..."

Sentence 2–3: MODERATELY FORMAL (develops naturally)
  → "En effet, le sport favorise la concentration et renforce la confiance en soi."
  → "On observe d'ailleurs que les élèves sportifs obtiennent de meilleurs résultats."

Sentence 4: SLIGHTLY LESS FORMAL (natural conclusion)
  → "Le sport est donc bien plus qu'un simple divertissement."

THIS GRADIENT:
  → Prevents the MONOTONY of uniform hyper-formality
  → Creates a NATURAL descent from formal to clear
  → Each sentence is STILL within the soutenu register
  → But the INTENSITY varies → Creating RHYTHM
  → This is what distinguishes AUTHENTIC formal writing from ROBOTIC formal writing

5. Calibrate "C'est" Usage

"C'EST" IN TEF SECTION B:

"C'est" is NOT informal — it is courant
It IS acceptable in TEF but should be used STRATEGICALLY:

✅ STRATEGIC USES OF "C'EST":
  → "C'est pourquoi" (consequence connector — Chapter 15) ✅
  → "C'est la raison pour laquelle" (formal consequence) ✅
  → "C'est...qui/que" (emphatic structure — Chapter 22 Weapon 2) ✅
  → "C'est-à-dire" (clarification — Chapter 19) ✅

❌ OVERUSE OF "C'EST":
  → "C'est important. C'est un problème. C'est vrai. C'est pour ça."
  → 4 sentences starting with "c'est" → Sounds oral → Register drops

UPGRADE "C'EST" WHEN POSSIBLE:
  "C'est un problème" → "Cela constitue un problème" / "Il s'agit d'un problème"
  "C'est important" → "Cela revêt une importance particulière" / "Cette question est fondamentale"
  "C'est vrai" → "Il est vrai que" / "Certes" (Chapter 16)
  "C'est pour ça" → "C'est pourquoi" / "C'est la raison pour laquelle"

TARGET: Maximum 2 "c'est" per response
  → Both in strategic positions (emphasis or consequence)
  → Never as simple "c'est + adjective" (too basic)

❌ WHAT SHOULD NOT DO

1. Do NOT Eliminate "Je" Entirely

❌ ZERO "JE" RESPONSE:
  "Il apparaît que... Il convient de... Il est indéniable que...
   On peut constater que... Il ne fait aucun doute que..."
  → WHO is arguing? → A machine? A textbook?
  → The evaluator sees NO personal voice → Suspects memorization
  → Dimension 2 concern: "Is this genuine argumentation?"

✅ BALANCED "JE" RESPONSE:
  Introduction: "Il me semble que..." (1 personal touch)
  Body: Impersonal throughout (0 "je")
  Conclusion: "J'estime donc que..." (1 personal touch)
  → 2 instances of personal voice → HUMAN presence confirmed
  → The rest is impersonal → ACADEMIC credibility maintained

2. Do NOT Use "Nous" as "Me and My Friends"

"NOUS" IN ACADEMIC FRENCH:

✅ EDITORIAL "NOUS" (includes the reader in a shared analysis):
  "Nous pouvons constater que le sport joue un rôle essentiel."
  → = "You and I, as reasonable people examining this issue, can observe..."
  → This is ACADEMIC "nous" → Appropriate

❌ PERSONAL "NOUS" (refers to you and specific people):
  "Nous faisons du sport tous les jours avec mes amis."
  → = "Me and my friends exercise every day"
  → This is PERSONAL "nous" → Inappropriate for argumentation
  → This belongs in Section A (letter writing), not Section B

3. Do NOT Mix Registers Within the Same Sentence

❌ REGISTER CLASH:
  "Il convient de souligner que les jeunes, genre, ils bougent pas assez."
  → "Il convient de souligner" = Soutenu
  → "genre" = Familier
  → "ils bougent pas" = Familier (missing "ne")
  → THREE register levels in ONE sentence → Evaluator confused → Register penalty

✅ CONSISTENT REGISTER:
  "Il convient de souligner que les jeunes ne pratiquent guère
   d'activité physique régulière."
  → "Il convient de souligner" = Soutenu
  → "ne...guère" = Soutenu
  → "activité physique régulière" = Soutenu
  → CONSISTENT register throughout → ✅

4. Do NOT Overuse Impersonal Constructions

IMPERSONAL CONSTRUCTION LIMIT: 2–3 per response

CHAPTER 17 TAUGHT: Type 7 (impersonal) as one of 7 sentence types
CHAPTER 23 REINFORCES: Impersonal constructions are ONE tool, not the ONLY tool

COUNT YOUR IMPERSONAL CONSTRUCTIONS:
  "Il est essentiel de..." → 1
  "Il convient de..." → 2
  "Il ne fait aucun doute que..." → 3
  → STOP → No more impersonal constructions in this response
  → Switch to noun subjects, "on," participial phrases, etc.

5. Do NOT Use the Conditional to Hedge EVERYTHING

❌ OVER-HEDGING:
  "Le sport serait peut-être important. Il pourrait éventuellement
   contribuer au bien-être. On pourrait éventuellement envisager
   de le rendre obligatoire."
  → Every verb is conditional → Nothing is stated with confidence
  → The evaluator reads: "This person is afraid to commit to any position"
  → Dimension 1 concern: "What IS their opinion?"

✅ STRATEGIC HEDGING:
  "Le sport est essentiel au bien-être des jeunes." (présent — confident)
  "Il me semble que le rendre obligatoire constituerait une mesure pertinente."
  (conditionnel — measured for the proposal)
  "On ne saurait ignorer ses bénéfices." (conditionnel of savoir — formal negation)
  → Mix of confident statements (présent) + measured proposals (conditionnel)
  → The evaluator sees: Confidence WHERE appropriate + Nuance WHERE appropriate

6. Do NOT Use "Etc." in Formal Writing

❌ "LAZY ENUMERATION":
  "Le sport développe la coopération, la discipline, etc."
  → "Etc." = "I cannot think of more examples" → Sounds lazy
  → In formal writing, "etc." is considered INCOMPLETE reasoning

✅ COMPLETE ENUMERATION:
  Option A: Name all items → "...la coopération, la discipline et le dépassement de soi."
  Option B: Use "entre autres" → "...la coopération et la discipline, entre autres."
  Option C: Use "notamment" → "...des valeurs fondamentales, notamment la coopération et la discipline."
  → Each option sounds DELIBERATE, not lazy

7. Do NOT Start Sentences With "Mais" or "Et" in Formal Writing

IN ORAL FRENCH:
  "Mais c'est pas vrai!" / "Et en plus, c'est cher!"
  → Starting with "mais" or "et" is NORMAL in speech

IN TEF SECTION B:
  → Starting with "Mais" = Borderline acceptable (some evaluators penalize, others don't)
  → Starting with "Et" = Generally avoided in formal French writing

SAFE ALTERNATIVES:
  "Mais..." → "Cependant, ..." / "Néanmoins, ..." / "Toutefois, ..."
  "Et..." → "De plus, ..." / "Par ailleurs, ..." / "En outre, ..."
  
  → These alternatives are ALWAYS safe → Use them by default
  → Exception: "Mais" in "Certes...mais" concession (Chapter 16) = ACCEPTABLE
    because it is part of a FIXED concessive structure

🚫 WHEN NOT TO USE

When NOT to Push Register Higher

  • When you are unsure of the sophisticated word's meaning

    • → A misused soutenu word is WORSE than a correctly used courant word
    • → Refer to Chapter 18 Escalation Ladders → Drop to B1 backup if uncertain
    • → The evaluator forgives simplicity but penalizes inaccuracy

  • When the sentence is already long and complex

    • → Adding sophisticated vocabulary to a 25-word complex sentence → Overloaded
    • → Use simpler vocabulary in longer sentences → Save sophisticated words for shorter, punchier sentences
    • → This creates the RHYTHM discussed in Chapter 17

  • When you have already used 3 impersonal constructions

    • → Adding a 4th → Robotic territory → Switch to another sentence type
    • → The variety itself signals competence more than any single construction

  • When the concept has no natural soutenu equivalent

    • → "Le sport" is "le sport" → Do NOT force "la pratique d'activités physiques et sportives" every time
    • → "Les enfants" is acceptable → Do NOT always say "les populations juvéniles"
    • → Forcing upgrades on EVERY word → Artificial → Evaluator detects it

🕳️ COMMON PITFALLS AND FAILURE MODES

Pitfall 1: The "Ça" Slip

  • Writing "ça" instead of "cela" even once
  • → The evaluator's eye CATCHES this immediately
  • → It is the most visible register error in French formal writing
  • Detection: During review, search for every "ça" → Replace with "cela"
  • Prevention: Train yourself to NEVER write "ça" — even in notes

Pitfall 2: The Missing "Ne" in Negation

  • Dropping "ne" from negative sentences (as in oral French)
  • → Second most visible register error
  • → Especially dangerous with "ne...que" (only): "C'est qu'un début" ❌ → "Ce n'est qu'un début" ✅
  • Detection: During review, check every "pas/plus/jamais/rien/aucun/que" → Is "ne" present before the verb?

Pitfall 3: The Impersonal Construction Overdose

  • Every sentence starting with "Il est... Il convient... Il apparaît... Il faut... Il ne fait aucun doute..."
  • → Linh's Response B problem
  • → Sounds like a bureaucratic report
  • Detection: Count impersonal "Il" constructions → More than 3? → Replace extras with noun subjects or "on"

Pitfall 4: The Register Roller Coaster

  • Alternating between high and low register unpredictably
  • → Sentence 1: "Il convient de souligner..." (soutenu)
  • → Sentence 2: "Genre, les jeunes, ils font pas de sport" (familier)
  • → This is MORE damaging than consistently courant register
  • Detection: Read the response aloud → Does the TONE stay consistent?

Pitfall 5: The Vocabulary Mismatch

  • Using a hyper-soutenu word inside a courant sentence
  • → "Les jeunes sont pas très motivés par les activités préconisées."
    • → "les jeunes sont pas" = familier (missing "ne")
    • → "préconisées" = soutenu
      • → The mismatch is JARRING — like wearing a tuxedo jacket with flip-flops
    • Detection: Check that the SENTENCE STRUCTURE matches the VOCABULARY LEVEL
    • Solution: If the vocabulary is soutenu → The grammar must also be soutenu

    Pitfall 6: The "Donc" Overuse

    • "Donc" is acceptable in formal writing BUT is heavily overused in oral French as a filler
    • → If "donc" appears 3+ times → Evaluator suspects oral habit, not logical reasoning
    • Solution: Use "donc" maximum 1 time → Replace others with "par conséquent" / "dès lors" / "c'est pourquoi" / "ainsi"

    Pitfall 7: The False Sophistication Trap

    • Using a sophisticated word INCORRECTLY because you memorized it without understanding its precise meaning
    • → "Le sport pallier les problèmes de santé" ❌ → "Pallier" is TRANSITIVE DIRECT → "Le sport pallie les problèmes de santé" ✅ (no "à")
    • → "Les élèves s'avèrent de faire du sport" ❌ → "S'avérer" means "to turn out to be" → Not used with "de faire"
    • Rule: If you are not 100% certain of a word's exact construction → Use the simpler equivalent you DO know

    Pitfall 8: The Subjunctive Avoidance OR Overuse

    AVOIDANCE (too cautious):
  → Never using "bien que + subjunctive" → Missing register opportunity
  → Never using "il est essentiel que + subjunctive" → Staying in courant zone
  → The evaluator sees: "This candidate avoids complex structures"

OVERUSE (too ambitious):
  → "Bien que... il faut que... pour que... avant que... quoique..."
  → 5 subjunctives in one response → Sounds forced
  → The evaluator sees: "This candidate is showing off, not writing naturally"

BALANCE:
  → 1–2 subjunctive triggers per response = NATURAL formal writing
  → "Bien que" (Chapter 16) + "pour que" or "il est essentiel que" = sufficient
  → Each one is IMPACTFUL because it is not overused

    What Chapter 23 Established

    REGISTER = THE TONE AND FORMALITY LEVEL OF YOUR WRITING
  → Too informal (familier) = Dimension 3 penalty
  → Too formal/robotic (hyper-soutenu) = Dimension 3 concern + authenticity doubt
  → TARGET = Soutenu naturel (formal AND authentic)

8 REGISTER CALIBRATION DIMENSIONS:
  A: Pronouns → "Je" 2–3x + "On" 2–3x + "Il" 2–3x + Noun subjects majority
  B: Negation → Always include "ne" + Use "ne saurait/guère" 1–2x
  C: Questions → Use inversion for rhetorical questions, "si" for indirect questions
  D: Vocabulary → 70% soutenu + 30% courant = natural formal blend
  E: Sentence openings → Maximum variety, no pattern repetition
  F: Opinion → "Il me semble" / "J'estime" (not "je pense")
  G: Oral markers → ZERO tolerance: no "ça/genre/du coup/y'a/missing ne"
  H: Tonal balance → Personal frame (intro/conclusion) + Impersonal body

THE PERSONAL FRAME TECHNIQUE:
  Introduction: Personal voice ("Il me semble que...")
  Body: Impersonal voice ("On observe..." / "Il convient de..." / Noun subjects)
  Conclusion: Personal voice returns ("J'estime donc que...")
  = HUMAN → ANALYTICAL → HUMAN

THE 60-SECOND REGISTER SCAN (during review):
  10 sec: Oral markers check
  10 sec: Pronoun balance check
  10 sec: Sentence opening variety check
  10 sec: Vocabulary level check
  10 sec: Negation check
  10 sec: Overall tone check

CRITICAL RULES:
  → "Ça" → ALWAYS "cela"
  → Missing "ne" → ALWAYS add it
  → "Tu" → NEVER in Section B
