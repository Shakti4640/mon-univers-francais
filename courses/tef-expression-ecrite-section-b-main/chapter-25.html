🟡 HOW TO APPROACH IT

Step 1: Understand WHY Self-Review Fails Without a Protocol

THE PSYCHOLOGY OF ERROR BLINDNESS:

PROBLEM 1: FAMILIARITY BLINDNESS
  → You just WROTE the text → Your brain KNOWS what it INTENDED to write
  → When you re-read, your brain sees what it MEANT, not what it WROTE
  → "les conséquences que j'ai observé" → Your brain reads "observées" because
     that is what you INTENDED → The missing "es" is INVISIBLE to you

PROBLEM 2: HOLISTIC READING
  → When you read your text "normally," you process MEANING
  → You think: "Yes, this argument makes sense" → You feel satisfied
  → But you are NOT checking GRAMMAR, COHESION, or REGISTER
  → You are checking CONTENT ONLY → 3 out of 4 dimensions are UNCHECKED

PROBLEM 3: FATIGUE BIAS
  → You just spent 25–30 minutes writing → Your brain is TIRED
  → Tired brains take SHORTCUTS → They skip details
  → The review becomes superficial → Errors survive

PROBLEM 4: TIME PRESSURE PANIC
  → "I only have 4 minutes left" → Panic → Rush through the review
  → Rushing = reading FASTER = catching LESS
  → Without a PROTOCOL, you do not know WHERE to look → You look everywhere → You see nothing

THE SOLUTION:
  → Do NOT read your text "normally"
  → Run TARGETED SCANS — each scan looks for ONE specific error type
  → Each scan takes 30–60 seconds
  → 5 scans × 45 seconds = 3 minutes 45 seconds
  → This catches MORE errors in LESS time than a single unfocused re-read

Step 2: Understand the Error Cost Hierarchy

  • Not all errors cost the same number of points
  • Some errors devastate your score; others cause minor deductions
  • The protocol must check HIGH-COST errors FIRST
ERROR COST HIERARCHY (from most costly to least costly):

TIER 1 — CATASTROPHIC ERRORS (Each one can drop you an entire level):
  → Off-topic response (Dimension 1 — Chapter 2)
  → Missing introduction OR conclusion (Dimension 2 — Chapters 10, 11)
  → No clear position stated (Dimension 1 — Chapter 3)
  → Word count severely under 150 (Dimension 1)

TIER 2 — MAJOR ERRORS (Each one costs significant points):
  → Missing cross-paragraph cohesion (Dimension 2 — Chapter 19)
  → Conclusion introduces new arguments (Dimension 2 — Chapter 11)
  → Connector repetition (3+ times same connector) (Dimension 3 — Chapter 13)
  → Register violations ("ça," missing "ne") (Dimension 3 — Chapter 23)
  → Subjunctive errors after triggers (Dimension 4 — Chapter 16)

TIER 3 — MODERATE ERRORS (Each one costs some points):
  → Past participle agreement errors (Dimension 4 — Chapter 17)
  → Gender agreement errors on key nouns (Dimension 4 — Chapter 18)
  → Missing backward glance between sentences (Dimension 2 — Chapter 19)
  → Vocabulary repetition (same B2 word used 3+ times) (Dimension 3 — Chapter 18)

TIER 4 — MINOR ERRORS (Small deductions, often forgiven):
  → Spelling errors on complex words (Dimension 4)
  → Minor preposition errors (Dimension 4)
  → Slightly awkward phrasing (Dimension 3)
  → Accent marks missing (Dimension 4)

THE PROTOCOL CHECKS: TIER 1 → TIER 2 → TIER 3 → TIER 4
  → If time runs out after Tier 2, you have already caught the MOST DAMAGING errors
  → This is STRATEGIC review, not random review

Step 3: Understand the 7-Scan Protocol Architecture

THE 7-SCAN SELF-EDITING PROTOCOL:

SCAN 1: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY CHECK (Tier 1 — 30 seconds)
SCAN 2: CONSIGNE COMPLIANCE CHECK (Tier 1 — 20 seconds)
SCAN 3: COHESION AND CONNECTOR CHECK (Tier 2 — 45 seconds)
SCAN 4: REGISTER CHECK (Tier 2 — 30 seconds)
SCAN 5: SUBJUNCTIVE AND MOOD CHECK (Tier 2 — 30 seconds)
SCAN 6: AGREEMENT CHECK (Tier 3 — 45 seconds)
SCAN 7: FINAL POLISH (Tier 4 — 30 seconds)

TOTAL TIME: 3 minutes 30 seconds to 5 minutes
  → Minimum viable protocol: Scans 1–4 (2 minutes 5 seconds)
  → Full protocol: Scans 1–7 (3 minutes 30 seconds to 5 minutes)
  → If you have only 2 minutes: Run Scans 1–4 ONLY
  → If you have 5 minutes: Run ALL 7 scans

🟢 HOW CAN WE SOLVE IT

THE COMPLETE 7-SCAN SELF-EDITING PROTOCOL

🔷 SCAN 1: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY CHECK

Time: 30 seconds | Tier: 1 (Catastrophic) | Chapters: 5, 10, 11, 14, 21

What You Check:

  • Do NOT read the TEXT — read the STRUCTURE ONLY
  • Look at your response from a BIRD'S EYE VIEW
CHECKLIST:

□ Are there VISIBLE PARAGRAPH BREAKS?
  → Introduction = separate paragraph ✅ or ❌
  → Body 1 = separate paragraph ✅ or ❌
  → Body 2 = separate paragraph ✅ or ❌
  → Body 3 / Synthèse (if present) = separate paragraph ✅ or ❌
  → Conclusion = separate paragraph ✅ or ❌

□ Does the Introduction EXIST and contain at least 2 sentences?
  → If missing → ADD a brief 2-sentence intro immediately
  → Even a weak introduction is better than NO introduction

□ Does the Conclusion EXIST and contain at least 1 sentence?
  → If missing → ADD a brief 1-sentence conclusion immediately
  → "En définitive, [restate position]." → 10 seconds to write

□ Is the BODY divided into at least 2 sections?
  → If it is one massive block → Add a paragraph break at the logical division point

□ Approximate WORD COUNT — does it FEEL like 200+ words?
  → Count the lines → Roughly 10–12 words per line
  → 18 lines × 12 words = ~216 words ✅
  → If under 15 lines → Possibly under 180 words → Consider adding a sentence

How to Fix Structural Issues in 15 Seconds:

Problem DetectedEmergency Fix
Missing introductionAdd: "La question de [topic] suscite un vif débat. Il me semble que [position]."
Missing conclusionAdd: "En définitive, [brief restatement of position]."
No paragraph breaksDraw a clear line or symbol (¶) to indicate paragraph separation
Word count too lowAdd one more sentence to the longest body paragraph — an example or consequence

🔶 SCAN 2: CONSIGNE COMPLIANCE CHECK

Time: 20 seconds | Tier: 1 (Catastrophic) | Chapters: 1, 2, 3

What You Check:

  • Re-read the PROMPT one more time (5 seconds)
  • Then check these 3 questions:
CHECKLIST:

□ Did I answer the EXACT question asked?
  → Re-read the prompt → Re-read your thesis statement
  → Do they MATCH?
  → If the prompt asks "les réseaux sociaux ont-ils un impact NÉGATIF?"
     and your thesis says "les réseaux sociaux sont utiles" → MISALIGNMENT
  → FIX: Adjust your conclusion to clearly address the original question

□ Did I state a CLEAR POSITION?
  → Can the evaluator identify my position in ONE sentence?
  → If your thesis is vague ("il y a des avantages et des inconvénients") → ADD clarity
  → FIX: Add "J'estime que..." or "Il me semble que..." to your introduction or conclusion

□ Did I include at least ONE concrete example?
  → The prompt says "à l'aide d'exemples" → Is there at least one?
  → If NO example exists → ADD a brief one to a body paragraph
  → FIX: "...comme en témoigne [specific example]." → 5 seconds to add

🔷 SCAN 3: COHESION AND CONNECTOR CHECK

Time: 45 seconds | Tier: 2 (Major) | Chapters: 7, 13, 19

What You Check:

3A — Connector Repetition Scan (20 seconds):
METHOD: Read ONLY the first 3–4 words of each sentence
  → You are looking for REPEATED connectors
  → Especially: "Cependant," "De plus," "En effet," "Par ailleurs"

WHAT TO LOOK FOR:
  → Same connector used 3+ times → PROBLEM
  → Same connector used in consecutive sentences → PROBLEM
  → "Cela" starting 3+ sentences → PROBLEM (Chapter 19 limit)

HOW TO FIX:
  → Replace the SECOND or THIRD instance with a synonym:
    "Cependant" → "Néanmoins" or "Toutefois"
    "De plus" → "Par ailleurs" or "En outre" or "De surcroît"
    "En effet" → "De fait" or (delete — sometimes unnecessary)
    "Cela" → "Ce phénomène" or "Cette tendance" or "Cette réalité"
3B — Cross-Paragraph Cohesion Scan (15 seconds):
METHOD: Read ONLY the last sentence of each paragraph
        AND the first sentence of the NEXT paragraph
  → Is there ANY link between them?
  → Does the first sentence of the new paragraph refer to ANYTHING from the previous one?

WHAT TO LOOK FOR:
  → Complete topic JUMP with no transition → PROBLEM
  → No demonstrative, no synonym, no connector at paragraph opening → PROBLEM

HOW TO FIX:
  → Add a transition word/phrase at the start of the new paragraph:
    "Cependant, ..." / "Toutefois, ..." / "Il convient néanmoins de nuancer..."
  → OR add a demonstrative reference:
    "Cette réalité amène toutefois à considérer..."
    "Face à ce constat, ..."
3C — Backward Glance Spot-Check (10 seconds):
METHOD: Pick 2–3 RANDOM sentence transitions in the body
  → Does sentence N+1 contain ANY reference to sentence N?
  → A pronoun? A synonym? A connector? A demonstrative?

WHAT TO LOOK FOR:
  → Two consecutive sentences with ZERO link → PROBLEM
  → Especially in body paragraphs where cohesion is most critical

HOW TO FIX:
  → Insert "Cela..." or "En effet,..." or "Ce/Cette..." before the disconnected sentence
  → Takes 2–3 seconds per fix

🔶 SCAN 4: REGISTER CHECK

Time: 30 seconds | Tier: 2 (Major) | Chapter: 23

What You Check:

THIS IS THE 60-SECOND REGISTER SCAN FROM CHAPTER 23 — COMPRESSED TO 30 SECONDS

PRIORITY ITEMS ONLY:

□ SEARCH 1: Find every "ça" → Replace with "cela" (5 seconds)
  → This is the #1 most visible register error
  → Your eyes should JUMP to any "ça" on the page

□ SEARCH 2: Find every negative sentence → Is "ne" present? (10 seconds)
  → Look for "pas," "plus," "jamais," "rien," "aucun"
  → For each one → Is "ne" before the verb? If not → ADD it immediately
  → "Je pense pas" → "Je ne pense pas"
  → "C'est pas vrai" → "Ce n'est pas vrai"

□ SEARCH 3: Find any oral markers (5 seconds)
  → Quick scan for: "genre," "du coup," "truc," "en fait" (as filler), "y'a"
  → If found → Replace or delete

□ SEARCH 4: Count impersonal "Il" constructions (5 seconds)
  → More than 3? → Change one to a noun subject or "On..."

□ SEARCH 5: Count "je" (5 seconds)
  → More than 3? → Change one to "On observe que..." or impersonal construction
  → Fewer than 1? → Consider adding "J'estime que..." in conclusion

Emergency Register Fixes (2–3 seconds each):

"du coup"Cross out → Write "par conséquent" or "de ce fait"

🔷 SCAN 5: SUBJUNCTIVE AND MOOD CHECK

Time: 30 seconds | Tier: 2 (Major) | Chapters: 16, 17

What You Check:

METHOD: Search for SUBJUNCTIVE TRIGGER WORDS in your text
  → These are specific words that REQUIRE subjunctive in the following clause
  → If you used any of these → Check that the verb after them IS subjunctive

SUBJUNCTIVE TRIGGERS TO SEARCH FOR:

TRIGGER 1: "Bien que" (Chapter 16)
  → SEARCH: Find "bien que" in your text
  → CHECK: Is the next verb SUBJUNCTIVE?
  → COMMON ERRORS:
    ❌ "Bien que les réseaux sociaux SONT utiles" → WRONG (indicative)
    ✅ "Bien que les réseaux sociaux SOIENT utiles" → CORRECT (subjunctive)

TRIGGER 2: "Pour que" / "Afin que" (Chapter 17)
  → SEARCH: Find "pour que" or "afin que"
  → CHECK: Is the next verb SUBJUNCTIVE?
  → ❌ "Pour que les élèves PEUVENT..." → WRONG
  → ✅ "Pour que les élèves PUISSENT..." → CORRECT

TRIGGER 3: "Il est essentiel que" / "Il est nécessaire que" / "Il faut que"
  → SEARCH: Find these expressions
  → CHECK: Subjunctive after "que"?
  → ❌ "Il est essentiel que la société PREND des mesures" → WRONG
  → ✅ "Il est essentiel que la société PRENNE des mesures" → CORRECT

TRIGGER 4: "Quoique" / "Avant que" (if used)
  → Same check → Subjunctive required

TRIGGER 5: "Est-il acceptable que" (Chapter 22 — rhetorical question)
  → ❌ "Est-il acceptable que des millions de personnes SONT exclues?" → WRONG
  → ✅ "Est-il acceptable que des millions de personnes SOIENT exclues?" → CORRECT

Quick Subjunctive Reference for Common Verbs:

If You Find a Subjunctive Error:

OPTION A: Correct the verb form
  → Cross out "sont" → Write "soient" above
  → Takes 2 seconds

OPTION B: Change the trigger to avoid subjunctive entirely
  → Cross out "Bien que les réseaux sociaux soient utiles"
  → Write "Même si les réseaux sociaux sont utiles" (Chapter 16 — "même si" takes indicative)
  → Takes 5 seconds → SAFER if you are unsure of the subjunctive form

OPTION B IS YOUR SAFETY NET:
  → If you cannot remember the subjunctive form of a verb
  → Switch the trigger to one that takes INDICATIVE
  → "Bien que" → "Même si"
  → "Pour que + subjunctive" → "Afin de + infinitive" (if same subject)
  → This is NOT a downgrade — it is a STRATEGIC SUBSTITUTION

🔶 SCAN 6: AGREEMENT CHECK

Time: 45 seconds | Tier: 3 (Moderate) | Chapters: 17, 18

What You Check:

6A — Past Participle Agreement After "Que" (20 seconds):
METHOD: Search for every "que" in a relative clause
  → Then find the PAST PARTICIPLE after it (if compound tense is used)
  → Check: Does the past participle AGREE with the noun "que" refers to?

SEARCH PATTERN:
  "les [NOUN] que [SUBJECT] + avoir + [PAST PARTICIPLE]"

EXAMPLES TO CHECK:
  "les conséquences que j'ai observé___" → "conséquences" = feminine plural
    → CORRECT: "observéES" ✅
    → WRONG: "observé" ❌

  "les mesures que le gouvernement a adopté___" → "mesures" = feminine plural
    → CORRECT: "adoptéES" ✅
    → WRONG: "adopté" ❌

  "les problèmes que cette situation a engendré___" → "problèmes" = masculine plural
    → CORRECT: "engendréS" ✅
    → WRONG: "engendré" ❌

THE RULE (from Chapter 17):
  → With AVOIR: Past participle agrees with the DIRECT OBJECT if it comes BEFORE the verb
  → "Que" always comes BEFORE → So agreement is ALWAYS required after "que" + avoir
  → This is the MOST COMMONLY TESTED agreement rule in TEF
6B — Adjective Agreement with Nouns (15 seconds):
METHOD: Spot-check 3–4 adjectives in your text
  → Does each adjective agree in GENDER and NUMBER with its noun?

COMMON ERRORS:
  "les conséquences ~~négatif~~" → WRONG → "négativES" (feminine plural)
  "une mesure ~~essentiel~~" → WRONG → "essentiellE" (feminine singular)
  "des problèmes ~~croissant~~" → WRONG → "croissantS" (masculine plural)
  "cette situation ~~préoccupant~~" → WRONG → "préoccupantE" (feminine singular)

HIGH-RISK ADJECTIVES (irregular feminine forms):
  → positif → positivE
  → négatif → négativE
  → actif → activE
  → public → publiquE
  → fondamental → fondamentalE
  → essentiel → essentiellE
  → professionnel → professionnellE

QUICK CHECK METHOD:
  → For each adjective → Ask: "What noun does it describe?"
  → Check: Same gender? Same number?
  → Fix if needed → Takes 2 seconds per fix
6C — Subject-Verb Agreement (10 seconds):
METHOD: Find 2–3 sentences with PLURAL subjects
  → Is the verb conjugated in PLURAL?

COMMON ERRORS:
  "Les réseaux sociaux ~~engendre~~" → WRONG → "engendreNT" (plural subject)
  "Les conséquences ~~est~~ préoccupante" → WRONG → "SONT préoccupanteS"
  "La majorité des jeunes ~~passent~~" → Actually CORRECT (both singular and plural accepted)

HIGH-RISK PATTERNS:
  → Long subject with embedded clauses → Verb far from subject → Agreement forgotten
  "Les conséquences de l'utilisation excessive des réseaux sociaux sur la santé
   mentale des adolescents ~~est~~ préoccupante."
  → Subject = "Les conséquences" (plural) → Verb should be "SONT"
  → The distance between subject and verb caused the error

QUICK CHECK METHOD:
  → For complex sentences → Find the MAIN SUBJECT → Find the MAIN VERB
  → Do they agree in number?

🔷 SCAN 7: FINAL POLISH

Time: 30 seconds | Tier: 4 (Minor) | All Chapters

What You Check:

THIS SCAN IS OPTIONAL — only if you have time remaining

CHECKLIST:

□ VOCABULARY REPETITION: Scan for any B2 word used 3+ times
  → If found → Replace the 2nd or 3rd instance with a synonym
  → "Considérable... considérable... considérable" → Replace with "significatif" or "majeur"

□ SENTENCE OPENING VARIETY: Read first word of each sentence
  → Any 3+ consecutive sentences starting the same way?
  → If found → Restructure one sentence to start differently

□ SPELLING OF HIGH-RISK WORDS:
  → "néanmoins" (not "néamoins")
  → "développement" (two p's, one l)
  → "environnement" (two n's)
  → "conséquence" (not "conséquance")
  → "indéniable" (not "indéniabe")
  → "préjudiciable" (not "préjudiciabe")

□ ACCENT MARKS on common words:
  → "à" (preposition) vs. "a" (verb avoir)
  → "où" (where) vs. "ou" (or)
  → "là" (there) vs. "la" (article)
  → "dû" (past participle of devoir) vs. "du" (article)

□ CONCLUSION CHECK: Does the conclusion introduce any NEW argument?
  → If yes → Cross out the new argument → Replace with synthesis of existing arguments
  → The conclusion REINFORCES — it does NOT introduce (Chapter 11)

□ FINAL IMPRESSION: Read the FIRST sentence and the LAST sentence ONLY
  → Do they create a strong FRAME?
  → First sentence: Does it engage the reader?
  → Last sentence: Does it leave a lasting impression?
  → If either is weak → Strengthen the vocabulary or add an emphatic structure

🔵 WHAT COULD BE DONE

Option A: Read the Entire Text Once From Start to Finish

  • → This is what Priya did → It FAILED
  • → Your brain reads for MEANING, not for ERRORS
  • → You catch content issues but miss grammar, register, and cohesion problems
  • → NOT recommended as sole review method

Option B: Read the Text Backwards (Sentence by Sentence)

  • → Classic proofreading technique → Breaks familiarity blindness
  • → Good for catching SPELLING errors
  • → BAD for checking COHESION (you cannot check links between sentences backwards)
  • → Partially useful — only for Scan 6 and 7

Option C: Run the 7-Scan Protocol in Sequence

  • → Each scan targets ONE specific error type
  • → You NEVER read "normally" — you always read with a TARGET
  • → High-cost errors checked FIRST → Maximum point protection
  • → THIS IS THE CORRECT APPROACH

Option D: Ask Someone Else to Review

  • → Impossible during the exam
  • → Useful for PRACTICE — have someone mark your errors AFTER you self-edit
  • → Compare: What did you catch vs. What did they catch? → Identifies your blind spots

✅ WHAT SHOULD DO

1. Memorize the 7-Scan Sequence as a Physical Habit

THE SEQUENCE (memorize this ORDER):

S-C-C-R-S-A-P

S = STRUCTURAL integrity (Scan 1)
C = CONSIGNE compliance (Scan 2)
C = COHESION + Connectors (Scan 3)
R = REGISTER check (Scan 4)
S = SUBJUNCTIVE + mood (Scan 5)
A = AGREEMENT check (Scan 6)
P = POLISH (Scan 7)

MNEMONIC: "Structure, Consigne, Cohésion, Registre, Subjonctif, Accord, Polish"
  → Or create your own mnemonic
  → The ORDER matters — Tier 1 first, Tier 4 last

3. Build Your Personal "Error Profile" — Know YOUR Weak Points

AFTER EACH PRACTICE RESPONSE:

Step 1: Self-edit using the 7-Scan Protocol
Step 2: Have a teacher, tutor, or language partner review the SAME text
Step 3: Compare:
  → Errors YOU caught → ✅ (your scans work for these)
  → Errors THEY caught that you MISSED → ❌ (your blind spots)
  → Record the CATEGORY of each missed error

AFTER 5–10 PRACTICE RESPONSES:

Compile your error profile:
  → "I consistently miss past participle agreement after 'que'" → Spend extra time on Scan 6A
  → "I consistently miss the 'ne' in negations" → Spend extra time on Scan 4 Search 2
  → "I consistently repeat 'Cependant'" → Spend extra time on Scan 3A
  → "I never miss register errors" → Spend LESS time on Scan 4 → Reallocate to weaker scans

YOUR ERROR PROFILE = YOUR PERSONALIZED SCAN PRIORITY
  → Spend MORE time on scans that catch YOUR most frequent errors
  → Spend LESS time on scans that catch errors you rarely make
  → This OPTIMIZES your 3–5 minutes for MAXIMUM error detection

4. Use Physical Marking Techniques During the Exam

DURING THE SCANS — USE YOUR PEN/PENCIL AS A POINTER:

SCAN 1 (Structure):
  → Point at each paragraph break → Count them → Should be 4–5

SCAN 3 (Connectors):
  → Underline or circle the FIRST WORD of each sentence as you scan
  → Repeated words become VISIBLE when circled

SCAN 4 (Register):
  → Move your pen under EVERY negative sentence
  → Check for "ne" as your pen passes

SCAN 5 (Subjunctive):
  → Circle every "bien que," "pour que," "il faut que" you find
  → Then check the verb IMMEDIATELY after each circle

SCAN 6 (Agreement):
  → Underline every past participle you find
  → Draw an arrow back to the noun it must agree with
  → Check: Do they match in gender/number?

PHYSICAL MARKING = EXTERNAL ATTENTION ANCHOR
  → Your brain CANNOT skip what your pen is pointing at
  → This overcomes the "familiarity blindness" problem

❌ WHAT SHOULD NOT DO

1. Do NOT Read Your Text "Normally" as Your Review

❌ NORMAL READING:
  "Let me read this from beginning to end and see how it sounds..."
  → Your brain processes MEANING → Misses FORM
  → You think: "The argument makes sense" → You feel satisfied
  → Grammar errors, register violations, cohesion gaps → ALL invisible

✅ TARGETED SCANNING:
  "Let me check for [specific error type] ONLY..."
  → Your brain is SEARCHING for one specific pattern
  → Everything else is BACKGROUND → The target pattern POPS OUT
  → You find errors you would NEVER see in normal reading

2. Do NOT Fix Errors You Are Not Sure About

CRITICAL RULE: A correct sentence left alone is BETTER than a correct sentence
"fixed" into an incorrect one

IF YOU ARE UNSURE:
  → "Is 'soient' the right subjunctive of 'être' here?"
  → If UNSURE → Do NOT change it → Leave it as-is
  → OR → Change the TRIGGER to avoid the issue entirely
    → "Bien que" → "Même si" (takes indicative → no subjunctive risk)

THE RISK CALCULATION:
  → Changing a correct form to incorrect = -1 point (you created an error)
  → Leaving a possible error = -1 point (if it IS an error) OR 0 points (if it was correct)
  → Changing an incorrect form to correct = +1 point (you fixed an error)
  → ONLY change things you are CONFIDENT about

3. Do NOT Spend Review Time REWRITING Sentences

❌ REWRITING DURING REVIEW:
  "This sentence sounds awkward... let me rewrite it completely."
  → Rewriting takes 30–60 seconds PER SENTENCE
  → In 4 minutes you can rewrite 4–6 sentences → But you checked NOTHING else
  → The REST of the text goes unchecked → Major errors survive

✅ TARGETED FIXING DURING REVIEW:
  → Fix SPECIFIC errors identified by the scans
  → Change ONE WORD, not the whole sentence
  → Cross out → Write above → Move on
  → 5–10 seconds per fix → 4 minutes = 24–48 fixes possible
  → FAR more efficient than rewriting

4. Do NOT Check Spelling Before Checking Structure

THE TEMPTATION:
  → You see a misspelled word → You fix it → You feel productive
  → But you spent 30 seconds on a TIER 4 error
  → Meanwhile, your conclusion introduces a NEW argument (TIER 2 error)
  → The TIER 2 error costs 5x more points than the spelling error

THE RULE:
  → ALWAYS follow the scan ORDER: 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 6 → 7
  → Tier 1 errors FIRST → Tier 4 errors LAST
  → If time runs out after Scan 4 → You still protected yourself from the WORST errors
  → Spelling can wait → Structure cannot

5. Do NOT Use the Protocol as an Excuse to Write Carelessly

WRONG MINDSET:
  "I'll write fast and messy → The protocol will catch everything"
  → The protocol catches 70–90% of errors → NOT 100%
  → Errors that are NOT caught still cost points
  → The protocol is a SAFETY NET, not a REPLACEMENT for careful writing

CORRECT MINDSET:
  "I'll write carefully using all my chapter tools → Then the protocol
   catches the few errors that slipped through despite my best effort"
  → Careful writing + Protocol = Maximum error elimination
  → Careless writing + Protocol = Moderate error elimination (insufficient)

🕳️ COMMON PITFALLS AND FAILURE MODES

Pitfall 1: The "I Don't Have Time" Excuse

  • Candidates who spend ALL their time writing and leave 0 minutes for review
  • → They lose 20–40 points from preventable errors
  • → They would have gained MORE by stopping writing 3 minutes earlier and running the protocol
  • Solution: Set a HARD STOP at minute 30 → Protocol begins at minute 30 regardless
  • → Refer to Chapter 26 (time management) for detailed allocation

Pitfall 2: The Perfectionist Trap

  • Candidate finds an error in Scan 2 → Spends 2 minutes rewriting the entire introduction
  • → Scans 3–7 never run → Major errors in body and conclusion survive
  • Solution: Fix each error in 5–10 seconds MAXIMUM → Move to next scan → No rewriting

Pitfall 3: The Wrong Scan Order

  • Candidate starts with Scan 7 (spelling) because it feels "easiest"
  • → Spends 2 minutes on spelling → Runs out of time → Missing conclusion never detected
  • Solution: ALWAYS follow the 1-2-3-4-5-6-7 sequence → Tier 1 first, Tier 4 last

Pitfall 4: The False Positive Fix

  • Candidate "fixes" something that was already correct
  • → Creates a NEW error where none existed
  • → Net result: Score DECREASES
  • Solution: If unsure → Leave it → Only fix errors you are CONFIDENT about

Pitfall 5: The Incomplete Scan

  • Running Scan 3 but only checking connector repetition → Missing cohesion gaps
  • → Each scan has SUB-CHECKS → All sub-checks must be completed
  • Solution: Follow the FULL checklist for each scan → Do not skip sub-checks

Pitfall 6: The Protocol Abandonment

  • Running Scans 1–3 → Finding few errors → Thinking "the rest is probably fine" → Stopping
  • → Scans 5–6 (subjunctive + agreement) catch DIFFERENT error types
  • → Finding few errors in Scans 1–3 does NOT predict few errors in Scans 5–6
  • Solution: Run ALL scans you have time for → Each scan is INDEPENDENT

Pitfall 7: The Messy Correction

  • Crossing out words and writing fixes in illegible handwriting
  • → Evaluator cannot read the correction → It counts as an error, not a fix
  • Solution:
    • → Cross out NEATLY with a single horizontal line (not scribbles)
    • → Write the correction CLEARLY above the crossed-out word
    • → Use a caret (^) to insert missing words
    • → Keep corrections MINIMAL — one word changes, not paragraph rewrites
