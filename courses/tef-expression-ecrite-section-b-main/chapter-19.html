📘 CHAPTER 19

INVISIBLE GLUE: MAKING EACH SENTENCE PULL THE READER INTO THE NEXT

🔴 HYPOTHETICAL PROBLEM

You are a TEF candidate named Dmitri
Dmitri has completed Chapters 1–18 thoroughly
He understands the scoring grid (Chapter 1), builds causal chains (Chapter 15), uses concession (Chapter 16), varies sentence structures across all 7 types (Chapter 17), and deploys B2 vocabulary from theme-specific banks (Chapter 18)
He writes a practice response to this prompt:

"Les transports en commun devraient-ils être gratuits pour tous? Exprimez et justifiez votre point de vue."

Dmitri's body paragraph:

"Les transports en commun jouent un rôle fondamental dans la vie urbaine. La pollution atmosphérique constitue un enjeu préoccupant dans les grandes métropoles. De nombreux citoyens ne peuvent pas se permettre d'acheter une voiture. Les embouteillages engendrent une perte de productivité considérable pour les entreprises. Rendre les transports gratuits favoriserait la mobilité durable. Les gouvernements doivent élaborer des politiques ambitieuses en matière de transport."

His vocabulary → rich and precise (Chapter 18 applied successfully)
His sentence structures → varied (Type 1, Type 3, Type 5 visible)
His B2 words → well deployed: "enjeu préoccupant," "engendrent," "mobilité durable," "politiques ambitieuses"
But his evaluator writes: "Idées pertinentes mais mal reliées. Le texte manque de fluidité. Les phrases semblent juxtaposées, pas enchaînées. Cohésion insuffisante."
Translation: "Relevant ideas but poorly connected. The text lacks fluidity. Sentences seem placed side by side, not chained. Insufficient cohesion."
Dmitri scored WELL on Dimensions 3 and 4 but only ADEQUATE on Dimension 2
He is frustrated — every sentence is individually excellent, but TOGETHER they feel disconnected

❓ THE CORE QUESTION

  • Why do individually excellent sentences still produce a disconnected paragraph?
  • What is the difference between COHERENCE (logical order of ideas) and COHESION (linguistic linking between sentences)?
  • What specific tools create the invisible glue BETWEEN sentences?
  • How do you make each sentence PULL the reader into the next one automatically?
  • What is "thematic progression" and why does it matter more than connectors alone?

🟡 HOW TO APPROACH IT

Step 1: Diagnose Dmitri's Actual Problem

  • Read Dmitri's six sentences and map their TOPICS:
Sentence #TopicConnection to Previous
1Role of public transport— (opening)
2Air pollution❌ NEW topic — no link to sentence 1
3Citizens who cannot afford cars❌ NEW topic — no link to sentence 2
4Traffic jams and productivity loss❌ NEW topic — no link to sentence 3
5Free transport and sustainable mobility❌ NEW topic — no link to sentence 4
6Government policies❌ NEW topic — no link to sentence 5
  • Every sentence introduces a COMPLETELY NEW topic
  • No sentence REFERS BACK to the previous one
  • No sentence PREPARES the next one
  • The paragraph is a STACK of isolated ideas, not a CHAIN of connected thoughts

Step 2: Understand the Difference Between Coherence and Cohesion

COHERENCE (already partially addressed in Chapters 5, 7, 10, 11, 14):
  → The LOGICAL ORDER of ideas
  → Does the text make sense as a whole?
  → Is there a clear progression from introduction to conclusion?
  → Are arguments organized meaningfully?
  → Coherence = THE MAP of your response

COHESION (THIS CHAPTER):
  → The LINGUISTIC LINKS between sentences
  → Does each sentence connect to the one before it?
  → Are there words/phrases that CREATE continuity?
  → Does the reader feel PULLED forward through the text?
  → Cohesion = THE ROADS connecting points on the map
  • You can have coherence WITHOUT cohesion → Dmitri's problem
    • → His ideas are logically relevant but linguistically disconnected
  • You can have cohesion WITHOUT coherence → Rare but possible
    • → Sentences link beautifully but the overall argument makes no sense
  • You need BOTH for a high Dimension 2 score

Step 3: Identify the 6 Cohesion Tools

  • French writing achieves cohesion through 6 specific mechanisms:
TOOL 1: Pronoun Reference (replacing nouns with pronouns that point backward)
TOOL 2: Lexical Repetition and Synonym Substitution (repeating or varying key terms)
TOOL 3: Demonstrative Reference (ce, cet, cette, ces, cela, celui-ci)
TOOL 4: Thematic Progression (how the topic flows from sentence to sentence)
TOOL 5: Logical Connectors (already learned in Chapters 7, 13 — NOW used for MICRO-linking)
TOOL 6: Information Packaging (given information first, new information second)
  • Chapters 7 and 13 taught Tool 5 (connectors) extensively
  • Chapter 15 introduced Tool 1 partially (the use of "Cela")
  • Chapter 19 now teaches ALL 6 tools as an integrated cohesion system

🟢 HOW CAN WE SOLVE IT

THE COMPLETE COHESION SYSTEM — 6 TOOLS

🔷 TOOL 1: PRONOUN REFERENCE

Replacing a noun from the previous sentence with a pronoun that POINTS BACK to it

1A → Subject Pronouns as Backward Pointers

Noun in Sentence 1Pronoun in Sentence 2Example
Les transports en communIlsLes transports en commun jouent un rôle fondamental. Ils permettent de réduire la congestion urbaine.
La gratuité des transportsElleLa gratuité des transports suscite un vif débat. Elle présente des avantages mais aussi des limites.
Les citoyensIlsDe nombreux citoyens dépendent des transports publics. Ils seraient les premiers bénéficiaires de cette mesure.

Critical Rule — Pronoun Clarity:

THE PRONOUN MUST HAVE ONE CLEAR REFERENT

❌ AMBIGUOUS:
  "Les gouvernements consultent les citoyens. Ils estiment que les transports
   devraient être gratuits."
  → WHO are "ils"? The governments or the citizens?
  → The reader GUESSES → Cohesion BREAKS

✅ CLEAR:
  "Les gouvernements consultent les citoyens. Ces derniers estiment que les
   transports devraient être gratuits."
  → "Ces derniers" = clearly the citizens (the LAST noun mentioned)
  → The reader KNOWS → Cohesion HOLDS

1B → "Y" and "EN" as Backward Reference Pronouns

PronounReplacesExample
Yà + noun / dans + noun / location referenceLes grandes villes souffrent de congestion. Les habitants y passent des heures chaque jour dans les embouteillages.
ENde + noun / quantity referenceLa pollution est un problème majeur. Les conséquences en sont multiples et préoccupantes.

Why "Y" and "EN" Are Cohesion Power Tools:

  • They create an INVISIBLE link between sentences
  • The reader does not consciously notice them but FEELS the connection
  • They eliminate repetition (you do not need to restate the full noun)
  • They signal B2 grammatical mastery to the evaluator (Dimension 4 bonus)

1C → "CELA / CECI / CE" as Idea Reference Pronouns

Already introduced in Chapter 15 for causal chains — now expanded as a full cohesion tool

PronounWhat It ReplacesExample
CelaThe ENTIRE previous idea/sentenceLes transports gratuits réduiraient la pollution. Cela contribuerait significativement à l'amélioration de la qualité de l'air.
CeciThe idea that FOLLOWS (forward reference — rare)Ceci mérite d'être souligné : la gratuité des transports profiterait à tous.
Ce qui / Ce queThe previous idea as subject/object of a relative clauseLes embouteillages coûtent des milliards. Ce qui pèse lourdement sur l'économie nationale.
Ce dontThe previous idea when "de" is requiredLa mobilité durable est un objectif essentiel. Ce dont dépend la qualité de vie urbaine.

The "Cela" Chain Technique (Chapter 15 Reinforcement + Expansion):

CHAPTER 15 TAUGHT:
  "Cela entraîne... / Cela provoque... / Cela engendre..."
  → "Cela" links a cause to its effect

CHAPTER 19 EXPANDS:
  "Cela" can also link to:
  → Cela signifie que... (This means that...)
  → Cela implique... (This implies...)
  → Cela soulève la question de... (This raises the question of...)
  → Cela confirme l'idée selon laquelle... (This confirms the idea that...)
  → Cela témoigne de... (This bears witness to...)
  → Cela met en lumière... (This highlights...)

EACH of these "Cela + verb" structures:
  1. REFERS BACK to the previous sentence (cohesion)
  2. INTRODUCES a new angle or consequence (progression)
  3. Uses a B2 verb (Chapter 18 vocabulary)
  → Triple benefit from a single technique

🔶 TOOL 2: LEXICAL REPETITION AND SYNONYM SUBSTITUTION

Repeating key terms OR replacing them with synonyms to maintain thematic continuity

2A → Strategic Repetition of Key Terms

  • In Chapter 18, you learned NOT to repeat the same word
  • EXCEPTION: The CENTRAL THEME WORD of your response should appear 2–3 times
  • This anchors the reader and reminds them what the text is about
CENTRAL THEME WORD for Dmitri's prompt: "transports en commun"

STRATEGIC REPETITION:
  Sentence 1: "Les transports en commun jouent un rôle fondamental..."
  Sentence 5: "Rendre les transports en commun gratuits favoriserait..."
  Sentence 9: "Les transports en commun constituent donc un investissement..."

  → The theme word appears 3 times across 9 sentences
  → NOT every sentence → Strategic placement at beginning, middle, end
  → The reader always knows what the text is ABOUT

2B → Synonym Substitution for Non-Central Terms

  • For words that are NOT your central theme word, use SYNONYMS to avoid repetition while maintaining cohesion
First MentionSynonym SubstitutionThird Option
les transports en communles transports publics / collectifsce mode de déplacement
les citoyensles usagers / les habitants / la populationles individus concernés
la pollutionla contamination / la dégradation de l'airce fléau environnemental
les embouteillagesla congestion routière / les bouchonsce phénomène
la gratuitécette mesure / cette initiative / ce dispositifcette proposition
les gouvernementsles pouvoirs publics / les autorités / les décideursles élus

The Synonym Chain Technique:

Sentence 1: "La gratuité des transports en commun..."
Sentence 3: "Cette mesure permettrait de..."
Sentence 5: "Ce dispositif, bien qu'il soit coûteux,..."
Sentence 7: "Une telle initiative exigerait..."

→ Same concept (free transport) → 4 DIFFERENT expressions
→ The reader understands all four refer to the SAME idea
→ Vocabulary variety (Dimension 3 ↑) + Cohesion (Dimension 2 ↑) simultaneously

2C → The "Hypernym" Technique (General Category Word)

  • A hypernym is a GENERAL word that encompasses a more specific word
  • Using a hypernym after a specific term creates natural cohesion
Specific Term (First Mention)Hypernym (Subsequent Reference)
le télétravailce mode de travail / cette pratique
les réseaux sociauxces plateformes / ces outils numériques
le réchauffement climatiquece phénomène / cette menace

la lecture de livrescette activité / cette pratique intellectuelle
la gratuité des transportscette mesure / cette politique publique

Example in Context:

"Le télétravail s'est considérablement développé ces dernières années.
 Ce mode de travail offre une flexibilité appréciable, mais cette pratique
 peut également engendrer un sentiment d'isolement."

→ "télétravail" → "ce mode de travail" → "cette pratique"
→ THREE different expressions for the SAME concept in THREE sentences
→ Cohesion is SEAMLESS — the reader never loses track of the topic

🔷 TOOL 3: DEMONSTRATIVE REFERENCE

Using demonstrative adjectives and pronouns to point back to previously mentioned elements

3A → Demonstrative Adjectives (ce, cet, cette, ces)

AdjectiveGender/NumberExample
ceMasculine singular (before consonant)Ce problème mérite une attention particulière.
cetMasculine singular (before vowel/h)Cet enjeu concerne l'ensemble de la population.
cetteFeminine singularCette mesure pourrait transformer la mobilité urbaine.
cesPlural (both genders)Ces arguments démontrent la nécessité d'agir.

How Demonstrative Adjectives Create Cohesion:

WITHOUT DEMONSTRATIVE:
  "La pollution est un problème. Un problème nécessite des solutions urgentes."
  → "Un problème" → INDEFINITE → Could be ANY problem → Weak link

WITH DEMONSTRATIVE:
  "La pollution est un problème. Ce problème nécessite des solutions urgentes."
  → "Ce problème" → DEFINITE → Points back to THE SPECIFIC problem mentioned → Strong link

3B → Demonstrative Pronouns (celui-ci, celle-ci, ceux-ci, celles-ci / celui-là, celle-là)

PronounReplacesExample
celui-ci / celle-ciThe MOST RECENTLY mentioned nounComparons le bus et le métro. Celui-ci est souvent plus rapide en zone urbaine. (= le métro)
celui-là / celle-làThe FIRST mentioned noun (farther back)Comparons le bus et le métro. Celui-là dessert davantage de zones rurales. (= le bus)
ces derniers / ces dernièresThe LAST mentioned groupLes usagers et les autorités ont des avis divergents. Ces dernières privilégient l'aspect financier. (= les autorités)

When to Use "Ces Derniers" vs. "Ils":

USE "ILS" when there is ONLY ONE possible referent:
  "Les citoyens dépendent des transports. Ils méritent un service de qualité."
  → Only one group mentioned → "Ils" is clear

USE "CES DERNIERS" when there are TWO OR MORE possible referents:
  "Les citoyens interpellent les élus. Ces derniers promettent des améliorations."
  → Two groups mentioned → "Ces derniers" = the SECOND group (les élus)
  → "Ils" would be AMBIGUOUS here

🔶 TOOL 4: THEMATIC PROGRESSION

This is the MOST IMPORTANT cohesion tool — and the one most candidates have never heard of
It determines HOW information flows from one sentence to the next

Understanding Thematic Progression

  • Every sentence has TWO parts:
    • THEME (what the sentence is ABOUT — usually at the beginning)
    • RHEME (what is SAID about the theme — the new information)
SENTENCE: "Les transports en commun [THEME] | jouent un rôle fondamental [RHEME]"
  • The way THEMES and RHEMES connect across sentences determines whether the paragraph FLOWS or STUTTERS

4A → Linear Progression (The Rheme Becomes the Next Theme)

The new information (rheme) of sentence 1 becomes the topic (theme) of sentence 2

PATTERN:
  Sentence 1: Theme A → Rheme B
  Sentence 2: Theme B (= previous Rheme) → Rheme C
  Sentence 3: Theme C (= previous Rheme) → Rheme D

EXAMPLE:
  "Les transports en commun [A] réduisent la pollution atmosphérique [B].
   Cette réduction de la pollution [B] améliore la qualité de vie urbaine [C].
   Une meilleure qualité de vie [C] attire davantage d'investissements dans les villes [D]."

VISUAL FLOW:
  A → B
       B → C
            C → D

→ Each sentence PICKS UP where the previous one LEFT OFF
→ The reader is PULLED forward automatically
→ This is the SAME principle as causal chains (Chapter 15) but applied to ANY content

Why Linear Progression Is the Most Powerful Cohesion Pattern:

  • It creates an unbreakable forward momentum
  • The reader CANNOT lose track because each sentence begins with something already known
  • It naturally produces the causal chains taught in Chapter 15
  • It is the default progression pattern for body paragraphs in TEF Section B

4B → Constant Progression (The Same Theme Repeats Across Sentences)

Every sentence starts with the SAME theme but adds new information

PATTERN:
  Sentence 1: Theme A → Rheme 1
  Sentence 2: Theme A → Rheme 2
  Sentence 3: Theme A → Rheme 3

EXAMPLE:
  "Les transports gratuits [A] réduiraient la pollution [1].
   Ils [A] favoriseraient également l'inclusion sociale [2].
   Ils [A] contribueraient en outre à la dynamisation économique des quartiers périphériques [3]."

VISUAL FLOW:
  A → 1
  A → 2
  A → 3

→ One subject, multiple attributes/consequences
→ Useful for LISTING multiple arguments about the same topic
→ Works well for the FIRST 2–3 sentences of a paragraph

Limitation of Constant Progression:

  • If used for MORE than 3 sentences → Becomes monotonous (Karim's problem from Chapter 17)
  • Solution → Use constant progression for 2–3 sentences → Then switch to linear progression
  • This COMBINATION creates the ideal paragraph flow

4C → Derived Progression (Multiple Themes Branch From One Super-Theme)

A general topic is introduced → Then different ASPECTS of that topic are explored in separate sentences

PATTERN:
  Sentence 1: Super-Theme X introduced
  Sentence 2: Aspect A of X → Rheme 1
  Sentence 3: Aspect B of X → Rheme 2
  Sentence 4: Aspect C of X → Rheme 3

EXAMPLE:
  "La gratuité des transports aurait des répercussions multiples [X].
   Sur le plan environnemental [A], elle réduirait les émissions polluantes [1].
   Sur le plan social [B], elle favoriserait la mobilité des plus démunis [2].
   Sur le plan économique [C], elle dynamiserait les échanges commerciaux [3]."

VISUAL FLOW:
       X
      /|\
     A B C
     |  |  |
     1  2  3

→ One super-theme BRANCHES into sub-themes
→ Very useful for presenting MULTIPLE DIMENSIONS of an argument
→ Creates a sense of thoroughness and organized thinking

When to Use Each Progression Type:

Progression TypeBest Used ForWhere in Response
LinearCausal chains, building one idea from the previousBody paragraphs — argument development
ConstantListing multiple features/consequences of one subjectBody paragraphs — opening 2–3 sentences
DerivedExploring multiple dimensions of a complex topicBody paragraphs — structured multi-angle analysis

🔷 TOOL 5: LOGICAL CONNECTORS AS MICRO-LINKERS

Chapters 7 and 13 taught connectors for PARAGRAPH-LEVEL organization
Chapter 19 now teaches connectors for SENTENCE-LEVEL micro-linking

The Difference Between Macro-Connectors and Micro-Connectors

MACRO-CONNECTORS (Chapters 7, 13):
  → Link PARAGRAPHS or major argument blocks
  → "D'une part... D'autre part..." / "En revanche..." / "En conclusion..."
  → Structural markers the reader can SEE

MICRO-CONNECTORS (Chapter 19):
  → Link INDIVIDUAL SENTENCES within a paragraph
  → They are SMALLER, LIGHTER, and more FREQUENT
  → They create FLOW rather than STRUCTURE

The Micro-Connector Toolkit:

Micro-ConnectorFunctionExample
En effetConfirms/explains the previous sentenceLes transports gratuits seraient bénéfiques. En effet, ils réduiraient la pollution.
D'ailleursAdds a supporting detailCette mesure est populaire. D'ailleurs, plusieurs villes l'ont déjà adoptée.
De surcroîtAdds an additional point (formal)La gratuité réduirait la pollution. De surcroît, elle favoriserait l'inclusion sociale.
En d'autres termesReformulates/clarifies the previous sentenceCe dispositif profiterait à tous. En d'autres termes, personne ne serait exclu du réseau de transport.
Plus précisémentNarrows down the previous general statementLes transports gratuits auraient un impact positif. Plus précisément, ils permettraient aux ménages modestes d'économiser considérablement.
Autrement ditRestates in different wordsLa mobilité serait accessible à tous. Autrement dit, le transport ne serait plus un privilège mais un droit.
À cet égardConnects to the same topic from a new angleLa question du financement se pose. À cet égard, certains économistes proposent des solutions innovantes.
Dès lorsDraws an immediate logical consequenceLa pollution diminuerait. Dès lors, la qualité de vie urbaine s'améliorerait.
Il s'ensuit queStates what logically followsLes citoyens se déplaceraient davantage. Il s'ensuit que l'activité économique serait stimulée.
Rappelons queRefers back to a previously stated pointCette mesure est coûteuse. Rappelons que les bénéfices à long terme dépassent l'investissement initial.

Micro-Connector Placement Rule:

MAXIMUM: 1 micro-connector every 2–3 sentences
NOT every sentence → That feels over-connected and heavy
NOT every 4–5 sentences → That feels disconnected (Dmitri's problem)

IDEAL RHYTHM:
  Sentence 1: No connector (opening statement)
  Sentence 2: Micro-connector ("En effet,...")
  Sentence 3: No connector (flows naturally via pronoun/synonym)
  Sentence 4: Micro-connector ("De surcroît,...")
  Sentence 5: No connector (flows via thematic progression)
  Sentence 6: Micro-connector or conclusion connector ("Dès lors,...")

🔶 TOOL 6: INFORMATION PACKAGING (Given → New)

Every sentence should begin with KNOWN information and end with NEW information

The Given-New Contract

PRINCIPLE:
  The BEGINNING of each sentence should contain information
  the reader ALREADY KNOWS (from the previous sentence)
  
  The END of each sentence should contain NEW information
  that advances the argument

THIS IS WHY:
  → The reader feels grounded at the start of each sentence (familiar territory)
  → The reader receives new information at the end (forward movement)
  → The transition from known to new is SMOOTH, not jarring

Dmitri's Original — Violating the Given-New Contract:

Sentence 1: "Les transports en commun jouent un rôle fondamental." [NEW + NEW]
Sentence 2: "La pollution atmosphérique constitue un enjeu préoccupant." [NEW + NEW]
Sentence 3: "De nombreux citoyens ne peuvent pas acheter une voiture." [NEW + NEW]

→ Every sentence starts with NEW information
→ The reader has to RESET their mental context every time
→ No sentence REFERS BACK to the previous one
→ This is WHY Dmitri's paragraph feels disconnected

Dmitri's Paragraph — Respecting the Given-New Contract:

Sentence 1: "Les transports en commun jouent un rôle fondamental
             dans la vie urbaine." [Theme established]

Sentence 2: "En effet, ces derniers permettent de réduire
             la congestion routière et la pollution atmosphérique." [GIVEN: ces derniers = transports | NEW: pollution]

Sentence 3: "Cette pollution, qui affecte la santé des citadins,
             représente un enjeu de santé publique majeur." [GIVEN: cette pollution | NEW: santé publique]

Sentence 4: "Dès lors, rendre les transports gratuits contribuerait
             à améliorer la qualité de l'air tout en favorisant
             l'inclusion sociale." [GIVEN: transports + health angle | NEW: free transport solution]

Sentence 5: "Une telle mesure bénéficierait en particulier aux ménages
             modestes, qui consacrent une part importante de leur budget
             aux déplacements quotidiens." [GIVEN: cette mesure | NEW: budget impact]

→ Each sentence BEGINS with something from the previous sentence
→ Each sentence ENDS with something new
→ The reader glides forward without effort
→ THIS is what cohesion FEELS like

🔵 WHAT COULD BE DONE

Option A: Add More Connectors Between Every Sentence

  • → Addresses only Tool 5 (micro-connectors)
  • → Ignores Tools 1–4 and 6
  • → Creates an over-connected text that feels mechanical
  • → Partially effective but insufficient

Option B: Use Only Pronoun Reference to Link Sentences

  • → Addresses only Tool 1
  • → Creates repetitive "Il... Elle... Ils... Cela..." patterns
  • → Monotonous pronoun chains
  • → Partially effective but insufficient

Option C: Combine ALL 6 Tools Strategically Across the Paragraph

  • → Different tools for different sentence transitions
  • → Maximum variety of cohesion mechanisms
  • → Natural, flowing text
  • → THIS IS THE CORRECT APPROACH

✅ WHAT SHOULD DO

1. Apply the "Backward Glance" Test to Every Sentence

  • After writing each sentence, ask yourself:
    • → "Does this sentence contain AT LEAST ONE element that refers back to the previous sentence?"
    • → If YES → Move to the next sentence
    • → If NO → Add a backward reference before continuing

The 6 Ways to Create a Backward Glance:

ToolBackward Glance MethodExample Phrase
Tool 1 (Pronoun)Replace a noun with a pronounIls... / Elle... / Cela...
Tool 2 (Synonym)Replace a noun with a synonymCette mesure... / Ce dispositif... / Cette initiative...
Tool 3 (Demonstrative)Use a demonstrative adjectiveCe phénomène... / Cette tendance... / Ces résultats...
Tool 4 (Theme pickup)Start with the rheme of the previous sentenceCette pollution [= previous rheme] affecte...
Tool 5 (Micro-connector)Use a connector that inherently refers backEn effet... / Dès lors... / C'est pourquoi...
Tool 6 (Given-New)Place known information at the sentence startBegin with something already mentioned

2. Use the "Cohesion Sandwich" for Every Paragraph

THE COHESION SANDWICH:

SENTENCE 1 (OPENING):
  → Introduce the paragraph's main claim
  → No backward glance needed (this IS the new topic)
  → Use a strong Chapter 17 sentence structure

SENTENCES 2–4 (FILLING):
  → Each sentence MUST have a backward glance to the previous one
  → Alternate between different cohesion tools:
    Sentence 2: Pronoun reference ("Cela..." / "En effet,...")
    Sentence 3: Synonym substitution ("Cette mesure..." / "Ce phénomène...")
    Sentence 4: Thematic progression (linear — pick up the rheme)
  → Each sentence ALSO adds new information (forward movement)

SENTENCE 5 (CLOSING):
  → Summarize or conclude the paragraph's argument
  → Use a consequence connector ("C'est pourquoi..." / "Dès lors...")
  → This sentence should point FORWARD to the next paragraph or to the conclusion

3. Master the "Cela + Verb" Cohesion System

  • Chapter 15 introduced "Cela entraîne / Cela provoque" for causal chains
  • Chapter 19 now expands this into a FULL cohesion system:

The Complete "Cela + Verb" Reference System:

Function"Cela + Verb" StructureWhen to Use
Cause → EffectCela entraîne / provoque / engendreWhen the previous sentence is a CAUSE and you state its EFFECT
Idea → ExplanationCela signifie que / Cela implique queWhen you EXPLAIN what the previous idea means
Idea → EvidenceCela se manifeste par / Cela se traduit parWhen you show HOW the previous idea appears in reality
Idea → ConfirmationCela confirme que / Cela démontre queWhen the previous sentence SUPPORTS a claim you made earlier
Idea → QuestionCela soulève la question de / Cela amène à s'interroger surWhen the previous sentence opens a NEW angle of inquiry
Idea → HighlightingCela met en lumière / Cela met en évidenceWhen you want to EMPHASIZE what the previous sentence revealed
Idea → Broader scopeCela témoigne de / Cela reflèteWhen the previous sentence illustrates a LARGER trend
Idea → NecessityCela nécessite / Cela exigeWhen the previous sentence creates a NEED for action
Idea → PossibilityCela permettrait de / Cela pourraitWhen the previous sentence opens a POTENTIAL outcome

Example — "Cela" System in a Full Paragraph:

Les transports en commun gratuits réduiraient significativement le nombre de véhicules sur les routes. Cela se traduirait par une diminution notable des émissions de gaz à effet de serre. Cela confirme l'idée selon laquelle la mobilité durable constitue un levier essentiel dans la lutte contre le changement climatique. Cela nécessite toutefois un investissement considérable de la part des pouvoirs publics.

  • 4 sentences → 3 uses of "Cela" → Each with a DIFFERENT verb → Seamless chain

WARNING — "Cela" Overuse:

MAXIMUM: 2–3 "Cela" references per paragraph
If you use "Cela" in EVERY sentence → Monotonous
ALTERNATE "Cela" with other cohesion tools (synonyms, demonstratives, pronouns)

GOOD PATTERN:
  Sentence 1: [Statement]
  Sentence 2: "Cela entraîne..."  (Tool 1 — pronoun)
  Sentence 3: "Cette tendance..." (Tool 3 — demonstrative)
  Sentence 4: "Dès lors,..."      (Tool 5 — micro-connector)
  Sentence 5: "Cela met en lumière..." (Tool 1 — pronoun)
  → Only 2 "Cela" in 5 sentences → Balanced

4. Use "Recall Phrases" to Refer Back to Earlier Arguments

  • Sometimes you need to refer back to something you said 3–4 sentences ago, not just the previous sentence
  • "Recall phrases" let you REACH BACK across multiple sentences:
Recall PhraseMeaningExample
Comme nous l'avons mentionnéAs we mentionedComme nous l'avons mentionné, la pollution constitue un enjeu majeur.
Ainsi que nous l'avons évoquéAs we discussedAinsi que nous l'avons évoqué, les transports gratuits présentent des avantages considérables.

When to Use Recall Phrases:

  • In the conclusion → To refer back to body paragraph arguments
  • In the antithèse → To contrast with a point made in the thèse
  • In the transition between paragraphs → To bridge from one argument block to the next
  • Do NOT use recall phrases within a SINGLE paragraph → They are for CROSS-PARAGRAPH cohesion

5. Combine All 6 Tools in Dmitri's Rewritten Paragraph

Dmitri's COMPLETE Rewrite:

Les transports en commun jouent un rôle fondamental dans la vie urbaine contemporaine. [Opening — Theme established]
En effet, ces derniers permettent de réduire non seulement la congestion routière mais aussi la pollution atmosphérique. [Tool 5: micro-connector "En effet" + Tool 3: demonstrative "ces derniers"]
Cette pollution, qui affecte la santé de millions de citadins, représente un enjeu de santé publique majeur. [Tool 4: linear progression (pollution = rheme of S2 → theme of S3) + Tool 2: synonym "citadins" for "habitants"]
Cela met en lumière la nécessité d'investir massivement dans les transports collectifs. [Tool 1: "Cela" pronoun reference + Tool 2: synonym "transports collectifs"]
Dès lors, rendre ces services gratuits constituerait une mesure à la fois écologique et sociale, dont les bénéfices dépasseraient largement le coût initial. [Tool 5: micro-connector "Dès lors" + Tool 3: demonstrative "ces services" + Tool 6: Given-New structure]

Cohesion Analysis:

Sentence TransitionTool(s) UsedBackward Glance Element
S1 → S2Tool 5 + Tool 3"En effet" + "ces derniers"
S2 → S3Tool 4 + Tool 2"Cette pollution" (linear progression)
S3 → S4Tool 1"Cela" (refers to entire previous idea)
S4 → S5Tool 5 + Tool 3 + Tool 6"Dès lors" + "ces services" + given-new packaging
  • 5 sentences → Every transition uses a DIFFERENT cohesion combination
  • Zero repetition of the same linking mechanism
  • The paragraph flows like a RIVER, not like a CHECKLIST

❌ WHAT SHOULD NOT DO

1. Do NOT Start Every Sentence With a Connector

❌ OVER-CONNECTED:
  "En effet, les transports sont importants. De plus, ils réduisent la pollution.
   Par ailleurs, les citoyens en bénéficient. En outre, l'économie s'améliore.
   Par conséquent, cette mesure est nécessaire."
  → 5 sentences → 5 connectors → Mechanical and exhausting
  → The connectors become NOISE instead of GLUE

✅ BALANCED:
  "Les transports en commun jouent un rôle fondamental. En effet, ces derniers
   réduisent la pollution atmosphérique. Cette réduction bénéficie directement
   aux citadins, dont la qualité de vie s'améliore. Cela stimule par ailleurs
   l'économie locale. Dès lors, investir dans les transports constitue une priorité."
  → 5 sentences → 2 connectors + 3 other cohesion tools → Natural flow

2. Do NOT Use "Cela" When the Referent Is Unclear

❌ AMBIGUOUS "CELA":
  "Les transports en commun réduisent la pollution et favorisent l'inclusion sociale.
   Cela est important."
  → WHAT is "Cela"? The pollution reduction? The inclusion? Both?
  → The reader must GUESS → Cohesion breaks

✅ CLEAR "CELA":
  "Les transports en commun réduisent la pollution. Cela contribue directement
   à l'amélioration de la qualité de l'air."
  → "Cela" = clearly the pollution reduction → One referent → Clear

ALTERNATIVE WHEN MULTIPLE REFERENTS EXIST:
  "Les transports en commun réduisent la pollution et favorisent l'inclusion sociale.
   Ces deux bénéfices justifient un investissement massif."
  → "Ces deux bénéfices" = explicit reference to BOTH ideas → Crystal clear

3. Do NOT Repeat the Same Synonym Chain Pattern Across Paragraphs

❌ REPETITIVE PATTERN:
  Paragraph 1: "Les transports en commun... Ce mode de déplacement... Cette mesure..."
  Paragraph 2: "La pollution... Ce phénomène... Cette problématique..."
  → Same pattern (specific → hypernym → demonstrative) in BOTH paragraphs
  → The reader senses a mechanical template

✅ VARIED PATTERNS:
  Paragraph 1: "Les transports en commun... Ils... Ces derniers... Ce dispositif..."
  Paragraph 2: "La pollution atmosphérique... Cela... Cette menace environnementale..."
  → Different cohesion tools in each paragraph → Feels natural

4. Do NOT Confuse "En effet" With "En fait"

ExpressionMeaningFunction
En effetIndeed / In fact (CONFIRMS previous statement)COHESION — supports what was just said
En faitActually / In reality (CORRECTS or CONTRASTS)OPPOSITION — contradicts what was expected
❌ WRONG:
  "Les transports gratuits seraient bénéfiques. En fait, ils réduiraient la pollution."
  → "En fait" suggests CONTRADICTION → But the second sentence SUPPORTS the first
  → Logical MISMATCH → Cohesion BREAKS

✅ CORRECT:
  "Les transports gratuits seraient bénéfiques. En effet, ils réduiraient la pollution."
  → "En effet" CONFIRMS → Second sentence supports the first → Cohesion HOLDS

5. Do NOT Create "Pronoun Chains" That Go On Too Long

❌ EXCESSIVE PRONOUN CHAIN:
  "Les transports en commun sont essentiels. Ils réduisent la pollution.
   Ils favorisent l'inclusion. Ils dynamisent l'économie. Ils méritent
   un investissement."
  → 5 sentences → 4 start with "Ils" → Karim's problem from Chapter 17 returns
  → Cohesion through pronouns ONLY = monotony

✅ VARIED COHESION:
  "Les transports en commun sont essentiels. En effet, ils réduisent la pollution.
   Ce mode de déplacement favorise également l'inclusion sociale. Cela dynamise
   par ailleurs l'économie locale. Dès lors, ces services méritent un investissement
   considérable."
  → 5 sentences → 5 DIFFERENT cohesion tools → Rich and natural

6. Do NOT Use Recall Phrases Within a Single Paragraph

  • "Comme nous l'avons mentionné" in the SAME paragraph where you mentioned it → Redundant
  • The reader just READ it 2 sentences ago — they do not need to be reminded
  • Recall phrases are for CROSS-PARAGRAPH references ONLY
  • Within a paragraph → Use Tools 1–5 instead

🚫 WHEN NOT TO USE

When NOT to Force Cohesion Tools

  • In the very first sentence of the Introduction

    • → There is nothing to refer back to
    • → Start fresh with a contextualizing statement (Chapter 10)
    • → Cohesion begins from sentence 2 onward

  • When two sentences are naturally connected by content alone

    • → Sometimes the content itself creates an obvious link
    • → Adding a connector or pronoun on TOP of a natural link → Over-engineering
    • → Trust the reader to see the connection if it is obvious

  • When you are under extreme time pressure

    • → Cohesion tools require THINKING about what came before
    • → Under time pressure → Write clearly and simply → Complete the response
    • → An incomplete response with perfect cohesion scores LOWER than a complete response with adequate cohesion
    • → Refer to Chapter 26 (time management) for strategies

The Cognitive Science Behind Cohesion

WHY GIVEN-NEW WORKS:
  → The human brain processes new information by ATTACHING it to known information
  → When a sentence starts with something known → The brain says "I recognize this"
  → When new information follows → The brain says "Now I can attach this new thing to what I know"
  → When a sentence starts with something completely new → The brain says "Wait, where am I?"
  → This is WHY Dmitri's paragraph felt disconnected — every sentence demanded a mental RESET

WHY THEMATIC PROGRESSION WORKS:
  → Linear progression mimics NATURAL THOUGHT FLOW
  → "I said X. X leads to Y. Y leads to Z."
  → This is how the brain NATURALLY chains ideas
  → Writing that follows this pattern FEELS logical and clear
  → Writing that VIOLATES this pattern FEELS chaotic even if ideas are individually valid

The Cohesion Scoring Grid — What the Evaluator Checks

DIMENSION 2 SUB-CRITERIA FOR COHESION:

CHECK 1: Are sentences linked to each other?
  → Pronoun references? Demonstratives? Synonyms?
  → If NO links → "Not achieved"
  → If SOME links → "Partially achieved"
  → If CONSISTENT links → "Well achieved"

CHECK 2: Are the links VARIED?
  → Only connectors? → "Adequate but mechanical"
  → Mix of pronouns + synonyms + connectors + thematic progression → "Well achieved"

CHECK 3: Is information packaged correctly?
  → Given-New respected? → Smooth reading → Higher score
  → New-New throughout? → Choppy reading → Lower score

CHECK 4: Is there cross-paragraph cohesion?
  → Paragraphs linked to each other? → Response feels unified
  → Paragraphs isolated? → Response feels like 3 separate texts

🕳️ COMMON PITFALLS AND FAILURE MODES

Pitfall 1: The Connector-Only Approach

  • Relying EXCLUSIVELY on connectors for cohesion
  • → Text sounds like a list of connected items, not flowing prose
  • → Connectors are only 1 of 6 tools → Use all 6
  • Detection → Count your connectors → If more than 50% of sentences start with one → Over-relying

Pitfall 2: The Ambiguous Pronoun

  • "Cela" or "ils" with unclear referent
  • → Reader guesses → Guesses wrong → Comprehension breaks
  • Detection → After each pronoun, ask: "Can the reader identify EXACTLY what this refers to?"
  • Solution → If ambiguous → Replace with a demonstrative + noun: "Ce phénomène..." / "Ces résultats..."

Pitfall 3: The Missing Bridge Between Paragraphs

  • Perfect cohesion WITHIN paragraphs but ZERO cohesion BETWEEN paragraphs
  • → Each paragraph is a cohesive island → But the islands are not connected
  • Detection → Read the LAST sentence of paragraph 1 and the FIRST sentence of paragraph 2 → Is there ANY link?
  • Solution → Use a recall phrase or transition connector + demonstrative at paragraph openings

Pitfall 4: The Monotonous Cohesion Pattern

  • Using the SAME cohesion tool for every sentence transition
  • → "Cela... Cela... Cela..." or "En effet... En effet... En effet..."
  • Detection → Read only the FIRST WORD of each sentence → Are they repetitive?
  • Solution → Alternate tools: pronoun → synonym → connector → demonstrative → thematic pickup

Pitfall 5: The Over-Cohesive Text

  • Linking EVERY sentence with MULTIPLE tools simultaneously
  • → Text feels dense, over-engineered, suffocating
  • → The reader cannot find natural breathing points
❌ OVER-COHESIVE:
  "Les transports sont essentiels. En effet, cela est dû au fait que ces derniers,
   comme nous l'avons évoqué, constituent ce mode de déplacement dont dépend
   cette société contemporaine."
  → 5 cohesion tools in ONE transition → Exhausting

✅ BALANCED:
  "Les transports sont essentiels. En effet, ils constituent un pilier
   de la vie urbaine moderne."
  → 1–2 cohesion tools per transition → Natural

Pitfall 6: Ignoring Cohesion During Concession Transitions

  • Chapter 16 taught concession structures
  • The transition INTO and OUT OF a concession must be cohesive
❌ DISCONNECTED CONCESSION:
  "Les transports gratuits réduiraient la pollution. Certes, le coût serait élevé.
   Les gouvernements doivent investir."
  → The concession FLOATS — no link to what came before or after

✅ COHESIVE CONCESSION:
  "Les transports gratuits réduiraient la pollution. Certes, le coût d'une telle
   mesure serait élevé, mais cet investissement se justifie pleinement au regard
   des bénéfices environnementaux et sociaux."
  → "une telle mesure" = Tool 2 (synonym for free transport)
  → "cet investissement" = Tool 3 (demonstrative referring to "coût")
  → "bénéfices environnementaux et sociaux" = Tool 4 (refers back to earlier arguments)
  → Concession is WOVEN into the cohesive fabric
