📘 CHAPTER 2

THINKING BEFORE WRITING: HOW TO READ A FRENCH PROMPT AND EXTRACT ITS HIDDEN QUESTION

🔴 HYPOTHETICAL PROBLEM

You are a TEF candidate named Marco
He studied Chapter 1 → He now understands the 4 scoring dimensions
He knows Dimension 1 (Respect de la consigne) is the absolute foundation
He sits for a practice test and sees this prompt:

"Certains pensent que les réseaux sociaux rapprochent les gens, tandis que d'autres estiment qu'ils créent une distance entre les individus. Qu'en pensez-vous? Développez votre point de vue à l'aide d'arguments et d'exemples précis."

Marco reads it quickly
He sees "réseaux sociaux" (social media) and immediately starts writing about how social media is addictive
He writes about screen time, health problems, children using phones too much
His French is decent, his structure follows intro-body-conclusion
Result: He scores poorly on Dimension 1
The evaluator notes: "Hors sujet partiel — le candidat n'a pas traité la question de rapprochement vs. distance"

❓ THE CORE QUESTION

  • The prompt was about whether social media BRINGS PEOPLE CLOSER or CREATES DISTANCE
  • Marco wrote about addiction and health — a RELATED but DIFFERENT topic
  • He saw the theme (social media) but missed the SPECIFIC ANGLE (closeness vs. distance)
  • How do you systematically extract the EXACT question from ANY French prompt?
  • How do you ensure you NEVER go off-topic?

🟡 HOW TO APPROACH IT

Step 1: Recognize That Every TEF Section B Prompt Has Exactly 3 Layers

  • Every prompt is NOT just one block of text
  • It is a carefully constructed instruction with 3 distinct layers
  • You must identify ALL 3 layers before writing anything

🔹 LAYER 1: LE CONTEXTE (The Context)

  • This is the background information or general topic introduction
  • It sets the scene
  • It tells you WHAT the world is talking about
  • In Marco's prompt:
    • → "Certains pensent que les réseaux sociaux rapprochent les gens, tandis que d'autres estiment qu'ils créent une distance entre les individus."
    • → This is the CONTEXT — it presents two opposing viewpoints that exist in society
  • The context is NOT the question itself
  • Many candidates confuse context with the question → This is Marco's exact mistake

🔹 LAYER 2: LA QUESTION (The Actual Question)

  • This is the specific thing you are being asked to respond to
  • It is almost always a direct question or a clear instruction
  • In Marco's prompt:
    • → "Qu'en pensez-vous?"
    • → This means: "What do YOU think about THIS specific debate (closeness vs. distance)?"
  • The question ALWAYS connects to the context
  • "Qu'en pensez-vous?" does NOT mean "tell me anything about social media"
  • The "en" in "qu'en pensez-vous" refers BACK to the specific debate presented in the context
  • This is the most critical sentence in the entire prompt

🔹 LAYER 3: LES CONSIGNES DE FORME (The Format Instructions)

  • This tells you HOW to respond
  • It specifies what tools your response must include
  • In Marco's prompt:
    • → "Développez votre point de vue à l'aide d'arguments et d'exemples précis."
    • → You must DEVELOP (not just state) your viewpoint
    • → You must use ARGUMENTS (logical reasons)
    • → You must use PRECISE EXAMPLES (not vague generalities)
  • Ignoring format instructions → Penalized on Dimension 1 even if your opinion is relevant

Step 2: Create a Mental Extraction Protocol

  • Every time you see a Section B prompt, run this protocol:
EXTRACTION PROTOCOL:
├── Read 1: Read the ENTIRE prompt without analyzing (just absorb)
├── Read 2: Identify and UNDERLINE the CONTEXT (what is the general topic?)
├── Read 3: Identify and CIRCLE the QUESTION (what EXACTLY am I asked?)
├── Read 4: Identify and BOX the FORMAT INSTRUCTIONS (how must I respond?)
└── Read 5: Restate the question IN YOUR OWN WORDS in your head
            → If you cannot restate it, you have not understood it

🟢 HOW CAN WE SOLVE IT

The Solution = Mastering the 7 Question Types That TEF Section B Uses

  • TEF does not invent random questions
  • Section B prompts fall into 7 recurring question patterns
  • If you recognize the pattern, you instantly know what is expected
  • Each pattern demands a slightly different response approach

QUESTION TYPE 1: Opinion directe (Direct Opinion)

Signal phrases you will see:

  • → "Qu'en pensez-vous?"
  • → "Êtes-vous d'accord avec cette affirmation?"
  • → "Partagez-vous ce point de vue?"

What it demands:

  • → State YOUR clear position (agree, disagree, or nuanced)
  • → Defend it with reasons and examples

Example prompt:

"Le sport devrait être obligatoire à l'école. Êtes-vous d'accord avec cette affirmation?"

What the evaluator expects:

  • → A clear YES, NO, or PARTIALLY answer
  • → Followed by organized justification

QUESTION TYPE 2: Avantages et inconvénients (Advantages and Disadvantages)

Signal phrases you will see:

  • → "Quels sont les avantages et les inconvénients de...?"
  • → "Quels sont les aspects positifs et négatifs de...?"
  • → "Cette évolution présente-t-elle plus d'avantages ou d'inconvénients?"

What it demands:

  • → Present BOTH sides (positive AND negative)
  • → You may or may not need to choose a side depending on the phrasing
  • → If the prompt asks "plus d'avantages ou d'inconvénients?" → You MUST choose

Example prompt:

"Le télétravail se développe de plus en plus. Quels en sont les avantages et les inconvénients?"

What the evaluator expects:

  • → At least one well-developed advantage
  • → At least one well-developed disadvantage
  • → Balanced treatment of both sides

QUESTION TYPE 3: Cause et solution (Problem, Cause, and Solution)

Signal phrases you will see:

  • → "Quelles sont les causes de ce phénomène?"
  • → "Comment peut-on résoudre ce problème?"
  • → "Quelles solutions proposeriez-vous?"

What it demands:

  • → Identify causes OR propose solutions (or both, depending on the prompt)
  • → Be specific and concrete

Example prompt:

"La pollution dans les grandes villes ne cesse d'augmenter. Quelles solutions pourrait-on envisager pour améliorer la situation?"

What the evaluator expects:

  • → Multiple concrete solutions (not just "we should help the environment")
  • → Feasibility and logic in your proposals

QUESTION TYPE 4: Comparaison (Comparison Between Two Options)

Signal phrases you will see:

  • → "Préférez-vous X ou Y?"
  • → "Lequel de ces deux choix vous semble le meilleur?"
  • → "X est-il préférable à Y?"

What it demands:

  • → Compare two specific things
  • → State your preference
  • → Justify WHY one is better than the other (or why both have merit)

Example prompt:

"Certains préfèrent vivre en ville, d'autres à la campagne. Quel mode de vie vous semble préférable?"

What the evaluator expects:

  • → Discussion of BOTH options (not just the one you prefer)
  • → Clear final preference with logical reasoning

QUESTION TYPE 5: Évolution et changement (Evolution and Change)

Signal phrases you will see:

  • → "Cette tendance est-elle positive ou négative?"
  • → "Comment jugez-vous cette évolution?"
  • → "Faut-il s'inquiéter de ce changement?"

What it demands:

  • → Evaluate a trend or change in society
  • → Give your judgment (positive, negative, mixed)
  • → Explain WHY you judge it this way

Example prompt:

"De plus en plus de jeunes choisissent de voyager avant de commencer leurs études universitaires. Comment jugez-vous cette tendance?"

What the evaluator expects:

  • → Clear evaluative position
  • → Reasoning that connects the trend to its consequences

QUESTION TYPE 6: Mesure et politique (Policy and Action)

Signal phrases you will see:

  • → "Faut-il interdire...?"
  • → "Le gouvernement devrait-il...?"
  • → "Est-il nécessaire de...?"

What it demands:

  • → Take a position on whether a specific ACTION should or should not be taken
  • → Justify with arguments about consequences, ethics, or practicality

Example prompt:

"Faut-il interdire les téléphones portables dans les écoles?"

What the evaluator expects:

  • → Clear YES or NO (or conditional position)
  • → Arguments about WHY this action is justified or not

QUESTION TYPE 7: Réflexion sur une citation (Reflection on a Quote)

Signal phrases you will see:

  • → "Que pensez-vous de cette affirmation de [author]?"
  • → "Commentez cette citation."
  • → "Dans quelle mesure êtes-vous d'accord avec cette idée?"

What it demands:

  • → Interpret what the quote MEANS
  • → State whether you agree, disagree, or partially agree
  • → Defend your position with arguments

Example prompt:

"« L'éducation est l'arme la plus puissante pour changer le monde. » — Nelson Mandela. Que pensez-vous de cette affirmation?"

What the evaluator expects:

  • → Evidence that you UNDERSTOOD the quote's meaning
  • → A clear position relative to that meaning
  • → Not just restating the quote but engaging with it critically

🔵 WHAT COULD BE DONE

Option A: Read the prompt once quickly and start writing immediately

  • → This is what Marco did
  • → High risk of partial or complete off-topic response
  • → Saves 2 minutes of reading time but costs potentially ALL points on Dimension 1

Option B: Memorize all 7 question types and practice identifying them

  • → This is highly effective
  • → Pattern recognition becomes automatic with practice
  • → You instantly know what TYPE of response is expected
  • → Combined with the 3-layer extraction protocol → Nearly eliminates off-topic risk

Option C: Translate the prompt to your native language to understand it

  • → Can help in early practice stages
  • → Dangerous in the exam because translation introduces interpretation errors
  • → French nuances often do not survive translation
  • → Use this ONLY as a temporary crutch, not a permanent strategy

Option D: Underline keywords physically on the exam paper

  • → Extremely effective
  • → Forces your eyes to focus on critical words
  • → Creates a visual anchor you can reference while writing
  • → Recommended as a permanent exam habit

✅ WHAT SHOULD DO

1. Always Spend the First 3–4 Minutes ONLY on Prompt Analysis

  • Do NOT touch your pen to write the response during this time
  • These 3–4 minutes are the highest-value minutes of your entire Section B experience
  • A misunderstood prompt → 30 minutes of wasted writing
  • A correctly understood prompt → Every minute of writing counts toward your score

2. Physically Underline or Mark 3 Things on Your Exam Paper

MARKING SYSTEM:
├── UNDERLINE → The specific TOPIC (what is the subject?)
├── CIRCLE → The specific QUESTION WORD or TASK VERB
│             (pensez-vous? / êtes-vous d'accord? / quels sont? / faut-il?)
└── BOX → The FORMAT requirements (arguments + exemples + développez)

3. Restate the Question in a Simple French Sentence in Your Head

  • If the prompt is: "Faut-il interdire la publicité destinée aux enfants?"
  • Your mental restatement: "La question est: est-ce que la publicité pour les enfants doit être interdite, oui ou non?"
  • If you CANNOT do this restatement → You have NOT understood the prompt → Read again

4. Identify the Question Type (from the 7 types above)

  • Ask yourself: "Is this asking for my opinion? A comparison? Advantages/disadvantages? Solutions?"
  • The question type determines your response STRUCTURE (which you will learn in Chapter 5)
  • Wrong identification → Wrong structure → Lower score on Dimension 2

5. Identify the SCOPE of the Question

  • SCOPE = How narrow or broad is the question?
  • "Faut-il interdire les téléphones dans les écoles?" → Narrow scope (phones + schools specifically)
  • "La technologie améliore-t-elle notre vie?" → Broad scope (all technology + all of life)
  • Narrow scope → Stay focused, do NOT expand beyond what is asked
  • Broad scope → Choose 2–3 specific aspects to discuss, do NOT try to cover everything

❌ WHAT SHOULD NOT DO

1. Do NOT Read Only the First Sentence and Assume You Know the Question

  • The first sentence is almost always CONTEXT (Layer 1), not the question
  • The question usually appears in the SECOND or THIRD sentence
  • Reading only the first sentence → You respond to the context, not the question → Marco's exact error

2. Do NOT Confuse the TOPIC with the QUESTION

  • TOPIC = Social media
  • QUESTION = Does social media bring people closer or create distance?
  • You can write a perfect essay about social media addiction, but if the question was about closeness vs. distance → You are off-topic
  • The topic is the territory. The question is the specific path you must walk on that territory.

3. Do NOT Add Your Own Question to the Prompt

  • If the prompt asks: "Faut-il interdire les voitures en centre-ville?"
  • Do NOT decide to also discuss public transportation funding, bicycle infrastructure, and urban planning theory
  • Stay within the EXACT boundaries of what is asked
  • Going beyond the question ≠ showing knowledge → It = showing inability to follow instructions

4. Do NOT Ignore Part of a Multi-Part Prompt

  • Some prompts have TWO questions:
    • → "Quelles sont les causes de ce problème? Quelles solutions proposeriez-vous?"
    • → You must address BOTH causes AND solutions
    • → Addressing only one = partial respect de la consigne = penalized

5. Do NOT Panic If You Do Not Know Much About the Topic

  • The evaluator is NOT testing your knowledge of the topic
  • They are testing your ability to ARGUE in French
  • Even with limited knowledge, you can:
    • → State a clear position
    • → Give logical (even simple) reasons
    • → Use general examples from daily life
  • An organized response with simple knowledge scores HIGHER than a disorganized response with expert knowledge

SituationWhat to Apply

Prompt has "avantages et inconvénients"→ Question Type 2 → Present both sides systematically
Prompt has "causes" or "solutions"→ Question Type 3 → Identify problems or propose fixes
Prompt presents two options and asks preference→ Question Type 4 → Compare and choose with justification
Prompt asks about a "tendance" or "évolution"→ Question Type 5 → Evaluate the trend positively or negatively
Prompt has "faut-il" or "devrait-on"→ Question Type 6 → Take a policy position

🚫 WHEN NOT TO USE

When NOT to Spend Too Long on Prompt Analysis

  • The extraction protocol should take 3–4 minutes maximum
  • If you spend 8–10 minutes analyzing the prompt, you lose critical writing time
  • Once you identify: TOPIC + QUESTION TYPE + FORMAT REQUIREMENTS → Move to planning
  • Over-analysis leads to paralysis, not precision

When NOT to Apply Question Type Identification Rigidly

  • Some prompts blend types (e.g., "What are the advantages and disadvantages? What is your personal opinion?")
  • In blended cases, address BOTH types but give more weight to the FINAL question asked
  • The final sentence of the prompt usually carries the PRIMARY instruction

⚙️ HOW IT ONLY WORKS (INTERNAL MECHANICS)

The Cognitive Process Behind Correct Prompt Decoding

STAGE 1: PERCEPTION
├── Your eyes scan the entire prompt
├── Your brain identifies French words it knows
├── DANGER ZONE: Brain latches onto familiar topic words
│   and starts generating ideas BEFORE understanding the question
└── This is why Marco failed — his brain saw "réseaux sociaux"
    and immediately activated everything he knew about social media

STAGE 2: SEPARATION
├── You consciously SEPARATE context from question from instructions
├── This requires DISCIPLINE — your brain wants to start writing
├── The 3-layer protocol FORCES separation
└── Without this protocol, your brain merges all 3 layers into
    one blurry understanding

STAGE 3: CLASSIFICATION
├── You match the question to one of 7 known types
├── This activates a MENTAL TEMPLATE for how to respond
├── Classification reduces cognitive load during writing
└── Instead of inventing a response structure from scratch,
    you activate a pre-built framework

STAGE 4: VERIFICATION
├── You restate the question in simple French
├── If your restatement matches the prompt → Proceed
├── If your restatement feels different → Re-read and re-classify
└── This is your SAFETY CHECK before committing 30 minutes to writing

Why This 4-Stage Process Works

  • It converts a COMPLEX task (understanding a nuanced French prompt) into 4 SIMPLE sequential tasks
  • Each stage has a clear output:
    • Stage 1 → Raw perception
    • Stage 2 → Three separated layers
    • Stage 3 → Question type identified
    • Stage 4 → Verified understanding
  • If any stage fails, you catch it BEFORE writing, not after

How This Connects to Chapter 1's Scoring Dimensions

  • This entire chapter is about Dimension 1: Respect de la consigne
  • The extraction protocol is your TOOL for maximizing Dimension 1 scores
  • Without this protocol → Random chance of addressing the right question
  • With this protocol → Near-certain alignment with what the evaluator expects

🕳️ COMMON PITFALLS AND FAILURE MODES

Pitfall 1: The Topic Trap (Marco's Error)

  • What happens: You see a familiar topic word and start writing about the topic generally
  • Why it happens: Your brain associates the topic word with everything you know, not with the specific angle
  • How to prevent: ALWAYS read the FULL prompt and identify the QUESTION sentence separately from the CONTEXT sentence

Pitfall 2: The False Confidence Trap

  • What happens: You read the prompt, think "I understand," and start writing immediately
  • Why it happens: The prompt is in relatively simple French, so you FEEL you understood it
  • How to prevent: Force yourself to do the RESTATEMENT test — if you cannot restate the exact question in your own words, you did not truly understand it

Pitfall 3: The Vocabulary Panic Trap

  • What happens: You encounter an unfamiliar word in the prompt and panic
  • Why it happens: You assume you cannot answer if you do not know every word
  • How to prevent: Focus on the QUESTION sentence and the FORMAT instructions — context sentences may contain harder vocabulary, but the question itself is usually straightforward
  • Even if you miss a context word, the question "Qu'en pensez-vous?" is always clear

Pitfall 4: The Double Question Trap

  • What happens: The prompt asks two things, you only answer one
  • Why it happens: Your brain focuses on the first question and forgets the second
  • How to prevent: COUNT the question marks in the prompt — if there are two, you need to address both

Pitfall 5: The Implicit Question Trap

  • What happens: The prompt does not have an explicit question mark but still expects an argumentative response
  • Example: "Certains affirment que l'art est essentiel dans l'éducation. Développez votre point de vue."
  • Why it happens: No "?" makes it feel like there is no question to answer
  • How to prevent: "Développez votre point de vue" IS the question — it means "give and defend your opinion on the statement presented"
  • Any instruction to "développer," "justifier," or "commenter" = you must argue
