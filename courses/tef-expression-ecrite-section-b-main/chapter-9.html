📘 CHAPTER 9

ONE ARGUMENT, ONE EXAMPLE: THE GOLDEN PAIR THAT PROVES YOUR POINT

🔴 HYPOTHETICAL PROBLEM

You are a TEF candidate named Mei
She completed Phase 1 (Chapters 1–6) → She can assemble complete responses
She studied Chapter 7 → She has a full connector toolkit
She studied Chapter 8 → She can think bilaterally and integrate concessions
She writes a response to this prompt:

"Pensez-vous que les voyages soient la meilleure façon de s'éduquer? Justifiez votre réponse à l'aide d'arguments et d'exemples."

Mei produces this response:

"Aujourd'hui, les voyages sont de plus en plus accessibles grâce au développement des transports. Je suis convaincue que voyager constitue l'un des moyens les plus enrichissants de s'éduquer.

Tout d'abord, les voyages permettent de découvrir de nouvelles cultures, car ils exposent les individus à des modes de vie différents. En effet, cette confrontation avec l'altérité développe la tolérance et l'ouverture d'esprit. Par conséquent, les voyageurs acquièrent une compréhension du monde que les livres seuls ne peuvent offrir.

Par ailleurs, voyager développe des compétences pratiques essentielles, puisque les situations imprévues obligent les individus à s'adapter rapidement. Cela signifie que le voyage constitue une véritable école de la vie, où l'on apprend l'autonomie et la résolution de problèmes.

Certes, d'autres formes d'apprentissage, comme la lecture ou l'enseignement formel, sont également précieuses. Cependant, le voyage offre une dimension expérientielle irremplaçable.

En définitive, les voyages représentent un outil éducatif incomparable qui enrichit tant les connaissances que la personnalité."

Word count: ~165 words
Structure: Excellent ✅
Connectors: Varied ✅
Bilateral thinking: Present (Certes... Cependant...) ✅
Grammar: Strong ✅

But the evaluator notes: "Argumentation cohérente mais abstraite — aucun exemple concret — le sujet demande explicitement des exemples — consigne partiellement respectée"
Dimension 1 partially penalized — the prompt EXPLICITLY asked for examples and Mei gave NONE

❓ THE CORE QUESTION

  • Mei's response is logically sound, well-structured, and even nuanced
  • But it is entirely ABSTRACT — every sentence is a general claim
  • "Voyages develop tolerance" — HOW? WHERE? WHO experienced this?
  • "Unexpected situations force adaptation" — WHAT situation? WHAT adaptation?
  • The evaluator sees arguments but NO evidence
  • How do you create concrete, specific examples that PROVE your arguments?
  • What makes an example effective versus useless?
  • Where exactly does an example go within a justification unit?

🟡 HOW TO APPROACH IT

Step 1: Understand WHY Examples Are Non-Negotiable in TEF Section B

THE 3 REASONS EXAMPLES ARE MANDATORY:
│
├── REASON 1: THE PROMPT DEMANDS THEM
│   ├── Almost every Section B prompt includes: "...à l'aide d'exemples"
│   ├── Or: "...avec des exemples précis"
│   ├── Or: "...illustrez votre propos"
│   ├── This is a DIRECT INSTRUCTION — part of the consigne
│   ├── Ignoring it = Dimension 1 penalty (Chapter 1)
│   └── Mei lost points specifically because the prompt said "arguments et exemples"
│       and she provided only arguments
│
├── REASON 2: EXAMPLES PROVE YOUR ARGUMENT IS REAL
│   ├── An argument without an example is a HYPOTHESIS
│   ├── An argument WITH an example is EVIDENCE
│   ├── "Travel develops tolerance" → Hypothesis (maybe true, maybe not)
│   ├── "When a French student spends a semester in Japan, they learn to navigate
│   │    cultural differences daily, which develops genuine tolerance" → Evidence
│   └── The evaluator can VERIFY the plausibility of evidence but not of hypotheses
│
└── REASON 3: EXAMPLES DEMONSTRATE VOCABULARY RANGE
    ├── Abstract arguments use abstract vocabulary (concepts, theories, generalities)
    ├── Concrete examples use SPECIFIC vocabulary (places, people, actions, details)
    ├── Using BOTH abstract AND concrete vocabulary = Dimension 3 maximized
    └── Mei's response was 100% abstract vocabulary → Limited range demonstrated

Step 2: Understand the Difference Between a REAL Example and a FAKE Example

FAKE EXAMPLES (what most candidates produce):
│
├── TYPE 1: The Disguised Generality
│   ├── "Par exemple, beaucoup de gens voyagent et apprennent des choses."
│   ├── This is NOT an example — it is a general statement with "par exemple" in front
│   ├── Removing "par exemple" changes nothing — the sentence remains equally vague
│   └── TEST: Does the sentence describe a SPECIFIC situation, person, or event?
│       → NO → It is a disguised generality, not an example
│
├── TYPE 2: The Circular Restatement
│   ├── "Par exemple, les voyages éduquent parce qu'ils sont éducatifs."
│   ├── This restates the argument using different words
│   ├── It provides NO new information, NO concrete evidence
│   └── TEST: Does the sentence add information that was NOT in the argument?
│       → NO → It is a circular restatement, not an example
│
└── TYPE 3: The Impossible-to-Verify Claim
    ├── "Par exemple, des études montrent que les voyageurs sont plus intelligents."
    ├── WHICH studies? WHERE published? This is a vague appeal to authority
    ├── Without specificity, this is not evidence — it is handwaving
    └── TEST: Could someone verify or visualize this example?
        → NO → It is too vague to function as evidence

REAL EXAMPLES (what the evaluator wants to see):
│
├── TYPE 1: The Specific Scenario
│   ├── "Par exemple, un étudiant qui passe un semestre au Japon apprend
│   │    à communiquer sans maîtriser la langue, ce qui développe sa capacité
│   │    d'adaptation."
│   └── SPECIFIC: Who? (a student) Where? (Japan) What happens? (communicates without language)
│       What results? (adaptation skill developed) → CONCRETE and VIVID
│
├── TYPE 2: The Real-World Reference
│   ├── "À titre d'exemple, le programme Erasmus permet chaque année à des milliers
│   │    d'étudiants européens de vivre dans un pays étranger, ce qui enrichit
│   │    considérablement leur perspective culturelle."
│   └── SPECIFIC: What program? (Erasmus) Who? (European students) What scale? (thousands per year)
│       What result? (cultural perspective enriched) → VERIFIABLE and CREDIBLE
│
└── TYPE 3: The Hypothetical-but-Vivid Scenario
    ├── "Imaginons un jeune diplômé qui voyage seul en Amérique du Sud pour la première
    │    fois : il doit négocier les prix au marché, trouver son chemin sans GPS,
    │    et résoudre des problèmes imprévus — autant de compétences que l'école
    │    n'enseigne pas."
    └── SPECIFIC: Who? (young graduate) Where? (South America) What actions? (negotiate, navigate, solve)
        What contrast? (school doesn't teach these) → VIVID and RELATABLE

Step 3: Understand the 3 Types of Examples You Can Use in TEF

EXAMPLE TYPE A: FACTUAL/REAL-WORLD EXAMPLES
├── Based on known facts, programs, countries, statistics, events
├── Highest credibility
├── You do NOT need exact numbers — approximate is fine
├── Examples: "Le programme Erasmus..." / "Dans des pays comme la Suède..." /
│   "Selon les données de l'OMS..."
├── Advantage: Evaluator recognizes real references → Credibility boost
└── Risk: If the fact is WRONG, evaluator may notice → Only use facts you are confident about

EXAMPLE TYPE B: HYPOTHETICAL/ILLUSTRATIVE SCENARIOS
├── Invented but PLAUSIBLE situations that illustrate your point
├── Very effective when you cannot think of a real-world fact
├── Examples: "Un étudiant qui voyage au Japon..." / "Prenons le cas d'une famille qui..."
│   / "Imaginons un travailleur qui..."
├── Advantage: You control the scenario — you can make it perfectly support your argument
├── Risk: If the scenario is IMPLAUSIBLE → Evaluator sees it as weak evidence
└── Rule: Hypothetical examples must be REALISTIC and RELATABLE

EXAMPLE TYPE C: PERSONAL EXAMPLES
├── Based on your own experience or observation
├── Examples: "Dans mon pays d'origine..." / "J'ai pu constater que..."
├── Advantage: Authentic and unique
├── Risk: Can sound anecdotal rather than analytical if overused
├── Rule: Maximum 1 personal example per response
└── Must be presented analytically, not narratively
    ❌ "Quand j'étais jeune, j'ai voyagé en Italie et c'était super."
    ✅ "Ayant eu l'occasion de séjourner en Italie, j'ai pu constater
        que l'immersion culturelle développe une compréhension des
        réalités locales qu'aucun manuel ne peut transmettre."

🟢 HOW CAN WE SOLVE IT

The Solution = Mastering the "Golden Pair" Structure (Argument + Example)

  • Every body argument should consist of a GOLDEN PAIR:
    • → Part 1: The ABSTRACT ARGUMENT (your logical claim)
    • → Part 2: The CONCRETE EXAMPLE (your evidence)
  • Together, they form a complete persuasive unit
  • Separately, each is incomplete
THE GOLDEN PAIR FORMULA:
│
├── ARGUMENT (Abstract — 2–3 sentences):
│   ├── Claim + Causal connector (Chapter 4)
│   ├── Elaboration (Chapter 4, Category 3)
│   └── Consequence or implication
│
├── BRIDGE (1 phrase — connects argument to example):
│   ├── Illustration connector (Chapter 7, Function 5)
│   └── "Par exemple," / "À titre d'exemple," / "Notamment,"
│
└── EXAMPLE (Concrete — 1–2 sentences):
    ├── Specific scenario, fact, or reference
    ├── Details that make it VIVID (who, where, what, how)
    └── Optional: Brief connection back to the argument

🔹 BUILDING THE GOLDEN PAIR: STEP-BY-STEP DEMONSTRATION

Prompt: "Pensez-vous que les voyages soient la meilleure façon de s'éduquer?"
Argument 1: Travel develops cultural tolerance

STEP 1: Write the Abstract Argument (Chapter 4 skill — already mastered)

"Tout d'abord, les voyages permettent de développer une véritable tolérance culturelle, car ils confrontent les individus à des modes de vie radicalement différents des leurs. En effet, cette immersion dans une culture étrangère oblige le voyageur à remettre en question ses propres certitudes et à adopter un regard plus ouvert sur le monde."

Analysis:

  • Sequence connector: "Tout d'abord" (Chapter 7) ✅
  • Causal connector: "car" (Chapter 4) ✅
  • Elaboration: "En effet" (Chapter 4, Category 3) ✅
  • Abstract claim: "développer une véritable tolérance culturelle" ✅
  • Missing: Any concrete evidence → Argument is a hypothesis, not proven

STEP 2: Add the Bridge (Illustration Connector from Chapter 7)

"À titre d'exemple, ..."

Choice of connector:

  • "Par exemple" → Standard, safe (used this already many times in practice)
  • "À titre d'exemple" → Slightly more formal, less overused → Better for Dimension 3 variety
  • "Notamment" → Would work for an embedded quick example, but we want a DEVELOPED example here
  • Decision: "À titre d'exemple" → Formal, developed example will follow

STEP 3: Write the Concrete Example

Choosing example type:

  • Type A (Factual): Could mention Erasmus program, exchange students
  • Type B (Hypothetical): Could create a scenario about a student traveling
  • Type C (Personal): Could mention own travel experience
  • Decision: Type A (Factual) — Erasmus program → High credibility, verifiable reference

"À titre d'exemple, le programme Erasmus permet chaque année à plus de 300 000 étudiants européens de vivre et d'étudier dans un pays étranger. Cette expérience les amène à côtoyer des personnes de cultures diverses, ce qui transforme durablement leur vision du monde."

Analysis:

  • WHO: 300,000+ European students (specific scale)
  • WHAT PROGRAM: Erasmus (verifiable real-world reference)
  • WHAT HAPPENS: Live and study in a foreign country
  • WHAT RESULT: Interact with diverse cultures → Transform worldview
  • CONNECTION TO ARGUMENT: "transforme durablement leur vision du monde" echoes "tolérance culturelle" and "regard plus ouvert"

STEP 4: Assemble the Complete Golden Pair

"Tout d'abord, les voyages permettent de développer une véritable tolérance culturelle, car ils confrontent les individus à des modes de vie radicalement différents des leurs. En effet, cette immersion dans une culture étrangère oblige le voyageur à remettre en question ses propres certitudes et à adopter un regard plus ouvert sur le monde. À titre d'exemple, le programme Erasmus permet chaque année à plus de 300 000 étudiants européens de vivre et d'étudier dans un pays étranger, ce qui transforme durablement leur vision du monde."

Word count of this Golden Pair: ~90 words
Contains: Abstract argument (2 sentences) + Bridge + Concrete example (1 sentence)
This is ONE complete argumentative unit — the evaluator sees BOTH logic AND evidence

🔵 WHAT COULD BE DONE

Option A: Always use Type A (Factual) examples

  • → Highest credibility
  • → But requires knowing real-world facts, programs, statistics
  • → If you invent a "fact" and it is obviously wrong → Credibility damaged
  • → Best for topics where you have genuine knowledge

Option B: Always use Type B (Hypothetical) examples

  • → Safest — you control the scenario completely
  • → No risk of wrong facts
  • → But less impressive than real-world references
  • → Best for topics where you lack specific factual knowledge
  • → Must be PLAUSIBLE and SPECIFIC (not vague)

Option C: Mix Type A and Type B across your two arguments

  • → This is the OPTIMAL strategy
  • → Argument 1: Type A example (real-world reference) → Demonstrates cultural knowledge
  • → Argument 2: Type B example (hypothetical scenario) → Demonstrates analytical creativity
  • → Variety of example types = Dimension 3 boost (vocabulary diversity)

Option D: Use Type C (Personal) for both examples

  • → Risky — too many personal anecdotes make the response sound subjective
  • → Maximum 1 personal example per response
  • → Always present it analytically, not as a story (Chapter 1 rule: Section B is not narrative)
  • → Best used as ONE of two examples, combined with Type A or B for the other

2. Follow the "1 Argument = 1 Example" Rule

THE GOLDEN RULE:
│
├── Every argument in your body SHOULD have at least 1 example
│
├── In a FULL response (2 arguments):
│   ├── Argument 1 → Example 1
│   └── Argument 2 → Example 2
│
├── In a MINI-response (1 argument):
│   └── Argument 1 → Example 1
│
├── WHY NOT "1 argument = 2 examples"?
│   ├── Word count constraint → 2 examples per argument = too many words
│   ├── 1 well-developed example > 2 shallow examples
│   └── The evaluator wants QUALITY of illustration, not QUANTITY
│
└── WHY NOT "2 arguments = 1 shared example"?
    ├── Each argument makes a DIFFERENT claim
    ├── Each claim needs its OWN evidence
    ├── A shared example suggests your arguments are not truly distinct
    └── Keep examples PAIRED with their specific argument

3. Apply the Specificity Checklist to Every Example

SPECIFICITY CHECKLIST (before finalizing an example):
│
├── □ Does my example mention WHO? (students / workers / residents / a specific group)
├── □ Does my example mention WHERE? (a country / a city / a program / an institution)
├── □ Does my example mention WHAT HAPPENS? (a specific action / outcome / change)
├── □ Does my example mention a SCALE or QUANTITY? (numbers / percentages / timeframes)
├── □ Could someone VISUALIZE this example? (see the situation in their mind)
│
├── SCORING:
│   ├── 5/5 checks: Excellent example → Maximum credibility
│   ├── 3–4/5 checks: Good example → Solid evidence
│   ├── 1–2/5 checks: Weak example → Barely better than a generality
│   └── 0/5 checks: NOT an example → It is a disguised generality (rewrite)
│
└── APPLY TO MEI'S MISSING EXAMPLE:
    "Les voyages développent la tolérance."
    ├── WHO? → Missing
    ├── WHERE? → Missing
    ├── WHAT HAPPENS? → Missing
    ├── SCALE? → Missing
    ├── VISUALIZE? → Cannot visualize
    └── Score: 0/5 → This is NOT an example — it is an abstract claim
    
    IMPROVED:
    "Le programme Erasmus permet chaque année à plus de 300 000 étudiants
     européens de vivre dans un pays étranger, développant ainsi leur
     tolérance interculturelle."
    ├── WHO? → 300,000 European students ✅
    ├── WHERE? → Foreign country (via Erasmus) ✅
    ├── WHAT HAPPENS? → Live abroad, develop tolerance ✅
    ├── SCALE? → 300,000 per year ✅
    ├── VISUALIZE? → Yes — can picture students in foreign universities ✅
    └── Score: 5/5 → Excellent example

4. Master 3 Sentence Patterns for Presenting Examples

PATTERN 1: THE STANDALONE EXAMPLE (1–2 full sentences after the argument)
│
├── Structure: [Argument sentences]. [Illustration connector], [full example].
├── Connector: "Par exemple," / "À titre d'exemple," / "C'est le cas de..."
├── Word cost: 20–40 words
├── Best for: Developed, detailed examples (Type A factual)
│
└── Example:
    "Par exemple, le programme Erasmus permet chaque année à plus de
     300 000 étudiants européens de vivre et d'étudier dans un pays
     étranger, ce qui enrichit considérablement leur perspective culturelle."

PATTERN 2: THE EMBEDDED EXAMPLE (woven into the argument sentence)
│
├── Structure: [Argument], notamment [specific instance].
├── Connector: "notamment" / "en particulier" / "comme"
├── Word cost: 5–15 words (integrated into existing sentence)
├── Best for: Quick, concise examples (when word budget is tight)
│
└── Example:
    "Cette mesure bénéficierait à de nombreuses catégories de la population,
     notamment les étudiants et les personnes âgées à revenus modestes."

PATTERN 3: THE HYPOTHETICAL SCENARIO (introduced with a framing expression)
│
├── Structure: [Argument sentences]. [Framing expression] [vivid scenario].
├── Framing expressions:
│   ├── "Prenons le cas de..." (Let us take the case of...)
│   ├── "Imaginons..." (Let us imagine...)
│   ├── "Considérons le cas d'un(e)..." (Let us consider the case of a...)
│   └── "Il suffit de penser à..." (One need only think of...)
├── Word cost: 20–40 words
├── Best for: Topics where you lack factual data but can create plausible scenarios
│
└── Example:
    "Prenons le cas d'un salarié parisien : en travaillant depuis son
     domicile, il économise en moyenne 1h30 de transport quotidien,
     soit plus de 300 heures par an qu'il peut réinvestir dans sa
     vie personnelle."

5. Control Example Length Within Word Budget

WORD BUDGET WITH EXAMPLES (Full response — 200–250 words):
│
├── INTRODUCTION: 30–50 words (NO examples here)
│
├── GOLDEN PAIR 1:
│   ├── Argument: 30–50 words (abstract reasoning)
│   ├── Example: 20–40 words (concrete evidence)
│   └── Subtotal: 50–90 words
│
├── TRANSITION: 5–15 words
│
├── GOLDEN PAIR 2:
│   ├── Argument: 30–50 words (abstract reasoning)
│   ├── Example: 20–40 words (concrete evidence)
│   └── Subtotal: 50–90 words
│
├── CONCLUSION: 30–50 words (NO examples here)
│
└── TOTAL: 175–285 words → Target sweet spot: 200–250 words

KEY PROPORTION:
├── Arguments (abstract): ~50–60% of body
├── Examples (concrete): ~40–50% of body
└── This balance shows BOTH analytical thinking AND concrete awareness

❌ WHAT SHOULD NOT DO

1. Do NOT Use "Par exemple" Followed by a General Statement

  • This is the most common example failure (Fake Example Type 1 from above)
  • ❌ "Par exemple, beaucoup de gens pensent que le voyage est important."
  • → Remove "par exemple" → The sentence is unchanged → It was never an example
  • ✅ "Par exemple, le programme Erasmus accueille chaque année 300 000 étudiants qui découvrent une nouvelle culture."
  • → Remove "par exemple" → You lose the framing, but the CONTENT is clearly a specific instance
  • TEST: Remove the illustration connector. Is the remaining sentence SPECIFIC or GENERAL?
    • → Specific → Real example ✅
    • → General → Fake example ❌

2. Do NOT Put Examples in the Introduction or Conclusion

  • Introduction = framing (context + problématique + thesis) → No examples
  • Conclusion = synthesis (summary + ouverture) → No examples
  • Examples belong EXCLUSIVELY in the body, attached to their corresponding argument
  • An example in the introduction feels premature (you have not argued yet)
  • An example in the conclusion feels like a new argument (violates Chapter 5's rule)

3. Do NOT Use the Same Example for Both Arguments

  • Each argument addresses a DIFFERENT aspect of the topic
  • Using the same example for both → Suggests you only know one thing about the topic
  • ❌ Argument 1 (tolerance): "Erasmus develops tolerance"
    → Argument 2 (practical skills): "Erasmus also develops practical skills"
    → SAME example used twice → Limited range demonstrated
  • ✅ Argument 1 (tolerance): "Erasmus develops tolerance"
    → Argument 2 (practical skills): "A young graduate traveling alone in South America..."
    → DIFFERENT examples → Rich, varied illustration

4. Do NOT Develop Examples Into Stories

  • Remember Chapter 1: Section B is argumentative, NOT narrative
  • An example should ILLUSTRATE, not NARRATE
  • ❌ "Par exemple, quand j'ai voyagé en Italie, j'ai pris un train à Rome, puis j'ai visité le Colisée, et ensuite je suis allé manger des pâtes dans un petit restaurant, et le serveur était très sympathique, et..."
  • → This is a STORY, not an example → Dimension 1 risk (wrong text type)
  • ✅ "Ayant eu l'occasion de séjourner en Italie, j'ai pu constater que l'immersion dans une culture étrangère développe une compréhension des réalités locales qu'aucun manuel ne peut transmettre."
  • → This is an ANALYTICAL reference to a personal experience → Appropriate for Section B
  • The rule: Examples are EVIDENCE, not ANECDOTES → State the fact, show the result, move on

5. Do NOT Invent Obviously False Statistics

  • Approximate statistics are fine → "environ 300 000" / "plus de 50%"
  • Invented but plausible statistics are acceptable → "près de 80% des étudiants interrogés"
  • Obviously wrong statistics damage credibility → ❌ "99% des gens qui voyagent deviennent plus intelligents"
  • If you are unsure about a number → Use qualitative language instead:
    • → "une proportion significative de..." instead of a specific percentage
    • → "un grand nombre de..." instead of an exact count
    • → "selon plusieurs études..." instead of citing a specific study you are not sure about

6. Do NOT Use "Par exemple" More Than Once Per Response

  • Chapter 7 and Chapter 3 both established: no repetition of connectors
  • If Argument 1 uses "Par exemple," → Argument 2 must use a DIFFERENT illustration connector
  • ✅ Argument 1: "Par exemple, ..." → Argument 2: "À titre d'exemple, ..." / "Notamment, ..." / "C'est le cas de..."
  • This variety demonstrates Dimension 3 (vocabulary range)

⏱️ WHEN TO USE WHAT

SituationWhich Example Type and Pattern
You know a real-world fact about the topic→ Type A (Factual) + Pattern 1 (Standalone) → Maximum credibility
You cannot think of any real-world fact→ Type B (Hypothetical) + Pattern 3 (Scenario) → "Prenons le cas de..."
Word count is tight (approaching 250 words)→ Type B (Hypothetical) + Pattern 2 (Embedded) → "notamment..." → Minimal word cost
The topic is about your country or personal context→ Type C (Personal) + Pattern 1 → "Dans mon pays d'origine, ..." → Maximum 1 per response
You have strong factual knowledge→ Type A for BOTH arguments → Shows cultural and intellectual breadth
You have limited factual knowledge→ Type A for Argument 1 + Type B for Argument 2 → Mix credibility with creativity
The prompt explicitly says "exemples précis"→ Use Pattern 1 (Standalone) for both → DEVELOPED, DETAILED examples → The prompt demands specificity
The prompt says "exemples" without "précis"→ Pattern 2 (Embedded) is sufficient for one of the two arguments
You are writing a mini-response→ ONE example, Pattern 2 (Embedded) or short Pattern 1 → Word budget is limited

🚫 WHEN NOT TO USE

When NOT to Use Type A (Factual) Examples

  • When you are NOT confident the fact is correct
  • A wrong fact is worse than no fact → Use Type B (Hypothetical) instead
  • When the fact is so well-known it sounds like a cliché → "Paris is the city of love" → This is not evidence, it is a cliché

When NOT to Use Type C (Personal) Examples

  • When you have already used one personal example in the response → Maximum 1
  • When the personal example does not clearly support the argument → "I went to Italy" does not prove "travel educates" unless you explain HOW it educated YOU
  • When the personal example becomes a narrative → Keep it analytical

When NOT to Develop an Example at Length

  • When your word count is already at 220+ → A long example will push you over
  • In the conclusion → Never develop an example in the conclusion
  • When the argument itself is already very long → Trim the argument to make room for the example, or use an embedded example (Pattern 2)

⚙️ HOW IT ONLY WORKS (INTERNAL MECHANICS)

The Evaluator's Example Assessment Process

EVALUATOR'S PROCESSING OF EXAMPLES:
│
├── CHECK 1: Are examples PRESENT?
│   ├── YES → Proceed to quality assessment
│   ├── NO → If prompt asked for examples → Dimension 1 penalty (consigne not fully followed)
│   └── NO → If prompt did not explicitly ask → Minor concern for Dimension 2 (depth)
│
├── CHECK 2: Are examples CONCRETE and SPECIFIC?
│   ├── Specific scenario, real reference, vivid details → Dimension 2 positive (developed argumentation)
│   ├── Vague generality with "par exemple" → Evaluator notes: "Pseudo-example" → No credit
│   └── The specificity checklist (WHO/WHERE/WHAT/SCALE/VISUALIZE) is what the evaluator
│       unconsciously applies
│
├── CHECK 3: Do examples SUPPORT the argument they are attached to?
│   ├── Clear logical connection between argument and example → Dimension 2 positive
│   ├── Example is tangentially related but not directly supportive → Partial credit
│   └── Example contradicts or is irrelevant to the argument → Negative signal
│
├── CHECK 4: Are examples VARIED?
│   ├── Different example types across arguments → Dimension 3 positive (vocabulary range)
│   ├── Same example repeated → Dimension 3 negative (limited range)
│   └── Different illustration connectors used → Dimension 3 positive
│
└── CHECK 5: Are examples appropriately INTEGRATED?
    ├── Example flows naturally from argument via illustration connector → Dimension 2 positive
    ├── Example appears disconnected or randomly placed → Dimension 2 negative
    └── Example takes over the argument (too long, becomes narrative) → Dimension 1 concern

Why the Golden Pair Works Cognitively

COGNITIVE PERSUASION MECHANISM:
│
├── ABSTRACT ARGUMENT ALONE:
│   ├── Activates the reader's ANALYTICAL brain
│   ├── Reader processes: "This is a logical claim"
│   ├── Response: "Maybe true, maybe not — I need proof"
│   └── Conviction level: LOW (hypothesis only)
│
├── CONCRETE EXAMPLE ALONE:
│   ├── Activates the reader's EXPERIENTIAL brain
│   ├── Reader processes: "This is a real situation I can picture"
│   ├── Response: "Interesting, but what does this prove?"
│   └── Conviction level: LOW (anecdote without argument)
│
├── GOLDEN PAIR (Argument + Example):
│   ├── Activates BOTH analytical AND experiential processing
│   ├── Reader processes:
│   │   1. "Here is a logical claim" (analytical)
│   │   2. "And here is real evidence" (experiential)
│   │   3. "The evidence supports the claim" (integration)
│   ├── Response: "This is both logical AND real — I am convinced"
│   └── Conviction level: HIGH (argument verified by evidence)
│
└── THIS IS WHY THE GOLDEN PAIR IS MORE THAN THE SUM OF ITS PARTS:
    ├── Argument alone = Theory (convinces the mind but not the gut)
    ├── Example alone = Story (convinces the gut but not the mind)
    └── Golden Pair = Theory + Evidence (convinces BOTH → Maximum persuasion)

How Examples Interact With the 4 Scoring Dimensions

DIMENSION 1 — CONSIGNE:
├── Prompt asks for "exemples" → Examples present → Consigne satisfied ✅
├── Prompt asks for "exemples précis" → Specific examples present → Fully satisfied ✅
└── No examples when prompt asks for them → Partially penalized ❌ (Mei's error)

DIMENSION 2 — COHÉRENCE/COHÉSION:
├── Examples integrated via illustration connectors → Cohesion strengthened ✅
├── Examples that support their paired argument → Coherence strengthened ✅
├── Examples disconnected from arguments → Coherence weakened ❌
└── Examples that are actually generalities → No cohesion benefit ❌

DIMENSION 3 — LEXIQUE:
├── Factual examples introduce SPECIFIC VOCABULARY:
│   ├── "Programme Erasmus" / "Dunkerque" / "loi de 2018" → Proper nouns
│   ├── "empreinte carbone" / "gaz à effet de serre" → Technical terms
│   ├── "fréquentation" / "seuil de pauvreté" → Domain-specific words
│   └── These ALL expand your demonstrated vocabulary range
├── Abstract arguments use CONCEPTUAL vocabulary only
├── The COMBINATION of conceptual + specific = Maximum Dimension 3 score
└── This is why Mei scored lower on vocabulary — she used only abstract language

DIMENSION 4 — GRAMMAIRE:
├── Examples often trigger varied grammatical structures:
│   ├── Relative clauses: "un programme qui permet..." / "des étudiants qui découvrent..."
│   ├── Participial phrases: "Ayant eu l'occasion de séjourner en Italie, j'ai constaté..."
│   ├── Quantitative expressions: "plus de 300 000" / "environ 85%"
│   └── These demonstrate grammatical RANGE
└── Pure abstract argument tends to use repetitive structures (subject + verb + concept)

🕳️ COMMON PITFALLS AND FAILURE MODES

Pitfall 1: The "Par Exemple" Reflex Without Content

  • What happens: Candidate writes "par exemple" automatically but follows it with a generality
  • Why it happens: Candidate knows examples are expected but does not know how to create specific ones
  • How to prevent: Apply the specificity checklist BEFORE writing the example → If you cannot answer WHO/WHERE/WHAT → Think harder or switch to a hypothetical scenario

Pitfall 2: The Example That Contradicts the Argument

  • What happens: The example actually undermines the candidate's point
  • Example: Argument: "Travel is the best way to educate yourself" → Example: "Par exemple, de nombreux touristes visitent des pays étrangers sans jamais quitter leur hôtel de luxe, ce qui montre que le voyage ne garantit pas l'apprentissage."
  • → This example CONTRADICTS the argument → The candidate just argued AGAINST themselves
  • How to prevent: After writing the example, ask: "Does this example SUPPORT or UNDERMINE my argument?" → If it undermines → Replace it or use it in the concession section (Chapter 8) instead

Pitfall 3: The Over-Detailed Example

  • What happens: Example becomes a 60-word story that consumes the entire word budget
  • Why it happens: Candidate gets carried away with narrative details
  • How to prevent:
    • → Hard rule: Maximum 2 sentences per example (Pattern 1)
    • → Or 1 embedded clause (Pattern 2)
    • → State the fact → Show the result → STOP
    • → No unnecessary narrative details (what time, what color, what happened next...)

Pitfall 4: The Example Without Connection to the Argument

  • What happens: Example is specific and concrete but does not clearly relate to the argument
  • Example: Argument: "Remote work improves productivity" → Example: "Par exemple, de nombreuses entreprises technologiques sont situées dans la Silicon Valley."
  • → Silicon Valley's location has nothing to do with remote work productivity
  • How to prevent: After writing the example, draw a mental arrow from the example to the argument → If the arrow is clear → Keep it → If the arrow is unclear → The example does not belong here

Pitfall 5: The "I Read Somewhere" Trap

  • What happens: "Par exemple, j'ai lu quelque part que les voyages sont bons pour la santé mentale."
  • → "J'ai lu quelque part" (I read somewhere) is the vaguest possible source attribution
  • → It adds NO credibility — it is an unverifiable claim
  • How to prevent: Either cite something specific ("Selon une étude de l'OMS...") or drop the attribution entirely and present the fact directly ("Les recherches démontrent que...")

Pitfall 6: The Same Type for Every Example

  • What happens: Both examples are Type B hypothetical → No factual grounding
  • → Or both are Type C personal → Sounds subjective, not analytical
  • How to prevent: Mix example types across your two arguments → Variety = richer demonstration of knowledge and skill

🔑 KEY TAKEAWAY

What Chapter 9 Established

The Golden Pair = Argument + Example (the fundamental persuasion unit)
├── Argument alone = Hypothesis (unconvincing)
├── Example alone = Anecdote (unanalytical)
└── Golden Pair = Evidence-based argument (convincing and analytical)

3 Types of examples:
├── Type A: Factual/Real-world (highest credibility — programs, countries, statistics)
├── Type B: Hypothetical/Illustrative (safest — plausible invented scenarios)
└── Type C: Personal (authentic but risky — maximum 1 per response, must be analytical)

3 Sentence patterns for presenting examples:
├── Pattern 1: Standalone (developed, 1–2 sentences after argument)
├── Pattern 2: Embedded ("notamment..." — concise, within a sentence)
└── Pattern 3: Hypothetical scenario ("Prenons le cas de..." — vivid, controlled)

The Specificity Checklist:
├── WHO? / WHERE? / WHAT HAPPENS? / SCALE? / CAN YOU VISUALIZE?
├── 5/5 = Excellent example
├── 3–4/5 = Good example
├── 1–2/5 = Weak example
└── 0/5 = Not an example (rewrite)

Key rules:
├── 1 argument = 1 example (Golden Pair)
├── Examples belong ONLY in the body (not intro or conclusion)
├── Never repeat "par exemple" — vary illustration connectors
├── Never use the same example for both arguments
├── Mix example types (Type A + Type B) across arguments
├── Maximum 2 sentences per example (stay concise)
└── Build a mental example bank for the 10 most common TEF themes
