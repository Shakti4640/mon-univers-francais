📘 CHAPTER 18

VOCABULARY POWER-UP: TOPIC-SPECIFIC WORD BANKS FOR THE 10 MOST COMMON TEF THEMES

🔴 HYPOTHETICAL PROBLEM

You are a TEF candidate named Sofia
Sofia has completed Chapters 1–17 diligently
She understands the scoring grid (Chapter 1), builds causal chains (Chapter 15), uses concession masterfully (Chapter 16), and varies her sentence structures across all 7 types (Chapter 17)
She writes a practice response to this prompt:

"La pollution de l'environnement est un problème mondial. Que peut-on faire pour protéger la nature? Donnez votre avis."

Sofia's body paragraph:

"La pollution est un grand problème. Les gens font beaucoup de mauvaises choses pour la nature. Ils mettent des choses mauvaises dans l'air et dans l'eau. Pour résoudre ce problème, il faut faire de bonnes choses. Par exemple, on peut utiliser des choses qui ne font pas de pollution. De plus, les gouvernements doivent faire des lois pour arrêter les mauvaises choses."

Her sentence structure → varied (Type 1, Type 2, Type 3 with "qui", Type 4)
Her causal logic → present
Her connectors → used correctly ("Par exemple," "De plus")
But her evaluator writes: "Lexique pauvre et répétitif. Vocabulaire imprécis. Niveau A2 sur le plan lexical."
Translation: "Poor and repetitive vocabulary. Imprecise vocabulary. A2 level lexically."
Sofia scored well on Dimensions 2 and 4 but FAILED on Dimension 3
She is devastated — her structures were sophisticated but her WORDS were childlike

❓ THE CORE QUESTION

  • Why does Sofia sound like a child despite using B2-level sentence structures?
  • What specific vocabulary does TEF Section B demand for common topics?
  • How do you replace vague, generic words ("choses," "faire," "grand," "mauvais") with precise, topic-appropriate vocabulary?
  • How do you build vocabulary BANKS that are ready to deploy regardless of which prompt appears?
  • What are the 10 most frequent TEF themes and what words does EACH require?

🟡 HOW TO APPROACH IT

Step 1: Diagnose Sofia's Actual Problem

  • Count the VAGUE words in Sofia's paragraph:
Vague WordTimes UsedWhat It Should Have Been
choses (things)4 timessubstances toxiques, déchets, énergies renouvelables, réglementations
faire (to do/make)3 timescommettre, adopter, mettre en œuvre, élaborer
grand (big)1 timemajeur, considérable, préoccupant
mauvais/mauvaises (bad)2 timesnocif, néfaste, nuisible, préjudiciable
problème (problem)2 timesenjeu, défi, fléau, menace
  • Total vague words: 12 out of approximately 65 words = 18% of her text is vocabulary FILLER
  • The evaluator reads this and sees: "This candidate can BUILD sentences but cannot FILL them with appropriate content"
  • The sentence structures (Chapter 17) are the SKELETON → Vocabulary is the FLESH
  • Without vocabulary, the skeleton is visible and unappealing

Step 2: Understand What "Compétence Lexicale" (Dimension 3) Actually Measures

  • Refer to Chapter 1 (Scoring Grid):
    • → Dimension 3 evaluates vocabulary on THREE sub-criteria:
SUB-CRITERION 3A: PRECISION
  → Are you using the RIGHT word for the RIGHT meaning?
  → "Choses" is never the right word — it means nothing specific
  → "Substances toxiques" is precise — it names exactly what you mean

SUB-CRITERION 3B: VARIETY
  → Are you using DIFFERENT words for similar concepts?
  → Saying "problème" twice → Repetitive
  → Saying "problème" once then "enjeu" once → Varied

SUB-CRITERION 3C: REGISTER APPROPRIATENESS
  → Are your words at the B2 level, not A1-A2?
  → "Grand problème" = A1 vocabulary
  → "Enjeu majeur" = B2 vocabulary
  → Same meaning — vastly different impression on the evaluator

Step 3: Understand the 10 Most Frequent TEF Section B Themes

  • TEF Section B prompts are NOT random — they cluster around predictable themes
  • Analysis of past TEF exams and preparation materials reveals these 10 recurring themes:
THEME 1:  Technologie et numérique (Technology and digital)
THEME 2:  Éducation et apprentissage (Education and learning)
THEME 3:  Environnement et écologie (Environment and ecology)
THEME 4:  Santé et bien-être (Health and well-being)
THEME 5:  Travail et emploi (Work and employment)
THEME 6:  Médias et information (Media and information)
THEME 7:  Vie urbaine et transports (Urban life and transportation)
THEME 8:  Culture et loisirs (Culture and leisure)
THEME 9:  Relations sociales et famille (Social relationships and family)
THEME 10: Mondialisation et société (Globalization and society)
  • If you build vocabulary banks for ALL 10 themes → You are prepared for virtually ANY prompt
  • Most prompts combine 2 themes (e.g., "Technology + Education" or "Health + Work")
  • Your banks can then be MIXED to address combined prompts

Step 4: Understand the Architecture of a Vocabulary Bank

  • Each theme bank is organized into 5 components:
COMPONENT 1: NOUNS — The concepts and objects specific to the theme
COMPONENT 2: VERBS — The actions and processes specific to the theme
COMPONENT 3: ADJECTIVES — The qualities and descriptions specific to the theme
COMPONENT 4: EXPRESSIONS — Fixed phrases and collocations specific to the theme
COMPONENT 5: ARGUMENT STARTERS — Ready-made sentence beginnings for the theme
  • This structure ensures you have the RIGHT word for every PART of a sentence
  • Refer to Chapter 17 → Your sentence structures need nouns (for subjects/objects), verbs (for predicates), adjectives (for descriptions), and expressions (for sophistication)

2. Apply the "UPGRADE CHAIN" Method to Transform Any Sentence

The Method:

STEP 1: Write your sentence naturally (even with vague words)
STEP 2: Identify every vague word
STEP 3: Replace each vague word with a precise B2 alternative
STEP 4: Check that the sentence still makes grammatical sense

Full Demonstration — Sofia's Paragraph UPGRADED:

Sofia's OriginalUpgraded Version
La pollution est un grand problème.La dégradation environnementale constitue un enjeu majeur.
Les gens font beaucoup de mauvaises choses pour la nature.Les activités humaines engendrent des conséquences néfastes pour l'écosystème.
Ils mettent des choses mauvaises dans l'air et dans l'eau.Les industries rejettent des substances toxiques dans l'atmosphère et les cours d'eau.
Il faut faire de bonnes choses.Il est impératif d'adopter des mesures concrètes.
On peut utiliser des choses qui ne font pas de pollution.On peut recourir à des énergies renouvelables qui respectent l'environnement.
Les gouvernements doivent faire des lois pour arrêter les mauvaises choses.Les gouvernements doivent élaborer des réglementations strictes pour limiter les émissions polluantes.

❌ WHAT SHOULD NOT DO

1. Do NOT Use a B2 Word You Do Not Fully Understand

  • Using a sophisticated word INCORRECTLY is worse than using a simple word correctly
  • ❌ "La technologie engendre des bienfaits." → "Engendrer" carries a negative/neutral connotation — it does NOT pair naturally with "bienfaits" (benefits)
  • ✅ "La technologie génère des bienfaits." → "Générer" is neutral and pairs correctly
  • ✅ "La technologie engendre des problèmes." → "Engendrer" pairs naturally with negative outcomes
  • RULE: If you are unsure about a word's exact connotation → Use the simpler alternative you ARE sure about

2. Do NOT Overload One Sentence With Too Many Sophisticated Words

❌ OVERLOADED:
  "La dégradation environnementale irréversible engendre des conséquences
   néfastes et délétères qui menacent de manière préoccupante la pérennité
   de la biodiversité écosystémique planétaire."
  → Every single word is B2–C1 → Sounds unnatural → Evaluator suspects memorization

✅ BALANCED:
  "La dégradation de l'environnement engendre des conséquences néfastes
   qui menacent la biodiversité à l'échelle planétaire."
  → Mix of B1 and B2 words → Sounds natural → Evaluator sees genuine competence

3. Do NOT Use French-English Cognates Without Verifying Meaning

  • Many English-French word pairs look similar but have DIFFERENT meanings (faux amis)

4. Do NOT Repeat the Same Sophisticated Word More Than Once

  • Using "néfaste" once → impressive
  • Using "néfaste" three times → repetitive and suspicious
  • RULE: For every concept, have at least TWO B2 synonyms ready

5. Do NOT Ignore Gender Agreement With New Vocabulary

  • When you learn a new noun, ALWAYS learn its gender
  • A gender error on a sophisticated word is MORE noticeable than a gender error on a basic word
  • ❌ "un conséquence néfaste" → "Conséquence" is FEMININE
  • ✅ "une conséquence néfaste"
  • ❌ "une enjeu majeur" → "Enjeu" is MASCULINE
  • ✅ "un enjeu majeur"

6. Do NOT Abandon ALL Simple Words

  • Your response should feel NATURAL, not like a thesaurus explosion
  • Target ratio: 60% B2 vocabulary + 40% standard B1 vocabulary
  • The B1 words provide readability → The B2 words provide scoring power
  • A response written ENTIRELY in C1 vocabulary but with B1 structures → Feels memorized
  • A response with B2 structures (Chapter 17) AND B2 vocabulary (Chapter 18) → Feels authentic

🚫 WHEN NOT TO USE

When NOT to Deploy Theme-Specific Vocabulary

  • When you are unsure of the word's exact meaning

    • → A misused sophisticated word costs more than a correctly used simple one
    • → Refer to the Escalation Ladders → Drop to B1 backup level if uncertain

  • When the word does not fit the sentence structure grammatically

    • → Some B2 expressions require specific prepositions or constructions
    • → "Recourir à" requires "à" → If you cannot remember the preposition, use "utiliser" instead
    • → "Porter atteinte à" requires "à" → If unsure, use "nuire à" (simpler but still B2)

  • When you have already used 3–4 sophisticated words in the same sentence

    • → Refer to the "overload" rule above
    • → Distribute sophisticated words ACROSS sentences, not within one sentence

  • When the theme bank does not match the prompt

    • → If the prompt is about family relationships, do NOT force environmental vocabulary
    • → Use universal upgrade words instead → They work in ANY theme

⚙️ HOW IT ONLY WORKS (INTERNAL MECHANICS)

How the Evaluator Processes Vocabulary

EVALUATOR'S MENTAL PROCESS FOR DIMENSION 3 (Lexique):

SCAN 1: Identify VAGUE words
  → Every "chose," "faire," "bon," "mauvais" = Mental penalty marker
  → Many vague words = "A2 vocabulary" = Low Dimension 3 score

SCAN 2: Identify PRECISE words
  → Every theme-specific term = Mental bonus marker
  → "Dégradation environnementale" instead of "pollution" = "This candidate has range"

SCAN 3: Check for VARIETY
  → Same sophisticated word repeated? = "Memorized, not mastered"
  → Different sophisticated words for similar concepts? = "Genuine vocabulary depth"

SCAN 4: Check for APPROPRIATENESS
  → Is the word used correctly in context?
  → Is the register consistent? (No mixing informal + formal)
  → Does the word fit the theme of the prompt?

FINAL ASSESSMENT:
  → Few vague words + Multiple precise words + Variety + Correct usage
     = "Well achieved" to "Excellently achieved" (B2–C1)

The Vocabulary-Structure Integration Model

Chapter 17 (Sentence Structure) = The CONTAINER
Chapter 18 (Vocabulary) = The CONTENT

CONTAINER without CONTENT:
  "Dans une société où les _____ sont _____, il est _____ de _____."
  → Beautiful structure → Empty

CONTENT without CONTAINER:
  "Dégradation. Environnementale. Préoccupant. Biodiversité. Enjeu."
  → Rich words → No structure → Incoherent

CONTAINER + CONTENT:
  "Dans une société où la dégradation environnementale est préoccupante,
   il est impératif de préserver la biodiversité."
  → Beautiful structure + Rich vocabulary = Maximum score on Dimensions 3 + 4

The Memory Architecture for Vocabulary Banks

DO NOT memorize word lists in isolation

INSTEAD, memorize in CONTEXTUAL SENTENCES:
  ❌ "néfaste = harmful" → Isolated → Easily forgotten → Hard to use naturally
  ✅ "Les conséquences néfastes de la pollution menacent la biodiversité."

MEMORY STRUCTURE:
  Each theme = 3–5 FULL SENTENCES memorized
  Each sentence contains 2–3 B2 vocabulary items
  When the theme appears in a prompt → Sentences surface naturally
  You adapt the memorized sentence to the specific prompt → Not copy, but ADAPT

🕳️ COMMON PITFALLS AND FAILURE MODES

Pitfall 1: The Thesaurus Effect

  • Replacing EVERY word with its most complex synonym
  • → Text sounds artificial and forced
  • → Evaluator suspects dictionary use, not genuine competence
  • Solution → 60/40 ratio: 60% B2 vocabulary, 40% natural B1 vocabulary

Pitfall 2: The Wrong Theme Bank

  • Activating Environment vocabulary for a Work prompt
  • → Vocabulary does not fit → Sentences feel disconnected from the topic
  • Solution → Always detect the theme FIRST (Step 2 of exam strategy) before selecting vocabulary

Pitfall 3: The Gender Catastrophe

  • Learning nouns without their genders → Systematic agreement errors
  • → "Un conséquence" / "Une enjeu" / "Un mesure" → Evaluator penalizes Dimension 4
  • Solution → ALWAYS learn: un enjeu, une conséquence, une mesure, un phénomène

Pitfall 4: The Preposition Amnesia

  • Using B2 verbs without their required prepositions
  • ❌ "Contribuer la société" → WRONG → Missing "à"
  • ✅ "Contribuer à la société"
  • ❌ "Nuire la santé" → WRONG → Missing "à"
  • ✅ "Nuire à la santé"
  • Critical B2 verb + preposition pairs to memorize:
VerbRequired PrepositionExample
contribueràcontribuer à l'amélioration

recouriràrecourir à des solutions
porter atteinteàporter atteinte à la vie privée
bénéficierdebénéficier d'un accès équitable
souffrirdesouffrir d'isolement
dépendrededépendre de la technologie
se soucierdese soucier de l'environnement
s'adapteràs'adapter aux changements
aboutiràaboutir à un compromis

Pitfall 5: The Vocabulary Island

  • Using a B2 word once then NEVER connecting it to the rest of the argument
  • → "La cyberdépendance..." → Then never mentioning technology again
  • Solution → Each B2 word should connect to the argument flow → Refer to Chapter 19 (cohesion) for techniques to maintain thematic continuity

Pitfall 6: The False Friend Disaster

  • Using faux amis as listed above
  • → "Actuellement" meaning "currently" not "actually"
  • → This error is IMMEDIATELY visible to French evaluators
  • Solution → Memorize the 6 most dangerous faux amis listed in "What Should Not Do" section
