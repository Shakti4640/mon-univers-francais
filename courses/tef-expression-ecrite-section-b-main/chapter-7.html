🟡 HOW TO APPROACH IT

Step 1: Understand What Connectors Actually DO

  • In Chapter 4, you learned CAUSAL connectors (car, puisque, en effet, grâce à...)
  • Those connectors serve ONE specific function: linking a reason to an opinion
  • But argumentation requires MANY types of logical relationships, not just cause
THE LOGICAL RELATIONSHIPS IN ARGUMENTATION:
│
├── CAUSE: Why does this happen? (Chapter 4 — ALREADY COVERED)
│   └── car, puisque, en raison de, grâce à...
│
├── SEQUENCE: In what order do my ideas come?
│   └── "First this, then this, finally this"
│
├── ADDITION: What else supports my point?
│   └── "Moreover, furthermore, in addition"
│
├── OPPOSITION: What contradicts or contrasts with this?
│   └── "However, but, on the other hand"
│
├── CONSEQUENCE: What results from this?
│   └── "Therefore, thus, as a result"
│
└── ILLUSTRATION: Can I show this concretely?
    └── "For example, such as, in particular"
  • Chapter 4 covered CAUSE
  • Chapter 7 covers SEQUENCE, ADDITION, OPPOSITION, CONSEQUENCE, and ILLUSTRATION
  • Together, Chapters 4 + 7 = your COMPLETE connector toolkit

Step 2: Understand WHY Lucia's Text Fails Without Connectors

LUCIA'S TEXT WITHOUT CONNECTORS:

"La gratuité réduirait la pollution."     → Statement 1
"Les gens utiliseraient moins leur voiture." → Statement 2
"Les émissions de CO2 diminueraient."      → Statement 3
"L'air serait plus pur dans les villes."   → Statement 4

READER'S EXPERIENCE:
├── Statement 1: "Okay, pollution would decrease."
├── Statement 2: "People would drive less. Is this related to Statement 1?"
├── Statement 3: "CO2 would decrease. Is this a RESULT of Statement 2? Or a separate point?"
├── Statement 4: "Air would be purer. Is this a RESULT of Statement 3? A NEW argument?"
│
└── The reader CANNOT TELL how these statements relate
    The reader must GUESS the logical relationships
    The evaluator should NOT have to guess → YOUR job is to SHOW the logic
THE SAME TEXT WITH CONNECTORS:

"Tout d'abord, la gratuité réduirait la pollution,
 car les gens utiliseraient moins leur voiture.
 Par conséquent, les émissions de CO2 diminueraient
 et l'air serait ainsi plus pur dans les villes."

READER'S EXPERIENCE:
├── "Tout d'abord" → "This is the FIRST argument"
├── "car" → "Here is the REASON why pollution would decrease"
├── "Par conséquent" → "This is the RESULT of less car use"
├── "ainsi" → "This is the FINAL OUTCOME of the chain"
│
└── The reader sees the EXACT logical chain without guessing
    Every relationship is EXPLICIT
    The evaluator thinks: "This candidate understands logical articulation"

  • You need connectors for 5 logical functions (cause is already covered in Chapter 4)
  • Each function has multiple French expressions at different register levels
  • You do NOT need to memorize all of them — you need a working arsenal of 3–4 per function

🟢 HOW CAN WE SOLVE IT

The Solution = Building a 5-Function Connector Toolkit

  • Each function below is presented with its expressions organized by register level
  • This mirrors Chapter 3's tier system and Chapter 4's category system
  • Your goal: Select 3–4 expressions from EACH function for your personal arsenal

🔹 FUNCTION 1: SEQUENCE CONNECTORS (Connecteurs de séquence)

Purpose: Telling the reader the ORDER of your arguments

Why sequence matters:

  • When you have 2 arguments, the reader needs to know: "Is this the first point or the second?"
  • Without sequence markers, arguments blur together
  • Sequence connectors create a ROADMAP for the reader
French ExpressionEnglish EquivalentRegisterUsage Position
D'abord / Tout d'abordFirst / First of allStandard / FormalStart of Argument 1
PremièrementFirstlyFormalStart of Argument 1
En premier lieuIn the first placeVery FormalStart of Argument 1
EnsuiteThen / NextStandardStart of Argument 2
DeuxièmementSecondlyFormalStart of Argument 2
En second lieuIn the second placeVery FormalStart of Argument 2
EnfinFinallyStandardStart of final argument or conclusion transition
En dernier lieuLastly / In the last placeVery FormalStart of final argument

Pairing rules:

NATURAL PAIRS (use these together):

PAIR A (Standard register):
  Tout d'abord, ... → Ensuite, ... → Enfin, ...

PAIR B (Formal register):
  En premier lieu, ... → En second lieu, ... → En dernier lieu, ...

PAIR C (Mixed — most natural for TEF):
  Tout d'abord, ... → Ensuite, ... (or Par ailleurs, ...) → Enfin, ...

DO NOT MIX REGISTERS WITHIN A PAIR:
  ❌ "En premier lieu, ... Ensuite, ..." (formal start → standard continuation)
  ❌ "D'abord, ... En second lieu, ..." (standard start → formal continuation)
  ✅ Keep the register consistent across your sequence markers

Application to Lucia's text:

BEFORE: "La gratuité réduirait la pollution."
         (No sequence marker — reader doesn't know this is Argument 1)

AFTER:  "Tout d'abord, la gratuité des transports réduirait significativement la pollution."
         ("Tout d'abord" tells the reader: THIS IS MY FIRST ARGUMENT)

🔹 FUNCTION 2: ADDITION CONNECTORS (Connecteurs d'addition)

Purpose: Adding more support, more evidence, more arguments to strengthen your position

Why addition matters:

  • When your second argument SUPPORTS the same position as your first
  • You need to signal: "Here is ADDITIONAL evidence for my point"
  • Without addition markers, the reader may think you are repeating yourself or changing topic
French ExpressionEnglish EquivalentRegisterNuance
De plusMoreover / FurthermoreStandardSimple addition of a new point
En outreFurthermore / In additionFormalSlightly more formal than "de plus"
De surcroîtMoreover / What is moreVery FormalSignals an even STRONGER additional point
Par ailleursFurthermore / BesidesFormalSignals a shift to a DIFFERENT ANGLE of the same topic
ÉgalementAlso / EquallyStandardSimple equivalence addition
Qui plus estWhat is moreVery FormalEmphatic addition — the new point is even more important
Non seulement... mais aussi / mais encoreNot only... but alsoFormalPaired connector — very powerful for combining two ideas in one sentence

Critical distinction — "De plus" vs. "Par ailleurs":

"De plus" = Adding a point on the SAME angle
├── Argument 1: Free transport reduces pollution (environmental angle)
├── "De plus, cela diminuerait le bruit dans les centres-villes." (ALSO environmental angle)
└── Same angle, more evidence

"Par ailleurs" = Shifting to a DIFFERENT angle
├── Argument 1: Free transport reduces pollution (environmental angle)
├── "Par ailleurs, cette mesure favoriserait l'égalité sociale." (NEW angle: social)
└── Different angle, same overall position

RULE: When moving from Argument 1 to Argument 2 (different angles),
      "Par ailleurs" is more precise than "De plus"
      "De plus" works within an argument for adding evidence on the same angle

Application to Lucia's text:

BEFORE: "La gratuité favoriserait l'égalité sociale."
         (No signal that this is a NEW, ADDITIONAL argument)

AFTER:  "Par ailleurs, la gratuité favoriserait l'égalité sociale."
         ("Par ailleurs" signals: I am shifting to a new angle of my argument)

🔹 FUNCTION 3: OPPOSITION CONNECTORS (Connecteurs d'opposition)

Purpose: Introducing a contrasting idea, an objection, or an alternative perspective

Why opposition matters:

  • Even if you are arguing FOR something, acknowledging the other side shows intellectual maturity
  • Opposition connectors signal: "I am aware of the counterargument"
  • They are ESSENTIAL for the thèse-antithèse pattern (Chapter 14) and concession (Chapter 16)
  • At this stage, you need basic opposition awareness
French ExpressionEnglish EquivalentRegisterNuance
MaisButBasic (A2)Simple opposition — use sparingly in formal writing
CependantHoweverFormal (B1–B2)The standard formal replacement for "mais"
ToutefoisHowever / NeverthelessFormal (B2)Slightly more nuanced than "cependant"
NéanmoinsNevertheless / NonethelessVery Formal (B2)Signals: "Despite what I just said, this other thing is also true"
En revancheOn the other handFormal (B2)Signals a CONTRAST between two things (not necessarily a contradiction)
PourtantYet / And yetStandard (B1)Signals surprise or unexpected contrast
OrNow / HoweverFormal (B2)Introduces a fact that changes the direction of reasoning

Critical distinction — "Cependant" vs. "En revanche":

"Cependant" = Despite what was said, there is an OBJECTION or LIMITATION
├── "La gratuité serait bénéfique pour l'environnement.
│    Cependant, son financement poserait des défis considérables."
└── The second point WEAKENS or LIMITS the first point

"En revanche" = In CONTRAST, there is a different perspective (not necessarily an objection)
├── "Les transports gratuits profiteraient aux citadins.
│    En revanche, les habitants des zones rurales n'en bénéficieraient guère."
└── The second point CONTRASTS with the first (different group, different experience)
     but does not necessarily WEAKEN it

RULE: "Cependant" for objections/limitations
      "En revanche" for contrasts/comparisons
      They are NOT interchangeable

When to use opposition connectors at this stage:

PRIMARY USE IN SECTION B:
├── In the CONCLUSION to add nuance: "Toutefois, il serait nécessaire de..."
├── In a CONCESSION within an argument: "Certes... cependant..." (Chapter 16 will deepen this)
├── When transitioning to a counterargument in thèse-antithèse (Chapter 14)
└── To show the evaluator you can think beyond one-sided argumentation

FREQUENCY IN A MINI/FULL RESPONSE:
├── Mini-response (80–120 words): 0–1 opposition connector (optional)
├── Full response (200–250 words): 1–2 opposition connectors (recommended)
└── Overusing opposition → Your position becomes unclear → Dimension 1 risk

🔹 FUNCTION 4: CONSEQUENCE CONNECTORS (Connecteurs de conséquence)

Purpose: Showing WHAT RESULTS FROM a previous statement

Why consequence matters:

  • Arguments are not just claims — they have OUTCOMES
  • Showing the consequence of a reason makes your argument MORE CONVINCING
  • Consequence connectors answer: "So what? What happens as a result?"
  • They create the FORWARD FLOW that was missing in Lucia's text
French ExpressionEnglish EquivalentRegisterGrammar Note
DoncSo / ThereforeStandard (B1)Can be placed within a sentence (not always at start)
AinsiThus / In this wayFormal (B2)Elegant — works at sentence start or mid-sentence
Par conséquentConsequently / As a resultFormal (B2)Always at sentence start — signals a clear logical result
C'est pourquoiThat is whyFormal (B1–B2)Connects directly to the cause — very clear logical chain
De ce faitAs a result of thisFormal (B2)Similar to "par conséquent" but slightly more concise
Dès lorsFrom that point / ConsequentlyVery Formal (B2–C1)Signals a logical turning point — very elegant
Il en résulte queIt follows that / The result is thatVery Formal (C1)Academic register — use sparingly
En conséquenceIn consequenceFormal (B2)Direct, clear, professional

How consequence connectors differ from causal connectors (Chapter 4):

CAUSAL CONNECTORS (Chapter 4):
├── Direction: BACKWARD — they point to the REASON
├── "La pollution diminue CAR les gens prennent les transports."
│                         ↑ points backward to the reason
└── They answer: "WHY does this happen?"

CONSEQUENCE CONNECTORS (Chapter 7):
├── Direction: FORWARD — they point to the RESULT
├── "Les gens prennent les transports. PAR CONSÉQUENT, la pollution diminue."
│                                      ↑ points forward to the result
└── They answer: "WHAT HAPPENS as a result?"

SAME LOGICAL RELATIONSHIP — DIFFERENT DIRECTION:
├── "La pollution diminue car les gens prennent les transports." (cause → effect)
├── "Les gens prennent les transports. Par conséquent, la pollution diminue." (effect → cause)
│
└── Having BOTH directions in your toolkit = maximum flexibility
    Use causal connectors when the REASON is more important
    Use consequence connectors when the RESULT is more important

Application to Lucia's text:

BEFORE: "Les émissions de CO2 diminueraient. L'air serait plus pur dans les villes."
         (Two statements with no explicit connection)

AFTER:  "Les émissions de CO2 diminueraient. Par conséquent, l'air serait nettement
         plus pur dans les villes."
         ("Par conséquent" explicitly shows: pure air is the RESULT of fewer emissions)

🔹 FUNCTION 5: ILLUSTRATION CONNECTORS (Connecteurs d'illustration)

Purpose: Introducing a concrete EXAMPLE to support an abstract argument

Why illustration matters:

  • Arguments without examples feel theoretical and unconvincing
  • The TEF prompt almost always asks for "arguments ET exemples"
  • Illustration connectors signal: "Here is PROOF of what I just claimed"
  • Chapter 9 will deepen the art of examples — this chapter provides the CONNECTOR tools
French ExpressionEnglish EquivalentRegisterUsage Context
Par exempleFor exampleStandard (B1)The most common — safe and clear
À titre d'exempleAs an example / By way of exampleFormal (B2)More elegant than "par exemple"
NotammentIn particular / NotablyFormal (B2)Highlights a specific instance within a broader category
En particulierIn particularFormal (B2)Similar to "notamment" — slightly more emphatic
C'est le cas de...This is the case of...Formal (B2)Introduces a specific real-world instance
Comme en témoigne...As demonstrated by... / As evidenced by...Very Formal (B2–C1)Introduces evidence that PROVES the point
Tel est le cas de...Such is the case of...Very Formal (C1)Highly academic — use only if confident

Critical distinction — "Par exemple" vs. "Notamment":

"Par exemple" = Introduces a COMPLETE, SEPARATE example
├── "La technologie facilite la communication. Par exemple, les applications
│    de messagerie permettent de contacter instantanément des personnes
│    à l'autre bout du monde."
└── The example is a full illustration — detailed and standalone

"Notamment" = Highlights a SPECIFIC INSTANCE within a broader statement
├── "La gratuité bénéficierait à de nombreuses catégories de la population,
│    notamment les étudiants et les personnes âgées."
└── The example is EMBEDDED within the sentence — concise and targeted

RULE: "Par exemple" for DEVELOPED examples (1+ sentences)
      "Notamment" for QUICK, EMBEDDED examples (within a sentence)

Application to Lucia's text:

BEFORE: "Les personnes défavorisées pourraient se déplacer librement.
         Elles accéderaient plus facilement à l'emploi."
         (Claim without concrete illustration)

AFTER:  "Les personnes défavorisées pourraient se déplacer librement.
         À titre d'exemple, un demandeur d'emploi résidant en banlieue
         pourrait se rendre à des entretiens sans se soucier du coût
         du transport."
         (Concrete, vivid, specific illustration)

🔵 WHAT COULD BE DONE

Option A: Memorize ALL connectors from all 5 functions and use as many as possible

  • → Overload → Text feels mechanical and over-connected
  • → Every sentence starting with a connector = unnatural rhythm
  • → Quality of connector usage > Quantity of connectors used

Option B: Master 2–3 connectors per function and rotate them across responses

  • → This is the OPTIMAL strategy
  • → Gives you ~15 total connectors across all functions
  • → Combined with Chapter 4's causal connectors → ~25 total tools
  • → Sufficient variety for any TEF Section B response
  • → Manageable memorization load

Option C: Use only sequence connectors and ignore the other functions

  • → Sequence alone creates a numbered list, not an argument
  • → "D'abord... Ensuite... Enfin..." without consequence or addition feels robotic
  • → You need MULTIPLE functions working together for natural flow

Option D: Use connectors only at the start of paragraphs, not within them

  • → This creates connection BETWEEN sections but not WITHIN them
  • → Intra-paragraph cohesion requires connectors INSIDE paragraphs too
  • → Both inter-paragraph AND intra-paragraph connectors are needed
  • → Chapter 19 will deepen intra-paragraph cohesion — this chapter provides the basic tools

2. Apply the "Maximum 1 Connector Per Sentence" Rule

CONNECTOR DENSITY RULES:
│
├── MAXIMUM: 1 connector per sentence (at sentence start OR mid-sentence)
│   ✅ "Par conséquent, les émissions diminueraient."
│   ✅ "Les émissions diminueraient ainsi de manière significative."
│
├── NOT EVERY sentence needs a connector
│   ✅ Some sentences can stand alone — this creates rhythm
│   Aim for connectors in approximately 60–70% of your sentences
│   The remaining 30–40% flow naturally without explicit connectors
│
├── NEVER stack two connectors:
│   ❌ "De plus, par conséquent, la pollution diminuerait."
│   → "De plus" (addition) + "par conséquent" (consequence) = logical confusion
│   → Is this an ADDITIONAL point or a RESULTING point? → Reader cannot tell
│
└── EXCEPTION: "Non seulement... mais aussi..." is a PAIRED connector
    → It counts as ONE connector unit, not two
    → ✅ "Non seulement cette mesure réduirait la pollution, mais elle favoriserait
          également l'égalité sociale."

3. Map Connectors to Your Response Structure

WHERE EACH FUNCTION TYPICALLY APPEARS:

INTRODUCTION:
├── Rarely needs connectors (it is only 2–3 sentences)
├── The thesis sentence may use NO connector — it stands as a declaration
└── Exception: If the introduction has 3 sentences, a light connector
    between sentences 1 and 2 is acceptable (e.g., "En effet," / "Face à...")

BODY — ARGUMENT 1:
├── SEQUENCE: "Tout d'abord, ..." (opens Argument 1)
├── CAUSE (Chapter 4): "car" / "puisque" (links reason to claim)
├── CONSEQUENCE: "Par conséquent, ..." / "Ainsi, ..." (shows the result)
└── ILLUSTRATION: "Par exemple, ..." / "Notamment ..." (provides evidence)

TRANSITION BETWEEN ARGUMENTS:
├── ADDITION: "Par ailleurs, ..." / "De plus, ..." / "En outre, ..."
├── Or SEQUENCE: "Ensuite, ..." / "En second lieu, ..."
└── Pick ONE — do not use both an addition AND a sequence connector for the same transition

BODY — ARGUMENT 2:
├── CAUSE (Chapter 4): Different connector than Argument 1
├── CONSEQUENCE: Different connector than Argument 1
├── ILLUSTRATION: If used in Argument 1, use different expression here
└── OPPOSITION (if applicable): "Cependant, ..." / "Toutefois, ..."

CONCLUSION:
├── SEQUENCE: "Enfin, ..." (if concluding a sequence) — BUT see warning below
├── Conclusion-specific openers (from Chapter 5): "En définitive, ..." / "En somme, ..."
├── OPPOSITION (for nuance): "Toutefois, ..." (in the ouverture)
└── WARNING: Do NOT use "Enfin" to start the conclusion if it sounds like
    "finally, one more argument" — "Enfin" can be ambiguous
    → Prefer "En définitive" / "En somme" for conclusion opening

5. Learn the Connector Placement Grammar Rules

CONNECTORS THAT START A SENTENCE (followed by comma):
├── Tout d'abord, ...
├── Ensuite, ...
├── Enfin, ...
├── De plus, ...
├── En outre, ...
├── Par ailleurs, ...
├── Cependant, ...
├── Toutefois, ...
├── Néanmoins, ...
├── Par conséquent, ...
├── En revanche, ...
├── Par exemple, ...
├── Ainsi, ... (when at sentence start)
└── C'est pourquoi ... (NO comma after — directly followed by subject)

CONNECTORS THAT GO MID-SENTENCE:
├── ... donc ... (usually after subject or verb)
│   ✅ "Cette mesure serait donc bénéfique."
├── ... ainsi ... (can go after verb)
│   ✅ "La pollution diminuerait ainsi de manière notable."
├── ... également ... (after verb)
│   ✅ "Cette politique favoriserait également l'égalité sociale."
├── ... notamment ... (before the specific element)
│   ✅ "...de nombreux groupes, notamment les étudiants."
└── ... en particulier ... (before the specific element)
    ✅ "...les populations vulnérables, en particulier les personnes âgées."

CONNECTORS THAT NEVER START A SENTENCE:
├── "car" → NEVER at sentence start (Chapter 4 rule — still applies)
├── "donc" → CAN start a sentence in informal French but better mid-sentence in formal
└── "or" → CAN start a sentence (formal) — but rare in TEF Section B

COMMON GRAMMAR ERRORS WITH CONNECTORS:
├── ❌ "Cependant la pollution augmente." (missing comma after "Cependant")
├── ✅ "Cependant, la pollution augmente."
├── ❌ "C'est pourquoi, cette mesure est nécessaire." (unnecessary comma)
├── ✅ "C'est pourquoi cette mesure est nécessaire."
└── These comma rules matter for Dimension 4 (Grammar)

❌ WHAT SHOULD NOT DO

1. Do NOT Use "Mais" as Your Primary Opposition Connector

  • "Mais" is the A2-level equivalent of "but"
  • In formal written French, "mais" at the start of a sentence sounds basic
  • ❌ "Mais cette mesure coûterait cher."
  • ✅ "Cependant, cette mesure impliquerait un coût considérable."
  • "Mais" is acceptable mid-sentence: "...efficace mais coûteuse"
  • "Mais" should NOT be your go-to opposition connector in Section B

2. Do NOT Use "Et" to Connect Arguments

  • "Et" (and) is the most basic connector in French
  • Using "et" between arguments = no logical relationship expressed
  • ❌ "La gratuité réduirait la pollution et elle favoriserait l'égalité."
  • ✅ "La gratuité réduirait la pollution. Par ailleurs, elle favoriserait l'égalité sociale."
  • "Et" is acceptable WITHIN a sentence for listing items
  • "Et" should NOT be the bridge between major arguments

3. Do NOT Use the Same Connector Twice in One Response

  • This mirrors Chapter 3's rule (no repeated opinion markers) and Chapter 4's rule (no repeated causal connectors)
  • ❌ "Tout d'abord... Par ailleurs... Par ailleurs..." (repeated)
  • ✅ "Tout d'abord... Par ailleurs... De surcroît..." (varied)
  • Variety across ALL connector functions = Dimension 3 (vocabulary) maximized
  • The evaluator notices repetition — even connector repetition

4. Do NOT Use Connectors That You Cannot Define

  • If you are not sure what "dès lors" means → Do NOT use it
  • A misused connector is WORSE than no connector
  • ❌ "Les transports seraient gratuits. Dès lors, les prix augmenteraient."
    → "Dès lors" means "from that point / consequently" — but higher prices are not a CONSEQUENCE of free transport in this logic → Misuse
  • Only use connectors whose EXACT meaning and function you understand

5. Do NOT Start Every Sentence With a Connector

  • If every sentence begins with a connector, the text sounds like a checklist
❌ OVER-CONNECTED TEXT:
"Tout d'abord, la gratuité réduirait la pollution.
 En effet, les gens utiliseraient moins leur voiture.
 Par conséquent, les émissions diminueraient.
 De plus, la gratuité favoriserait l'égalité.
 En effet, les personnes défavorisées pourraient se déplacer.
 Par exemple, un demandeur d'emploi pourrait se rendre à des entretiens.
 En définitive, cette mesure serait bénéfique."

→ Every single sentence starts with a connector
→ Sounds robotic and mechanical
→ No sentence stands on its own
→ Rhythm is monotonous
✅ NATURALLY CONNECTED TEXT:
"Tout d'abord, la gratuité des transports réduirait considérablement la pollution
 urbaine, car les citoyens privilégieraient les bus et les métros au détriment
 de leur véhicule personnel. Les émissions de CO2 diminueraient ainsi de manière
 significative. Par ailleurs, cette mesure favoriserait l'égalité sociale.
 Les personnes à revenus modestes pourraient se déplacer librement, ce qui
 faciliterait notamment leur accès à l'emploi. En définitive, la gratuité
 des transports constituerait une avancée à la fois écologique et sociale."

→ Some sentences have connectors, some do not
→ Natural rhythm with variation
→ Connectors appear where logic DEMANDS them
→ Other sentences flow naturally without forced linking

6. Do NOT Confuse Addition and Consequence

COMMON CONFUSION:

"La gratuité réduirait la pollution. De plus, l'air serait plus pur."
→ "De plus" (addition) suggests a NEW, SEPARATE point
→ But "purer air" is a RESULT of reduced pollution, not a separate point
→ CORRECT: "Par conséquent, l'air serait plus pur." (consequence)

TEST: Ask yourself — "Is the second statement a RESULT of the first,
      or a SEPARATE point that supports the same thesis?"
├── If RESULT → Use consequence connector (donc, ainsi, par conséquent)
└── If SEPARATE POINT → Use addition connector (de plus, par ailleurs, en outre)

⏱️ WHEN TO USE WHAT

SituationWhich Function to Use
Starting your FIRST argument in the body→ SEQUENCE: "Tout d'abord, ..."
Explaining WHY your argument is true→ CAUSE (Chapter 4): "car" / "puisque" / "en raison de"
Showing WHAT RESULTS from your argument→ CONSEQUENCE: "Par conséquent, ..." / "Ainsi, ..."
Providing a concrete EXAMPLE→ ILLUSTRATION: "Par exemple, ..." / "Notamment ..."
Moving to your SECOND argument→ ADDITION: "Par ailleurs, ..." or SEQUENCE: "Ensuite, ..."
Acknowledging a weakness in your argument→ OPPOSITION: "Cependant, ..." / "Toutefois, ..."
Adding nuance to your conclusion→ OPPOSITION: "Toutefois, il convient de noter que..."
Linking the conclusion back to the introduction→ CONSEQUENCE: "C'est pourquoi..."
Embedding a quick example within a sentence→ ILLUSTRATION: "notamment" / "en particulier"
Showing that your second point is EVEN STRONGER→ ADDITION: "De surcroît, ..." / "Qui plus est, ..."

🚫 WHEN NOT TO USE

When NOT to Use Sequence Connectors

  • If you have only ONE argument (mini-response from Chapter 6)
    • → "Tout d'abord" implies a sequence → If there is no "ensuite," the reader expects one
    • → In a mini-response with 1 argument, skip sequence connectors
    • → Use a different opening: "En effet, ..." or directly state the argument

When NOT to Use Opposition Connectors

  • In your FIRST response attempts, avoid opposition if you are not yet comfortable with Chapter 14 (Thèse-Antithèse) and Chapter 16 (Concession)
  • A poorly placed opposition connector can CONFUSE your position
  • If the evaluator cannot tell whether you are FOR or AGAINST after reading an opposition connector → Dimension 1 risk
  • Master addition, sequence, and consequence FIRST → Add opposition later

When NOT to Use Illustration Connectors

  • When you do not have a concrete, specific example ready
  • ❌ "Par exemple, beaucoup de gens pensent cela." → This is NOT an example — it is a vague generality
  • If your "example" is actually a general statement → Do NOT introduce it with "par exemple"
  • Wait for Chapter 9 to master the art of concrete examples → Then use illustration connectors confidently

⚙️ HOW IT ONLY WORKS (INTERNAL MECHANICS)

The Evaluator's Connector Assessment Process

EVALUATOR'S PROCESSING:
│
├── SCAN 1: Are connectors PRESENT?
│   ├── YES → Proceed to quality assessment
│   └── NO → Dimension 2 (cohesion) immediately flagged as weak
│
├── SCAN 2: Are connectors VARIED?
│   ├── Multiple functions used (sequence + cause + consequence + illustration)
│   │   → Dimension 3 (vocabulary) positive signal
│   ├── Only one function used (e.g., only "de plus" repeated)
│   │   → Dimension 3 limited range signal
│   └── Only basic connectors (mais, et, parce que)
│       → Dimension 3 basic level signal
│
├── SCAN 3: Are connectors CORRECTLY USED?
│   ├── Logical relationship matches the connector function?
│   │   ├── "Par conséquent" used for actual consequence → Correct ✅
│   │   └── "Par conséquent" used for addition → Incorrect ❌
│   ├── Grammar rules followed? (comma placement, sentence position)
│   │   ├── "Cependant, ..." with comma → Correct ✅
│   │   └── "Cependant ..." without comma → Error ❌
│   └── Register appropriate? (formal connectors in formal text)
│       ├── "Toutefois" in argumentative text → Appropriate ✅
│       └── "Mais bon" in argumentative text → Inappropriate ❌
│
└── SCAN 4: Do connectors create genuine FLOW?
    ├── Reader can follow the argument without effort → High cohesion score
    ├── Reader has to re-read to understand connections → Moderate cohesion
    └── Reader is confused by connector misuse → Low cohesion score

How Connectors Create Flow Mechanically

WITHOUT CONNECTORS (Lucia's original):
│
├── Sentence A: [Statement]
├── Sentence B: [Statement]
├── Sentence C: [Statement]
│
├── Reader's brain must:
│   ├── Read Sentence A → Store in memory
│   ├── Read Sentence B → Store in memory
│   ├── INDEPENDENTLY determine relationship between A and B
│   ├── Read Sentence C → Store in memory
│   ├── INDEPENDENTLY determine relationship between B and C
│   └── INDEPENDENTLY determine relationship between A, B, and C together
│
└── This is HIGH COGNITIVE LOAD on the reader
    The reader does YOUR job of organizing the logic
    The evaluator penalizes YOU for making them work harder

WITH CONNECTORS:
│
├── Sentence A: [Statement]
├── "Par conséquent," Sentence B: [Result of A]
├── "Par ailleurs," Sentence C: [New angle, same position]
│
├── Reader's brain:
│   ├── Read Sentence A → Store
│   ├── See "Par conséquent" → INSTANTLY know B is a result of A
│   ├── Read Sentence B → Connect to A effortlessly
│   ├── See "Par ailleurs" → INSTANTLY know C is a new angle
│   ├── Read Sentence C → Connect to overall argument effortlessly
│   └── NO independent work needed — YOU did the organizing
│
└── This is LOW COGNITIVE LOAD on the reader
    The reader follows YOUR logic instead of building their own
    The evaluator rewards YOU for making the text easy to follow

The Interaction Between All Connector Types in a Full Response

COMPLETE CONNECTOR MAP OF A FULL RESPONSE:

INTRODUCTION:
"De nos jours, ... [NO CONNECTOR — opening statement]
 [problématique — may use light connector or none]
 [thesis — NO CONNECTOR — declaration stands alone]"

BODY — ARGUMENT 1:
"Tout d'abord, ... [SEQUENCE — opens first argument]
 ...car... [CAUSE from Ch.4 — links to reason]
 En effet, ... [CAUSE ELABORATION from Ch.4 — develops reason]
 Par conséquent, ... [CONSEQUENCE — shows result]
 Par exemple, ... [ILLUSTRATION — provides evidence]"

TRANSITION:
"Par ailleurs, ... [ADDITION — signals shift to new angle]"

BODY — ARGUMENT 2:
"...puisque... [CAUSE from Ch.4 — different connector than Arg 1]
 Cela signifie que... [CAUSE ELABORATION from Ch.4]
 Ainsi, ... [CONSEQUENCE — different connector than Arg 1]

VARIETY: No connector repeated
THIS = Maximum Dimension 2 + Dimension 3 score potential

🕳️ COMMON PITFALLS AND FAILURE MODES

Pitfall 1: The Connector Carpet Bombing

  • What happens: Candidate uses 15+ connectors in a 200-word response
  • Why it happens: Candidate learned connectors and wants to demonstrate ALL of them
  • How to prevent: Aim for ~10–12 connectors in a full response → Approximately 1 per 20 words → Not every sentence needs one
  • The rule: If removing a connector does not change the logical clarity → It was unnecessary → Remove it

Pitfall 2: The Wrong Function Selection

  • What happens: Candidate uses an addition connector where a consequence connector belongs (or vice versa)
  • Example: "Les gens utiliseraient les transports en commun. De plus, la pollution diminuerait."
  • → "De plus" (addition) is wrong here — less pollution is a RESULT of more transport use, not a separate point
  • → Correct: "Par conséquent, la pollution diminuerait."
  • How to prevent: Before inserting any connector, ask: "What is the LOGICAL RELATIONSHIP between these two sentences?" → Match the relationship to the correct function

Pitfall 3: The Register Clash

  • What happens: Candidate mixes very formal connectors with informal language
  • Example: "De surcroît, c'est vraiment super pour les gens."
  • → "De surcroît" (very formal) + "vraiment super" (very informal) = register clash
  • How to prevent: Match connector register to sentence register
  • → If using formal connectors → Ensure the rest of the sentence is also formal

Pitfall 4: The "Enfin" Confusion

  • What happens: Candidate uses "Enfin" to start the conclusion
  • Why it is risky: "Enfin" can mean "finally" (end of a sequence) OR "lastly, one more point"
  • → If the evaluator reads "Enfin" as "one more point" → They expect another argument, not a conclusion
  • How to prevent: Use dedicated conclusion openers from Chapter 5 ("En définitive," "En somme," "Pour conclure,") instead of "Enfin" for the conclusion

Pitfall 5: The Missing Transition Between Arguments

  • What happens: Argument 1 ends and Argument 2 begins with no linking connector
  • Why it happens: Candidate focuses on developing each argument internally but forgets the BRIDGE between them
  • How to prevent: Always place an ADDITION or SEQUENCE connector at the start of Argument 2
  • → "Par ailleurs, ..." / "Ensuite, ..." / "De surcroît, ..." / "En outre, ..."
  • This single connector between arguments dramatically improves perceived cohesion

Pitfall 6: The "Or" Misunderstanding

  • What happens: Candidate uses "or" thinking it means "or" (the English word)
  • Reality: French "or" ≠ English "or"
  • → French "or" = "now" / "however" (introduces a fact that changes direction)
  • → English "or" = French "ou"
  • → This is a false friend trap
  • How to prevent: If you mean "or" (choice between two things) → Use "ou"
  • → If you want to use French "or" (direction change) → Make sure the following statement CHANGES the logical direction

└── Time: 15 minutes per text

├── Write responses WITHOUT connectors
├── Re-read and INSERT appropriate connectors
├── Check: Is the function correct? Is the grammar correct?
└── Time: 10 minutes per response

STAGE 3: Connector Integration (automatic skill)
├── Write full responses WITH connectors from the start
├── Connectors should flow naturally during writing, not be inserted after
├── This is the GOAL — automatic, natural connector usage
└── Typically achieved after 15–20 full practice responses

├── Replace basic connectors with more nuanced alternatives
├── "De plus" → "De surcroît" / "Qui plus est"
├── "Cependant" → "Néanmoins" / "Il n'en demeure pas moins que"
└── This is register ELEVATION — same function, higher vocabulary level

🔑 KEY TAKEAWAY

What Chapter 7 Established

Connectors serve 5 logical functions beyond causality (Chapter 4):

FUNCTION 1 — SEQUENCE: Ordering arguments
├── Tout d'abord → Ensuite → Enfin
└── Purpose: Creates a roadmap for the reader

FUNCTION 2 — ADDITION: Adding more support
├── De plus / Par ailleurs / En outre / De surcroît
└── Purpose: Shows additional evidence for the same position

FUNCTION 3 — OPPOSITION: Introducing contrast or objection
├── Cependant / Toutefois / Néanmoins / En revanche
└── Purpose: Acknowledges complexity and counterarguments

FUNCTION 4 — CONSEQUENCE: Showing results
├── Par conséquent / Ainsi / C'est pourquoi / De ce fait
└── Purpose: Makes the OUTCOME of your argument explicit

FUNCTION 5 — ILLUSTRATION: Introducing examples
├── Par exemple / Notamment / À titre d'exemple
└── Purpose: Provides concrete evidence

Key rules:
├── Maximum 1 connector per sentence
├── Not every sentence needs a connector (~60–70% is optimal)
├── Never repeat the same connector in one response
├── Match connector function to ACTUAL logical relationship
├── Match connector register to sentence register
├── Full response should use 4–5 different functions
└── Chapter 4 (Cause) + Chapter 7 (5 other functions) = Complete toolkit
