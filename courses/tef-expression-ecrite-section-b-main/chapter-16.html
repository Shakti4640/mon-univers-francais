📘 CHAPTER 16

THE ART OF CONCESSION: AGREEING PARTIALLY TO STRENGTHEN YOUR POSITION

🔴 HYPOTHETICAL PROBLEM

You are a TEF candidate named Fatima
Fatima has completed Chapters 1–15 diligently
She understands the scoring grid (Chapter 1), can decode prompts (Chapter 2), formulates clear opinions (Chapter 3), builds causal chains (Chapter 15), and structures her body in Thèse-Antithèse format (Chapter 14)
She writes a practice response to this prompt:

"Certains pensent que les études universitaires ne sont plus nécessaires pour réussir professionnellement. Partagez-vous cette opinion? Justifiez votre réponse."

Fatima's Thèse paragraph (FOR university studies):

"Les études universitaires sont absolument essentielles. Elles permettent d'acquérir des connaissances approfondies. De plus, elles offrent un diplôme reconnu par les employeurs. Par conséquent, il est impossible de réussir sans aller à l'université."

Fatima's Antithèse paragraph (AGAINST — the opposing view):

"Cependant, certaines personnes réussissent sans diplôme. Par exemple, des entrepreneurs comme Steve Jobs n'ont pas terminé leurs études. Néanmoins, ces cas sont exceptionnels et ne représentent pas la majorité."

Her grammar → correct
Her structure → clear Thèse-Antithèse (Chapter 14)
Her connectors → varied (Chapter 13)
Her causal logic → present (Chapter 15)
But her evaluator writes this comment: "Argumentation manque de nuance. Position trop catégorique. Raisonnement binaire."
Translation: "Argumentation lacks nuance. Position too categorical. Binary reasoning."
Fatima scored adequately but NOT well on Dimension 2 — and she does not understand why

❓ THE CORE QUESTION

  • Why does a clearly structured Thèse-Antithèse response still get criticized for lacking nuance?
  • What is the difference between ACKNOWLEDGING the other side and CONCEDING to it strategically?
  • How do you agree with your opponent partially WITHOUT weakening your own position?
  • What are the French expressions that create this sophisticated "yes, but actually..." effect?
  • How does concession transform a B1-level argument into a B2–C1-level argument?

🟡 HOW TO APPROACH IT

Step 1: Diagnose Fatima's Actual Problem

  • Fatima's Thèse paragraph is ABSOLUTE:
    • → "absolument essentielles"
    • → "impossible de réussir sans"
    • → No room for ANY exception, ANY doubt, ANY nuance
  • Fatima's Antithèse paragraph MENTIONS the opposing view but DISMISSES it immediately:
    • → "ces cas sont exceptionnels et ne représentent pas la majorité"
    • → She acknowledges Steve Jobs but slams the door shut instantly
  • The evaluator sees BLACK vs. WHITE thinking
  • There is no GREY zone — no moment where Fatima shows she can HOLD two ideas simultaneously

Step 2: Understand What "Nuance" Means in French Argumentation

  • In the French academic tradition, argumentation is NOT about being the loudest voice
  • It is about being the WISEST voice
  • A wise voice:
    • → Recognizes complexity
    • → Admits that the opposing view has SOME validity
    • → Then explains WHY their own position is STILL stronger despite that partial validity
  • This is called CONCESSION (la concession)

Step 3: Understand the Psychological Power of Concession

  • When you say "The other side is completely wrong" → The evaluator thinks: "This person cannot see complexity"
  • When you say "The other side has a point, BUT my position remains stronger because..." → The evaluator thinks: "This person thinks like an intellectual"
  • Concession does not WEAKEN your argument — it STRENGTHENS it
  • WHY? → Because you demonstrate that even AFTER considering the opposition, your position still holds
  • It is like a bridge that has been tested with weight → More trustworthy than a bridge that has never been tested

Step 4: Understand Where Concession Lives in the Response Structure

  • Refer to Chapter 14 (Thèse-Antithèse):
CHAPTER 14 STRUCTURE:
  Introduction
  Paragraph 1: Thèse (your main position)
  Paragraph 2: Antithèse (opposing view)
  Conclusion

CHAPTER 16 UPGRADE — CONCESSION CAN APPEAR IN THREE LOCATIONS:
  Location A: INSIDE your Thèse paragraph (conceding a weakness of your own side)
  Location B: INSIDE your Antithèse paragraph (conceding a strength of the other side before countering)
  Location C: As a TRANSITION between Thèse and Antithèse paragraphs
  • Location B is the most common and most powerful
  • Location A shows extraordinary intellectual maturity
  • Location C creates smooth structural flow

  • → Recommended ONLY for C1 targeting with careful balance
  • → THIS IS THE MASTERY TARGET

✅ WHAT SHOULD DO

1. Master the TWO-PART Concession Structure

  • Every concession has exactly TWO parts:
PART 1: THE CONCESSION (what you admit/acknowledge)
PART 2: THE COUNTER-ARGUMENT (why your position still holds)
  • PART 2 must ALWAYS be longer and stronger than PART 1
  • This is the GOLDEN RULE of concession
  • If Part 1 is stronger than Part 2, you have accidentally argued AGAINST yourself

Visual Weight Rule:

❌ WRONG BALANCE:
  "Bien que l'université offre des connaissances approfondies, un réseau professionnel,
   une culture générale et des opportunités internationales,
   certains réussissent sans diplôme."
  → Concession (Part 1) = 18 words | Counter (Part 2) = 5 words
  → You just DESTROYED your own argument

✅ CORRECT BALANCE:
  "Bien que certains autodidactes réussissent brillamment,
   les études universitaires demeurent essentielles car elles offrent
   des connaissances structurées, un diplôme reconnu et un réseau professionnel
   indispensable dans un marché du travail de plus en plus compétitif."
  → Concession (Part 1) = 5 words | Counter (Part 2) = 25 words
  → Your argument is STRENGTHENED by the concession

2. Learn the Three Placement Strategies

Strategy 1: Concession INSIDE the Thèse Paragraph

You are arguing FOR something → You briefly admit a limitation of your own side → You immediately override it

POSITION: Les études universitaires sont essentielles.
CONCESSION INSIDE THÈSE:
  "Certes, le coût des études peut constituer un obstacle pour certaines familles.
   Mais les bénéfices à long terme — un emploi stable, un salaire supérieur,
   une ouverture intellectuelle — dépassent largement cet investissement initial."
  • Effect → You showed you are aware of the weakness of your own argument
  • Effect → You neutralized it BEFORE your opponent could use it against you
  • Evaluator reaction → "This candidate thinks ahead — intellectual maturity"

Strategy 2: Concession as TRANSITION Between Paragraphs

Your Thèse paragraph ends → Instead of simply saying "Cependant" → You create a concessive bridge

END OF THÈSE: "...les études universitaires offrent donc des avantages indéniables."

CONCESSIVE BRIDGE:
  "Il serait toutefois excessif d'affirmer que l'université est l'unique voie
   vers la réussite. En effet, d'autres parcours méritent d'être considérés."

START OF ANTITHÈSE: "Certains entrepreneurs, par exemple, ont bâti..."
  • Effect → The transition is SMOOTH, not abrupt
  • Effect → You demonstrated that your shift to the opposing view is THOUGHTFUL, not mechanical
  • Evaluator reaction → "Excellent cohesion between paragraphs — well achieved on Dimension 2"

Strategy 3: Concession INSIDE the Antithèse Paragraph

You present the opposing view → You concede it has some merit → You counter it powerfully

ANTITHÈSE WITH INTERNAL CONCESSION:
  "Il est vrai que certains individus réussissent remarquablement sans diplôme
   universitaire. On ne peut nier que l'entrepreneuriat et les formations
   professionnelles offrent des alternatives viables. Cependant, ces réussites
   demeurent exceptionnelles et ne sauraient constituer un modèle généralisable,
   car la grande majorité des professions qualifiées exigent des compétences
   que seul un cursus universitaire peut garantir."
  • Effect → You gave GENUINE weight to the opposition (not a strawman dismissal)
  • Effect → Your counter-argument is MORE convincing because it overcame a REAL objection
  • Evaluator reaction → "Nuanced argumentation — B2 to C1 level reasoning"

3. Build the "Certes...Mais" Reflex

  • Among ALL concession expressions, "Certes...mais" is the most versatile
  • It works in virtually ANY argument
  • It is instantly recognizable to French evaluators as a sophistication marker
  • If you learn ONLY ONE concession expression, make it this one

The "Certes...Mais" Template:

Certes, [brief acknowledgment of opposing point],
mais [your stronger counter-argument that outweighs the concession].

EnvironmentCertes, les énergies renouvelables coûtent cher à développer, mais elles représentent un investissement vital pour l'avenir.

4. Combine Concession With Causal Chains (Chapter 15 Integration)

  • This is the MOST POWERFUL combination in TEF Section B argumentation
  • You concede a point → Then use a causal chain to show why your counter-argument is stronger

Example of Concession + Causal Chain:

Bien que les réseaux sociaux permettent de maintenir le contact avec des proches éloignés, cette connectivité permanente engendre paradoxalement un isolement social. En effet, les interactions virtuelles remplacent progressivement les rencontres en personne. Cela entraîne un appauvrissement des relations humaines, car les échanges numériques manquent de profondeur émotionnelle. De ce fait, de nombreux jeunes souffrent d'un sentiment de solitude malgré leurs centaines de « contacts » en ligne.

Structural Analysis:

"...cette connectivité permanente engendre paradoxalement..."COUNTER (Part 2 — begins)Chapter 15 — Category C single-sentence cause-effect
"En effet, les interactions virtuelles remplacent..."CAUSE — root explanationChapter 13 — "En effet" connector
"Cela entraîne un appauvrissement..."EFFECT A in causal chainChapter 15 — Category B verb-based effect
"De ce fait, de nombreux jeunes souffrent..."EFFECT B — final consequenceChapter 15 — Category B connector-based effect
  • This single paragraph demonstrates: concession + causal chain + varied connectors + subjunctive grammar
  • It hits ALL FOUR scoring dimensions simultaneously

  • Refer to Category D above
  • These compress the entire concession into 3–5 words
  • Useful when you are approaching 250 words and need to add nuance without adding length

❌ WHAT SHOULD NOT DO

1. Do NOT Over-Concede

  • Maximum concessions per response: 2–3
  • If every paragraph contains a concession, your argument appears WEAK, not nuanced
  • You are not writing a philosophy thesis — you are writing a 200–250 word TEF response
  • One strong concession in the body is optimal
  • Two concessions (one in thèse, one in antithèse) is maximum
  • Three concessions is risky unless each is very brief (Category D compressed forms)

2. Do NOT Make the Concession Longer Than the Counter-Argument

  • Refer to the Golden Rule established above
  • Part 2 (counter) must ALWAYS outweigh Part 1 (concession)
  • If you find your concession expanding into 2–3 sentences, you are BUILDING the opponent's case for them
  • Stop → Cut the concession to one clause → Expand the counter instead

3. Do NOT Concede Your MAIN Thesis Point

  • You can concede SECONDARY aspects of the opposing view
  • You CANNOT concede the CENTRAL claim you are arguing against
❌ FATAL ERROR:
  Your position: "University education is essential"
  Your concession: "Certes, on peut réussir parfaitement sans diplôme universitaire..."
  → You just conceded the EXACT point you are supposed to argue against
  → Your entire argument collapses

✅ CORRECT:
  Your position: "University education is essential"
  Your concession: "Certes, le coût des études peut être un obstacle..."
  → You conceded a SIDE issue (cost), not the main claim
  → Your main argument remains intact

4. Do NOT Use "Bien que" With Indicative

  • This is the MOST COMMON grammar error with concession expressions
  • "Bien que" REQUIRES the subjunctive — always, without exception
  • ❌ "Bien que les gens pensent que..." → WRONG (indicative)
  • ✅ "Bien que les gens pensent que..." → Actually "pensent" looks identical here
  • Better example to illustrate the difference:
    • ❌ "Bien que cette idée est populaire..." → WRONG (indicative "est")
    • ✅ "Bien que cette idée soit populaire..." → CORRECT (subjunctive "soit")
    • ❌ "Bien que les jeunes peuvent accéder..." → WRONG (indicative "peuvent")
    • ✅ "Bien que les jeunes puissent accéder..." → CORRECT (subjunctive "puissent")

5. Do NOT Confuse "Même si" and "Bien que"

ExpressionMood RequiredRegisterNuance
Même siINDICATIVENeutral to formalPresents a REAL, factual situation being conceded
Bien queSUBJUNCTIVEFormal to very formalPresents a situation whose reality is ACKNOWLEDGED but minimized
  • They are NOT interchangeable grammatically
  • Using subjunctive after "même si" → grammar error → Dimension 4 penalty
  • Using indicative after "bien que" → grammar error → Dimension 4 penalty

6. Do NOT Place Concession in the Conclusion

  • The conclusion is for SYNTHESIS and REINFORCEMENT (Chapter 11)
  • Introducing a NEW concession in the conclusion:
    • → Opens a debate you cannot close (no space left)
    • → Weakens your final impression
    • → Confuses the evaluator about your actual position
  • Concessions belong in the BODY paragraphs ONLY

7. Do NOT Use "Malgré que"

  • Many French learners assume "malgré que" exists as a combination of "malgré" + "que"
  • "Malgré que" is considered INCORRECT by most French grammarians and TEF evaluators
  • It is sometimes used in spoken French but is stigmatized in formal writing
  • → Use "malgré" + NOUN: "Malgré les difficultés..." ✅
  • → Use "bien que" + SUBJUNCTIVE for a clause: "Bien que les difficultés soient réelles..." ✅
  • → NEVER: "Malgré que les difficultés soient réelles..." ❌

🚫 WHEN NOT TO USE

When NOT to Use Concession

  • In the Introduction

    • → The introduction FRAMES the debate (Chapter 10) — it does not concede anything yet
    • → You have not even stated your position — conceding before arguing is illogical
    • → Exception: A very brief "Si certains estiment que..., d'autres considèrent que..." is acceptable as framing but this is PRESENTATION, not concession

  • In the Conclusion

    • → As stated above — the conclusion reinforces (Chapter 11), it does not introduce new doubts
    • → The ONLY acceptable conclusion-adjacent concession is a brief "ouverture" that gestures toward complexity: "Bien que ma position soit claire, la question mérite une réflexion continue."
    • → Even this is risky and should be used only by advanced candidates

  • When Your Argument Is Already Very Short

    • → If your total response is under 150 words, spending 20–30 words on concession steals space from your actual arguments
    • → Build strong arguments FIRST → Add concession only if you have room
    • → Refer to Chapter 6 (mini-response) → At that level, concession is premature

  • When You Are Not Sure of the Subjunctive Form

    • → If you are unsure whether you can conjugate the subjunctive correctly after "bien que"
    • → Do NOT attempt it — the grammar error costs more than the sophistication gains
    • → Use "même si" + indicative or "certes...mais" instead — SAFE alternatives that still show concession

⚙️ HOW IT ONLY WORKS (INTERNAL MECHANICS)

The Cognitive Architecture of Concession

WITHOUT CONCESSION (Chapters 1–15):
  YOUR SIDE → Strong arguments → YOUR SIDE wins
  OPPONENT'S SIDE → Mentioned briefly → Dismissed
  
  Problem: The evaluator sees two SEPARATE blocks
  Problem: No interaction between the sides
  Problem: Feels like two monologues, not a dialogue

WITH CONCESSION (Chapter 16):
  YOUR SIDE → Strong arguments
    ↕ (concession creates INTERACTION)
  OPPONENT'S SIDE → Acknowledged as partially valid
    ↕ (counter-argument creates RESOLUTION)
  YOUR SIDE → Wins MORE convincingly because it survived a challenge
  
  Result: The evaluator sees INTELLECTUAL ENGAGEMENT
  Result: Your argument was TESTED and SURVIVED
  Result: This IS the definition of nuanced argumentation

Why Concession Works Psychologically on the Evaluator

TechniqueEvaluator's Subconscious Reaction
No concession — absolute statements only"This person sees the world in black and white — limited thinker"
Concession too strong — undermines own argument"This person is confused about their own position"
Concession balanced — acknowledges then overrides"This person is intellectually mature — they can hold complexity"

The Grammatical Engine Behind Each Category

CATEGORY A (Subjunctive Concession):
  Trigger word: bien que / quoique / encore que
  → FORCES subjunctive in the subordinate clause
  → Main clause uses indicative (normal)
  → Brain must process: concessive meaning + mood shift = high cognitive load
  → This is WHY evaluators reward it — it proves grammatical control under pressure

CATEGORY B (Indicative Concession):
  Trigger words: même si / il est vrai que / certes
  → Uses indicative throughout (no mood shift)
  → Concessive meaning comes from VOCABULARY, not GRAMMAR
  → Safer but less impressive grammatically
  → Still highly effective for Dimension 2 (coherence/nuance)

CATEGORY C (Verbal Concession):
  Trigger phrases: on ne peut nier / il faut reconnaître / force est de constater
  → Concessive meaning embedded in the VERB PHRASE itself
  → Grammar follows standard indicative rules
  → Sophistication comes from LEXICAL RICHNESS (Dimension 3)

CATEGORY D (Prepositional Concession):
  Trigger words: malgré / en dépit de / nonobstant
  → Followed by NOUN only (never a clause)
  → Concession compressed into minimum words
  → No grammar risk — pure vocabulary play
  → Useful for word-count economy

How the Evaluator Scores Concession Across All Four Dimensions

DimensionHow Concession Impacts It
Dimension 1 (Consigne)Shows you understood the prompt asks for JUSTIFIED opinion, not just opinion
Dimension 2 (Cohérence/Cohésion)Creates logical nuance + paragraph transitions + interactive argumentation
Dimension 3 (Lexique)Introduces high-register vocabulary (certes, néanmoins, en dépit de, force est de constater)
Dimension 4 (Grammaire)Triggers subjunctive usage (bien que) + complex sentence structures
  • Concession is the ONLY technique that can positively impact ALL FOUR dimensions simultaneously
  • This is why it is considered the single most powerful argumentation tool in French writing

🕳️ COMMON PITFALLS AND FAILURE MODES

Pitfall 1: The Accidental Self-Destruction

  • Conceding the MAIN point of the opposition instead of a secondary point
  • Detection method → After writing your concession, ask: "Did I just agree with the exact thing I am arguing against?"
  • If yes → Delete and concede a SIDE issue instead

Pitfall 2: The Grammatical Trap of "Bien que"

  • Using indicative instead of subjunctive after "bien que"
  • Detection method → After writing "bien que," check: Is the next verb in subjunctive form?
  • Common verbs to watch:
    • être → soit (not "est")
    • avoir → ait (not "a")
    • pouvoir → puisse (not "peut")
    • faire → fasse (not "fait")
    • aller → aille (not "va")
    • savoir → sache (not "sait")
  • If unsure about ANY subjunctive form → Switch to "même si" + indicative → SAFE

Pitfall 3: The Double Concession Collapse

  • Conceding in both Thèse AND Antithèse paragraphs with equal weight
  • Effect → Your response sounds like you agree with EVERYONE and have no real position
  • Solution → If you concede in Thèse, make it BRIEF (one clause) → If you concede in Antithèse, make it the main concession with full counter-argument

Pitfall 4: The Missing Counter-Argument

  • Writing "Certes, la technologie a des avantages." → Then moving to a completely different topic
  • Problem → You conceded but never countered → The concession stands UNCHALLENGED
  • Rule → Every "Certes" MUST be followed by "mais/cependant/néanmoins/toutefois" within the SAME passage

Pitfall 5: The Mechanical "Certes...Mais" Repetition

  • Using "Certes...mais" three times in the same response
  • → Same problem as repeating "parce que" four times (Chapter 15)
  • → VARY your concession expressions just as you vary your causal expressions
  • → One "Certes...mais" + one "Bien que" + one "Malgré" = excellent variety

Pitfall 6: Conceding Something Irrelevant

  • The concession must be RELATED to the argument being made
  • ❌ "Certes, le temps est beau aujourd'hui, mais les réseaux sociaux posent problème."
  • → The concession about weather has NOTHING to do with social media
  • ✅ "Certes, les réseaux sociaux facilitent la communication, mais ils appauvrissent les relations humaines."
  • → The concession is directly RELEVANT to the argument

🔑 KEY TAKEAWAY

What Chapter 16 Established

CONCESSION = Acknowledging the opposing view's partial validity
              THEN overriding it with a stronger counter-argument

FOUR CATEGORIES OF CONCESSION EXPRESSION:
  Category A: Subjunctive triggers (bien que, quoique) → Highest sophistication
  Category B: Indicative structures (même si, certes...mais, il est vrai que...mais) → Safe and effective
  Category C: Verbal phrases (on ne peut nier que, il faut reconnaître que) → Lexically rich
  Category D: Prepositional compression (malgré, en dépit de) → Word-count efficient

THE GOLDEN RULE:
  Part 2 (counter-argument) must ALWAYS be longer and stronger than Part 1 (concession)

THREE PLACEMENT STRATEGIES:
  Inside Thèse → Conceding your own side's weakness (high maturity)
  As Transition → Bridging Thèse to Antithèse smoothly (high cohesion)
  Inside Antithèse → Conceding opponent's strength then overriding (most common)

THE ULTIMATE COMBINATION:
  Concession + Causal Chain (Chapter 15) = Maximum scoring potential
  One technique shows NUANCE → The other shows LOGIC → Together they are unstoppable

CRITICAL GRAMMAR RULES:
  "Bien que" → ALWAYS subjunctive
  "Même si" → ALWAYS indicative
  "Malgré" → ALWAYS followed by noun (never clause)
  "Malgré que" → NEVER use in TEF writing

CONCESSION IS THE ONLY TECHNIQUE THAT HITS ALL 4 SCORING DIMENSIONS AT ONCE
