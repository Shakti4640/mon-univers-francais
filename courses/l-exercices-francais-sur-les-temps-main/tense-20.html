Quick Reference

The participe passé (past participle) is one of the most essential forms in French. It is used in:

  • All compound tenses (passé composé, plus-que-parfait, futur antérieur, etc.)
  • Passive voice (La porte est fermée.)
  • As an adjective (une porte fermée)

Formation of Regular Past Participles:

Verb GroupRuleInfinitivePast Participle
-ER verbsRemove -er, add -éparlerparlé
-IR verbsRemove -ir, add -ifinirfini
-RE verbsRemove -re, add -uvendrevendu

Examples:

-ER → -é-IR → -i-RE → -u
manger → mangéchoisir → choisiattendre → attendu
danser → danséréussir → réussirépondre → répondu
travailler → travailléobéir → obéiperdre → perdu
écouter → écoutégrandir → grandidescendre → descendu

Quick Reference — Past Participle Agreement Rules

RULE 1: With ÊTRE (including DR MRS VANDERTRAMP verbs)

Past participle AGREES with the SUBJECT in gender and number.

SubjectExampleAgreement
ilIl est parti.masc. sing.
elleElle est partie.fem. sing. (+e)
ilsIls sont partis.masc. pl. (+s)
ellesElles sont parties.fem. pl. (+es)

RULE 2: With AVOIR — NO agreement with subject

Past participle does NOT agree with the subject when using avoir.

✅ Elle a mangé une pomme. (NOT mangée — no agreement with "elle")
✅ Ils ont fini le travail. (NOT finis)

RULE 3: With AVOIR — Agreement with PRECEDING Direct Object

Past participle AGREES with the direct object ONLY if it comes BEFORE the verb.

SituationExampleAgreement
D.O. after verbJ'ai mangé la pomme.NO agreement
D.O. before verb (pronoun)Je l'ai mangée.YES — la pomme = fem.
D.O. before verb (que)La pomme que j'ai mangée.YES — la pomme = fem.
D.O. before verb (quelle)Quelle pomme as-tu mangée ?YES

Agreement Endings:

MasculineFeminine
Singular— (no change)+ e
Plural+ s+ es

Example with "parti":

MasculineFeminine
Singularpartipartie
Pluralpartisparties

Quick Reference — Tricky Agreement Cases

Reflexive Verbs: When the Past Participle Does NOT Agree

The past participle of reflexive verbs agrees with the reflexive pronoun ONLY when it functions as a direct object.

If the reflexive pronoun is an indirect object, there is NO agreement.

How to tell the difference:

Ask: "She [verb] WHAT?" or "She [verb] WHOM?"

  • If the reflexive pronoun answers the question → it's a direct object → AGREE
  • If something else answers the question → the reflexive pronoun is an indirect object → NO AGREEMENT
ExampleAnalysisAgreement?
Elle s'est lavée.She washed HERSELF (se = D.O.)✅ YES
Elle s'est lavé les mains.She washed WHAT? les mains (se = I.O.)❌ NO
Elles se sont téléphoné.They phoned TO EACH OTHER (se = I.O.)❌ NO
Elles se sont regardées.They looked at EACH OTHER (se = D.O.)✅ YES
Elle s'est acheté une robe.She bought WHAT? une robe (se = I.O.)❌ NO
La robe qu'elle s'est achetée.She bought WHAT? que = la robe (preceding D.O.)✅ YES

Verbs where "se" is always INDIRECT:

These verbs take an indirect object in their non-reflexive form (verb + à + person), so "se" is always indirect:

VerbMeaningAgreement?
se parlerto talk to each other❌ NO
se téléphonerto call each other❌ NO
se sourireto smile at each other❌ NO
s'écrireto write to each other❌ NO
se mentirto lie to each other❌ NO
se plaireto please each other❌ NO
se ressemblerto resemble each other❌ NO
se succéderto follow each other❌ NO
se nuireto harm each other❌ NO
se rendre compteto realize❌ NO

Quick Reference — Advanced Agreement Cases

1. Agreement with "en"

When the preceding direct object is en, the past participle generally does NOT agree in standard French.

ExampleAgreement?
Des pommes ? J'en ai mangé.❌ NO (standard rule)
Des livres ? J'en ai lu trois.❌ NO

Note: Some grammarians accept agreement with "en" in literary French, but the standard rule is NO agreement.

2. Agreement with "combien," "que (de)," "lequel/laquelle"

ExampleAgreement?
Combien de pommes as-tu mangées ?✅ YES (combien refers to pommes, fem. pl.)
Laquelle as-tu choisie ?✅ YES (laquelle = fem. sing.)
Que de bêtises il a faites !✅ YES (que de bêtises = fem. pl.)

3. Agreement with Impersonal Verbs

Past participles of impersonal verbs (il faut, il y a, il pleut, il neige, il fait) NEVER agree.

ExampleAgreement?
La patience qu'il a fallu...❌ NO
Les chaleurs qu'il a fait...❌ NO
Les inondations qu'il y a eu...❌ NO

4. Agreement with Verbs of Measurement/Quantity

When the "direct object" expresses measurement, weight, duration, or price, it is actually an adverbial complement, NOT a true direct object. No agreement.

ExampleAgreement?Reason
Les deux kilos que ce sac a pesé...❌ NOweight = adverbial
Les vingt euros que ce livre a coûté...❌ NOprice = adverbial
Les trois heures que le film a duré...❌ NOduration = adverbial
Les kilomètres que nous avons couru...❌ NOdistance = adverbial
Les dangers que nous avons courus...✅ YEStrue direct object (dangers = what we ran/faced)

5. Agreement with "faire + infinitive" (Causative)

The past participle fait followed by an infinitive NEVER agrees.

ExampleAgreement?
Elle s'est fait couper les cheveux.❌ NO (never)
Je les ai fait venir.❌ NO (never)
La maison qu'il a fait construire.❌ NO (never)

6. Agreement with "laisser + infinitive"

Since the 1990 spelling reform, laissé before an infinitive also does NOT agree (following the model of "fait").

ExampleAgreement?
Elle s'est laissé tomber.❌ NO (reformed rule)
Je les ai laissé partir.❌ NO (reformed rule)

Note: Traditional grammar required agreement in some cases, but the simplified rule (no agreement before infinitive) is now standard.

FrenchEnglish

  1. Une fois le travail terminé et les outils rangés, les ouvriers sont rentrés chez eux, fatigués mais satisfaits.

Analysis: Absolute constructions: "le travail terminé" (masc. sing.), "les outils rangés" (masc. pl.). "rentrés" → être verb, agrees with "les ouvriers" (masc. pl.). "fatigués" and "satisfaits" → adjectives, agree with "les ouvriers."

  1. Elle s'est laissé convaincre, même si les arguments qu'elle avait entendus ne semblaient pas très convaincants.

Analysis: "laissé + infinitive" → never agrees (reformed rule). "entendus" → "que" = les arguments = masc. pl. = preceding D.O. Here, she heard the arguments directly (the arguments are the thing heard), so agreement applies. "convaincants" = adjectif verbal, agrees with "les arguments."

Exercise G — Analytical Essay (Sample Answer)

Past participle agreement is considered one of the most difficult aspects of French grammar because it involves a complex web of rules, exceptions, and special cases that even native speakers struggle with. The core rules are relatively straightforward: with être, the participle agrees with the subject; with avoir, it agrees with a preceding direct object. However, complications arise in numerous scenarios.

Reflexive verbs require speakers to determine whether the reflexive pronoun is a direct or indirect object — a distinction that demands knowledge of verb transitivity (e.g., "se laver" vs. "se laver les mains," "se téléphoner" vs. "se regarder"). Impersonal verbs, measurement verbs, and the pronoun "en" create additional exceptions where agreement is blocked despite the apparent presence of a preceding object.

The "fait + infinitive" rule (no agreement) was extended to "laissé + infinitive" by the 1990 spelling reform, reflecting a broader trend toward simplification. This reform acknowledged that the traditional rules were so complex that even educated native speakers routinely made errors — particularly in distinguishing agent from object with perception verbs, or in handling the reflexive D.O./I.O. distinction.

The fundamental difficulty is that agreement is primarily a feature of written French — in speech, the differences are often inaudible (mangé/mangée/mangés/mangées all sound identical). This means speakers have less natural reinforcement for correct written forms, making the rules feel arbitrary and hard to internalize. The evolution toward simplification reflects an ongoing tension between prescriptive grammatical tradition and the practical reality of how the language is used.
