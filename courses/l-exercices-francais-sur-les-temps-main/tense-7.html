Quick Reference

The futur proche (near future) describes an action that is about to happen or going to happen in the near future.

English equivalent: going to do something

Formation:
SUBJECT + ALLER (present tense) + INFINITIVE

Subjectaller (present)+ infinitive
jevaisparler
tuvasparler
il/elle/onvaparler
nousallonsparler
vousallezparler
ils/ellesvontparler

⚠️ This is one of the simplest tenses to form!

  • You only need to know the present tense of aller
  • The main verb stays in the infinitive (no conjugation needed)

Quick Reference — Negative Futur Proche

Structure: Subject + ne + aller (present) + pas + infinitive

AffirmativeNegative
Je vais manger.Je ne vais pas manger.
Elle va partir.Elle ne va pas partir.
Nous allons travailler.Nous **n'**allons pas travailler.

⚠️ The ne...pas wraps around aller, NOT around the infinitive!

Other negative structures:

NegativeExample
ne...plusJe ne vais plus manger ici.
ne...jamaisIl ne va jamais comprendre.
ne...rienElle ne va rien dire.
ne...personneNous n'allons voir personne.

Futur Proche with Reflexive Verbs

Structure: Subject + reflexive pronoun + aller + infinitive (with "se")

Wait — actually:
Subject + aller + se + infinitive — BUT the reflexive pronoun goes BEFORE the infinitive!

AffirmativeNegative
Je vais me lever.Je ne vais pas me lever.
Elle va se coucher.Elle ne va pas se coucher.
Nous allons nous promener.Nous **n'**allons pas nous promener.

⚠️ The reflexive pronoun matches the SUBJECT and goes directly before the infinitive.

Exercise G — Error Correction

  1. Je ne vais pas manger. (missing "ne")
  2. Elle ne va pas partir. (va, not vas — elle = 3rd person)
  3. Nous allons nous promener. (reflexive pronoun must match subject: nous, not se)
  4. Ils ne vont pas se lever tôt. (infinitive "lever," not conjugated "lèvent")
  5. Tu vas finir tes devoirs. (vas, not va — tu = 2nd person)
  6. Je ne vais pas me lever tôt. (ne...pas wraps around "aller," not after the reflexive)
  7. Il va aller au cinéma. (à + le = au, not "à le")
  8. Elles ne vont pas se coucher tard. (ne...pas wraps around "aller")

Quick Reference — Futur Proche vs. Futur Simple (Preview)

Futur ProcheFutur Simple (Tense #8)
Near / immediate futureGeneral / more distant future
Intention, plan, certaintyPrediction, promise, general future
Je vais manger à midi.Je mangerai demain.
I'm going to eat at noon.I will eat tomorrow.
Spoken French preferenceWritten/formal French preference
Subjective — speaker's perspectiveMore objective/neutral

⚠️ In modern spoken French, the futur proche is used FAR more frequently than the futur simple, even for events that are not immediate.

  1. Both are natural. "J'arrive" (present) is slightly more natural for something very immediate (I'm coming in 5 minutes).
  2. Both are natural. "Elle part demain" (present) is common for fixed plans; "Elle va partir demain" emphasizes intention.

Quick Reference — Futur Proche in Other Tenses

The futur proche can exist in OTHER tenses by conjugating aller in a different tense!

Aller in...MeaningExample
Presentis going to (standard)Il va manger.
Imparfaitwas going to / was about toIl allait manger (quand...)
Conditionnelwould be going toIl irait manger... (rare)
Passé composéwas going to (and did)Il est allé manger. (= He went to eat — slightly different meaning)

⚠️ The most important variation is aller (imparfait) + infinitive = was going to / was about to

This is extremely common for expressing interrupted intentions or near-misses:

  • J'allais partir quand le téléphone a sonné. (I was about to leave when the phone rang.)
  • Elle allait dire quelque chose, mais elle s'est arrêtée. (She was about to say something, but she stopped.)

Exercise D — Stylistic Effects

  1. The FP makes the departure feel more certain, decided, and imminent. "Il va partir" feels like a done deal — the decision has been made and he's practically already leaving. "Il partira" is more neutral and distant.

  2. The narrative FP creates suspense and dramatic tension. Instead of reporting a past event neutrally ("il a ouvert"), the speaker builds anticipation — "and then, he's GOING TO open the door..." The listener leans in, waiting. It mimics real-time experience.

  3. "Elle allait devenir médecin" implies this was the expected trajectory — it was going to happen, it was on track. It creates a sense of fate or destiny interrupted (or fulfilled). "Elle serait devenue" is more hypothetical and conditional.

  4. The FP + exasperation ("Tu ne vas pas faire ça!") is more emotional, personal, and direct than the imperative "Ne fais pas ça!" It expresses disbelief and indignation — "You're NOT going to do that, are you?!" It challenges the person rather than simply ordering them.

  5. "Ça va aller" feels more warm, personal, and intimate than "Tout ira bien." It's the reassurance of a friend, not a formal promise. The futur simple version sounds more like a prediction; the futur proche version sounds like empathy.

Il allait fermer les volets quand il s'est rendu compte que le chien avait disparu. (4 — past near future, interrupted) « Le chien! Où est le chien? » Le petit garçon s'est mis à pleurer. « Ne t'inquiète pas, on va le retrouver, » a dit la mère doucement. (7 — reassurance)

Le père a pris sa décision : « Je vais sortir le chercher. Vous, vous restez à l'intérieur et vous ne bougez pas. » (2 — firm intention) La mère a protesté : « Tu ne vas pas sortir par ce temps! Tu es fou! » (6 — exasperation)

Et là, tu sais quoi — le père va ouvrir la porte, et le chien est là, assis sur le paillasson, trempé mais content. (5 — narrative futur proche, storytelling) Tout le monde a ri. La tempête allait être terrible, mais au moins, toute la famille était ensemble.

Key Takeaways for Futur Proche:

  1. Simplest formation — aller (present) + infinitive
  2. Most used future tense in spoken French — far more common than futur simple
  3. Negative wraps around aller — Je ne vais pas manger (NOT je vais ne pas manger)
  4. Pronouns go before the infinitive — Je vais LE faire (NOT je LE vais faire)
  5. Reflexive pronouns match the subject — Nous allons NOUS lever (NOT se lever)
  6. Past variant = aller (IMP) + inf — J'allais partir = I was going to leave
  7. Reported speech: va → allait — Il a dit qu'il allait partir
  8. Multiple functions — warning, intention, prediction, threat, reassurance, exasperation, narrative, rhetorical
  9. FP vs. FS — FP = immediate/spoken/subjective; FS = distant/formal/objective
  10. FP vs. Present — Present for schedules; FP for personal intentions/predictions
